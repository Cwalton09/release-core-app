Why are you so tired, anxious, or depressed before your period? In the days before your period, your hormones drop, and that shift affects mood, sleep, and energy. For many women, stress makes those symptoms much stronger. Research shows women under higher stress are far more likely to have significant PMS, and a nervous system that's already on edge reacts more strongly to those monthly hormone changes.

Every month, the same week. You're exhausted, snapping at people, crying at commercials, or feeling like nothing is going right. Then your period starts and it lifts.

## What happens before your period

In the second half of your cycle, estrogen and progesterone rise and then fall right before your period. Those hormones affect brain chemicals like serotonin and GABA, which help regulate mood, calm, and sleep.

When they drop, many people feel:

- Tired or drained
- Anxious, irritable, or on edge
- Low, teary, or hopeless
- Bloated, achy, or craving food
- Restless sleep

## How stress makes PMS worse

Your stress system and your hormone system talk to each other. When your nervous system is under constant stress, it can make your body more sensitive to those monthly hormone shifts.

In a study of women in Spain, those with high stress had about 5 times the odds of PMS and nearly 6 times the odds of PMDD compared with women with low stress. The researchers couldn't completely rule out that the symptoms themselves added stress, but the link was strong.

So if your PMS has gotten worse during a stressful season, that's not your imagination.

## Could it be PMDD?

PMDD, premenstrual dysphoric disorder, is a more severe form of PMS. Symptoms like depression, anxiety, anger, or hopelessness are strong enough to disrupt work, relationships, or daily life, and they ease once your period starts.

If that sounds familiar, please talk with your doctor. PMDD is real and treatable. If you ever feel like harming yourself, call or text 988.

## Signs stress is part of your monthly pattern

- Your PMS got worse during a hard season
- You feel on edge most of the month, and it peaks before your period
- You're running on empty, with little rest or support
- Your symptoms ease when life feels calmer
- You notice other stress signs, like poor sleep or a tight jaw

## Common questions

**Why am I so tired before my period?** Falling hormone levels can affect sleep, energy, and mood. Stress and poor sleep make it worse.

**Why do I feel anxious before my period?** Hormone drops affect calming brain chemicals, and a stressed nervous system reacts more strongly.

**Can stress make my period late?** Yes. Significant stress can delay or change your cycle. If your period is late, take a pregnancy test and talk to your doctor if it keeps happening.
