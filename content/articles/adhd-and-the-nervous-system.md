I was diagnosed with ADHD and medicated for 23 years. I know firsthand what it's like to live with the fog, the fatigue, and the feeling that your brain is working against you.

So let me be clear from the start: ADHD is real, it's neurodevelopmental, and it deserves proper care. Never stop or change ADHD medication without talking to your doctor.

But there's a part of ADHD that doesn't get talked about enough, and it has a lot to do with the nervous system.

## ADHD isn't just about attention

Most people know ADHD as trouble focusing, restlessness, and impulsivity. But many people with ADHD also live with big emotions, fast frustration, and feeling overwhelmed much more easily than others.

Researchers call this emotional dysregulation. It's so common in ADHD that some European diagnostic guidelines list it as a core feature, and many researchers think it deserves far more attention than it gets.

## Stress and ADHD feed each other

Research shows that stress, anxiety, and emotional dysregulation are closely linked with ADHD. Many people find their symptoms get noticeably worse when they're stressed, tired, or overwhelmed.

And ADHD itself can create stress: missed deadlines, lost keys, feeling misunderstood, being told to "just try harder." Over time, that can keep the nervous system on alert, which makes focusing even harder.

## Common nervous system patterns with ADHD

- Going from calm to overwhelmed very quickly
- Feeling emotions intensely and all at once
- Shutting down when there's too much to do
- Sensitivity to noise, clutter, or crowds
- Rejection feeling unbearably painful
- Exhaustion after trying to hold it together all day
- Believing something is wrong with you

## The beliefs that build up

Many people with ADHD grew up hearing they were too much, lazy, careless, or not living up to their potential. The nervous system can take that in as beliefs like "I'm always messing up" or "I have to work twice as hard to be okay."

Those beliefs don't cause ADHD. But they can keep your nervous system braced, and that can make everything harder. You can read more about [sensory overload](/articles/sensory-overload-nervous-system) and [brain fog](/articles/brain-fog-nervous-system).
