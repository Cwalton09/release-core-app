You open your eyes, and before your feet even hit the floor, your stomach is already in knots. Maybe it's a dull ache. Maybe it's cramping, nausea, or an urgent need to run to the bathroom. By lunch, it's usually better.

If mornings are the hardest part of the day for your stomach, there's a reason.

## Your body's morning stress spike

Every morning, your body gives itself a jolt of cortisol, your main stress hormone, to help you get up and going. Scientists call it the **cortisol awakening response**.

A 2014 review in *Sleep Medicine Reviews* found that cortisol typically **rises by about 38% to 75%** in the first 30 to 45 minutes after you wake up.

For most people, that's just part of waking up. But if your nervous system is already on high alert, that morning spike can push it over the edge.

## Why your gut feels it first

Your gut is wired directly to your brain through your nervous system. When stress hormones rise, your digestion changes: your stomach can tighten, your intestines can speed up, and your gut becomes more sensitive.

Mornings can also be when your mind jumps straight to everything you have to face that day. Before you've even thought it through, your body may already be bracing.

## Signs your morning stomach ache is stress-related

- Your stomach hurts or feels sick within minutes of waking
- It gets better as the day goes on
- It's worse on workdays or school days than weekends
- You wake up already tense, anxious, or with a clenched jaw
- You often don't feel like eating breakfast

Morning stomach pain can also come from reflux, ulcers, IBS, food intolerances, gallbladder problems, medications, and other causes. See a doctor for ongoing pain, vomiting, blood in your stool, or weight loss.
