You're a grown adult with your own life. Then you walk into your parents' house for the holidays, and within an hour you feel 12 years old again. Your shoulders tense. You get snappy or go quiet. You find yourself bracing for the comment, the look, the old argument.

Then you drive home exhausted and wonder why you can't just enjoy it like everyone else.

## What the research found

In a 2023 survey of more than 2,000 U.S. adults, the American Psychological Association found that **89%** said something about the holiday season stressed them out.

The top stressors were:

- **Money** and the cost of the holidays: **58%**
- **Missing loved ones** who have passed away or can't be there: **38%**
- **Family conflict**: in a separate 2023 poll from the American Psychiatric Association, 37% of adults said they were worried about difficult family dynamics during the holidays

So if the season feels heavy, you're in the majority.

## Why you feel like a kid again

Your nervous system learned how to survive in your family long before you could explain it. Maybe you learned to keep the peace, stay small, perform, or watch everyone's mood. Those patterns kept you safe back then.

When you walk back into the same house, with the same people, the same smells and sounds, your body recognizes it instantly. And it goes right back to the old way of protecting you. Not because you haven't grown, but because your body never got the message that you're safe now.

That's why one comment from a parent can undo months of feeling good.

## Signs your body is reacting to old patterns

- You feel anxious for days or weeks before a family visit
- You turn into an old version of yourself around family
- You people-please, go quiet, or get defensive without meaning to
- One comment can ruin your whole day
- You feel exhausted or get sick afterward
- You feel guilty for not enjoying it more

## Why it keeps coming back

Most of the time, we don't know which moment taught our body to brace around family. We can guess, but the real trigger is usually something we would never think of on our own. Until it's found, every holiday sets off the same old reaction.
