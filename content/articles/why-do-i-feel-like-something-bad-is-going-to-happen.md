Why do I feel like something bad is going to happen? That feeling of dread often comes from a nervous system that struggles with not knowing. Researchers call it fear of the unknown, and one major review argues it may be one of the most basic fears underneath anxiety. When your body learned that surprises meant pain, uncertainty itself starts to feel dangerous, even when nothing is wrong.

You might feel a quiet dread most days, wait for the other shoe to drop when life is good, or feel uneasy without being able to say why.

## When to get help

A sudden "sense of impending doom" together with chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting can be a medical emergency. Call 911. If dread is constant or makes daily life hard, talk to a doctor or therapist. If you're in crisis, call or text 988.

## What research shows

In a review in the *Journal of Anxiety Disorders*, researcher R. Nicholas Carleton examined which fears might sit underneath anxiety. He argued that fear of the unknown may be a, or even the, fundamental fear behind anxiety. People who find uncertainty hard to tolerate tend to fill the unknown with worst-case guesses, which feels like dread.

## Why your body expects the worst

- **Uncertainty feels unsafe.** Not knowing can feel worse than bad news.
- **Old patterns.** If good times were often followed by something painful, your body learned to brace.
- **Constant scanning.** An alert nervous system keeps looking for what might go wrong.
- **Stress builds up.** Long-term stress keeps your threat system turned up.

## Common questions

**Is a feeling of dread a symptom of anxiety?** Yes. Ongoing dread is one of the most common anxiety symptoms.

**Why do I feel anxious when things are going well?** If good times were followed by pain in the past, your body may have learned to get ready when things are good.

**Can this feeling go away?** Yes. When your body learns it's safe now, the dread often eases.
