You went to bed on time. You slept seven or eight hours. And you still woke up feeling like you never slept at all.

"Why am I always tired even when I sleep?" is one of the most searched health questions of the year. If that's you, the issue may not be how long you sleep. It may be how deeply your body is able to rest.

## What the research found

Torbjörn Åkerstedt, a sleep researcher at the Karolinska Institute and Stockholm University, reviewed the research on stress and sleep in 2006 in the Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment and Health.

He found that stress is closely tied to poor sleep. Sleep studies showed that stress is linked to:

- Shorter sleep
- More broken, fragmented sleep
- Possibly less deep sleep, the stage where your body does most of its repair

He also described a loop: poor sleep raises stress hormones like cortisol, and higher stress makes sleep worse. Later work from the same research group found that stressful thoughts and worries at bedtime were one of the biggest predictors of how well people slept.

## Why you can sleep and still not rest

Deep sleep only happens when your body feels safe enough to fully let go. If your nervous system is still on guard, part of you stays half awake all night. You may not remember waking up, but your sleep is lighter and more broken than it should be.

So you get the hours, but not the rest.

## What keeps the nervous system in fight or flight

At night, when everything gets quiet, whatever your body has been holding finally has room to come up. If your nervous system learned that it isn't safe to fully relax, that something might go wrong if you stop paying attention, it won't let you drop all the way into deep sleep.

That's not a willpower problem. It's protection. Your body is doing what it learned to do, long after the danger is gone.

## Signs your body isn't fully resting at night

- You wake up tired no matter how long you sleep
- You wake up already tense, anxious, or with a clenched jaw
- Your mind starts racing as soon as your head hits the pillow
- You wake up at the same time most nights
- You feel better on vacation, away from your normal life

Ongoing exhaustion can also come from sleep apnea, thyroid problems, anemia, and other medical causes, so check with your doctor if it doesn't improve.
