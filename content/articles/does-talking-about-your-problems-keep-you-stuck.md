You've told the story a hundred times. To your best friend, your partner, your mom. You know every detail of what happened and why.

So why do you still feel exactly the same?

## Talking about it isn't the same as healing it

Let me be clear first: talking to people you trust matters. Connection is healing, and working with a good therapist can be life-changing. This isn't about staying quiet.

But there's a difference between talking something through and going over it again and again without anything changing. Every time you retell a painful story, your nervous system can relive it, without ever getting to finish it. The loop opens, the feelings rush up, and then it all gets packed back away, unfinished.

## What research calls co-rumination

Researchers have a name for this. Psychologist Amanda Rose defined co-rumination as excessively discussing problems with a friend: talking about them over and over, guessing at the causes, and focusing on the bad feelings.

Her research found that co-rumination brings friends closer, but it's also linked to more anxiety and depression. In a 2007 study following young people over time, co-rumination predicted both closer friendships and increases in anxiety and depression symptoms. Most of this research has been done with teens and young adults, and the effects can vary from person to person.

In other words, rehashing can feel supportive in the moment and still keep you stuck.

## Why rehashing keeps the loop open

When you go over the same problem again and again, you keep reactivating it. But nothing new happens. Your body doesn't get the safety, resolution, or new perspective it needed. So the loop stays open, and your nervous system stays on alert.

## Signs you might be stuck in the retelling

- You tell the same story often, but it never feels lighter
- You feel worse, not better, after venting
- You replay conversations in your head long after they're over
- Friends have heard it so many times they don't know what to say
- You understand the situation completely and still react the same way

## Going to the root instead

Healing doesn't come from telling the story more times. It comes from finding what your body learned from it, and giving your body a new experience while that old memory is open.
