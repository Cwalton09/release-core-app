How do you fall back asleep in the middle of the night? Sleep experts recommend not fighting it. Don't check the clock. Keep the lights low and your phone away. If you've been awake a while and feel frustrated, get up and do something quiet and dull in dim light, then go back to bed when you feel sleepy. These steps come from cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), which the American Academy of Sleep Medicine strongly recommends.

Waking up at night is normal. Staying awake usually means your body flipped back into alert mode.

## When to get help

If you can't sleep most nights for three months or more, snore loudly, stop breathing in your sleep, or feel exhausted during the day, talk to your doctor. Sleep apnea and other conditions need real treatment.

## What research shows

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine reviewed the research on non-medication treatments for chronic insomnia. It strongly recommends CBT-I. One of its core parts, stimulus control, teaches your brain to link bed with sleep instead of with lying awake and worrying.

## What to do when you wake up

- **Don't check the time.** Your body reads the time as a reason to stay alert.
- **Breathe out slowly.** Make your exhale longer than your inhale.
- **Let your body get heavy.** Notice the bed holding you up.
- **Get up if you're frustrated.** Sit somewhere dim and do something calm until you're sleepy.
- **Keep your phone away.** Bright screens and scrolling wake your brain up.

## Common questions

**Should I get up if I can't fall back asleep?** Sleep experts often say yes. Get up, keep the lights low, and come back to bed when you feel sleepy.

**Why do I wake up at the same time every night?** It can be stress, a sleep cycle, or a habit your body has learned. See [why you wake up at 3 AM](/articles/why-do-i-wake-up-at-3am).

**Is waking up at night normal?** Yes. Brief waking is normal. The problem is when your body won't settle back down.
