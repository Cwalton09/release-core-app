Can stress cause tooth pain? Yes, often indirectly. Stress makes many people clench or grind their teeth, during the day or in their sleep. That's called bruxism. All that pressure can make teeth ache, feel sensitive, or even crack, and it can make your jaw sore and tired. A 2020 meta-analysis found that stressed people were about twice as likely to have bruxism.

You might wake up with sore teeth or a tired jaw, notice your teeth pressed together during the day, or feel an ache across several teeth with no clear cavity.

## See a dentist if…

Tooth pain can come from cavities, infections, cracked teeth, gum disease, or sinus problems. Please see a dentist for tooth pain that lasts, swelling, fever, a bad taste, or pain when biting. Get urgent care for facial swelling that spreads or trouble swallowing or breathing. Jaw pain with chest pain or shortness of breath can be a heart problem, so call 911.

## How stress leads to tooth pain

When your nervous system is on alert, your body braces, and the jaw is one of the first places it shows. Many people clench without noticing, while driving, working, or falling asleep. At night, stress and poor sleep can increase grinding.

Your jaw muscles are among the strongest in your body. Hours of clenching put heavy pressure on your teeth and the joint in front of your ears.

## What research shows

A 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis in *Frontiers in Neurology* found that people with stress had about twice the odds of bruxism compared with people without stress. The authors noted the evidence is still limited and more studies are needed. Another review found a link between sleep bruxism and stress symptoms in adults.

## Signs stress may be behind your tooth pain

- Your teeth or jaw are sore when you wake up
- You catch yourself clenching during the day
- Several teeth ache, not just one
- Your dentist sees wear on your teeth
- You also have headaches, ear pain, or a tight neck

## Common questions

**Can anxiety make your teeth hurt?** Yes. Anxiety often leads to clenching and grinding, which can make teeth ache and feel sensitive.

**How do I know if I grind my teeth at night?** Signs include a sore jaw or headache in the morning, worn teeth, or a partner hearing grinding. Your dentist can check.

**What helps stress tooth pain?** A dentist may suggest a night guard. During the day, notice your jaw and let your teeth part with your tongue resting on the roof of your mouth.
