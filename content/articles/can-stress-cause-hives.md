Can stress cause hives? Yes, stress can trigger or worsen hives and itching in many people. Your skin is full of mast cells, immune cells that release histamine, and they respond to stress signals from your nervous system. When they release histamine, you can get red, itchy welts or itching all over, sometimes with no clear allergy behind it.

You might notice it on a hard day, before a big event, or after weeks of pressure. Your skin starts itching, welts appear, and you can't figure out what you reacted to.

## Get urgent care if…

Call 911 or go to an emergency room if hives come with swelling of your lips, tongue, or throat, trouble breathing, dizziness, or fainting. Those can be signs of a serious allergic reaction.

## How stress reaches your skin

Your skin and nervous system are closely connected. Nerve endings in your skin sit right next to mast cells, and stress signals from your nervous system can make those mast cells release histamine and other chemicals. Histamine causes itching, redness, and swelling.

That's why itching and hives can flare when nothing about your food, soap, or environment changed.

## What research shows

In a study of people with chronic hives, 74% said emotional stress made their hives worse, and 62% had gone through a stressful life event before their hives started. It was a small study, but it matches what many people with hives notice.

Reviews of chronic hives also list stress as one of the factors that can make flares worse, along with infections and certain medications.

## Signs stress may be a trigger for you

- Your hives or itching flare on stressful days
- They started during or after a hard season
- You can't find a clear allergy trigger
- You itch more at night, when you finally slow down
- You also have other stress signs, like poor sleep or a racing mind

Hives can have many causes, so please see a doctor or allergist, especially if they last more than a few weeks.

## Common questions

**What do stress hives look like?** Raised, red or skin-colored welts that itch, often on the chest, neck, face, or arms. They can move around and come and go.

**Can anxiety make you itch all over?** Yes. Anxiety and stress can make your skin more sensitive and trigger itching, even without hives.

**How long do stress hives last?** Single welts often fade within hours. If hives keep coming back for six weeks or more, that's called chronic hives, and it's worth seeing a doctor.
