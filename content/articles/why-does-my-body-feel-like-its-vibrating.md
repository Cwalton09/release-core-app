Why does your body feel like it's vibrating? Many people with anxiety describe a buzzing, humming, or trembling feeling inside the body that no one else can see. Doctors sometimes call this an internal tremor. It's often linked to a nervous system on high alert, flooded with stress hormones and muscle tension. It can also have medical causes, so it's worth getting checked, especially if it's new.

It might feel like your phone is buzzing in your chest. Or like your whole body is humming when you lie down. You look at your hands and they're perfectly still.

## What internal vibrations feel like

- A buzzing or humming sensation inside your chest, legs, or whole body
- A trembling feeling you can't see from the outside
- Often worse when lying still, at night, or during stress
- Sometimes comes with a racing heart, restlessness, or feeling wired

## How anxiety can cause it

When your nervous system senses danger, it releases adrenaline and gets your muscles ready to move. Muscles tense and fire. Your heart speeds up. Your senses sharpen.

If that state never fully switches off, your body can stay in a low-level ready-to-run mode. Many people feel that as a constant hum or vibration inside them, a body that's revving with nowhere to go.

In one clinical review of people with internal tremor, about two-thirds also had anxiety, and the feeling often improved when the anxiety was treated. Researchers still don't fully understand the cause and say more study is needed.

## When to see a doctor

Please get checked if the vibrations:

- Are new, constant, or getting worse
- Come with numbness, weakness, or trouble walking
- Come with a visible tremor, balance problems, or changes in your movement
- Happen with dizziness, fainting, or heart palpitations

Internal tremors can sometimes be linked to medical conditions, including thyroid problems, medication side effects, too much caffeine, and neurological conditions. Your doctor can help rule these out.

## Signs it may be your nervous system

- It gets stronger with stress and calmer when you feel safe
- You also feel wired, restless, or on edge
- It shows up when you finally lie down to rest
- It came on during a stressful season
- Your medical tests came back normal

## Common questions

**Can anxiety cause internal vibrations?** Many people with anxiety report them, and research links internal tremor with anxiety. A doctor should still rule out other causes.

**Why does my body vibrate when I lie down?** When you're finally still, you notice sensations you were too busy to feel, and a nervous system on alert often revs up at rest.

**Are internal tremors dangerous?** Often they're not, but they can be linked to conditions worth checking, so talk to your doctor.
