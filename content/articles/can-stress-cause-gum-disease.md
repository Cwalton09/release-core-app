Can stress cause gum disease? Stress doesn't cause gum disease on its own, but research shows it can make it worse. Gum disease starts with bacteria along the gumline. Long-term stress affects your immune system and inflammation, which changes how your gums respond to that bacteria. Stress also tends to change habits like brushing, flossing, sleep, smoking, and clenching. A 2022 review found that stress and anxiety play a role in how gum disease progresses.

You might notice bleeding when you brush, swollen or tender gums, bad breath, or gums pulling back during or after a hard season.

## See a dentist if…

Bleeding, swollen, or receding gums need a dentist, whatever the cause. See a dentist soon for loose teeth, pus, pain when chewing, or a change in your bite. Gum disease is linked to other health problems, including heart disease and diabetes, so it's worth treating early. Get urgent care for facial swelling or fever.

## How stress reaches your gums

Your immune system and nervous system are closely connected. Long-term stress keeps cortisol and inflammation signals high, which can weaken your defenses against gum bacteria and make inflammation in your gums stronger.

Stress also changes behavior. When you're overwhelmed, it's easy to skip flossing, eat more sugar, sleep poorly, or clench your teeth, and all of these affect your gums.

## What research shows

A 2022 systematic review and meta-analysis in *Quintessence International* looked at anxiety, psychological stress, and gum health. The authors concluded that stress and anxiety play a role in the progression of periodontitis, the more serious form of gum disease.

## Signs stress may be affecting your gums

- Your gums got worse during a stressful season
- You've been clenching or grinding your teeth
- Your routine slipped when life got hard
- You also get mouth sores or other stress flare-ups

## Common questions

**Can anxiety make your gums bleed?** Anxiety doesn't directly cause bleeding, but stress can worsen gum inflammation. Bleeding gums should be checked by a dentist.

**Can gum disease be reversed?** Early gum disease (gingivitis) can usually be reversed with good care. Advanced gum disease can be managed with treatment.

**Does dry mouth affect gums?** Yes. Saliva protects your gums and teeth, and stress-related dry mouth can increase your risk.
