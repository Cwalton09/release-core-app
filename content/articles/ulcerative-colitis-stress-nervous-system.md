You were in remission. Things were finally calm. Then a hard year happened: a move, a loss, a job that wore you down. And slowly, the urgency, the bleeding, and the cramping came back.

If you live with ulcerative colitis, you may have wondered whether stress had something to do with it. Research says it may.

## What ulcerative colitis is

Ulcerative colitis (UC) is a type of inflammatory bowel disease. The immune system causes inflammation and sores in the lining of the colon and rectum. It can cause bloody diarrhea, urgency, cramping, and exhaustion. Stress doesn't cause UC. But it may play a part in when it flares.

## What the research found

**Long-term stress and flares.** In a study published in the *American Journal of Gastroenterology*, Italian researchers followed 62 people with UC who were in remission, some for more than five years. People with the highest stress over the previous two years had **about 2.8 times the risk of a flare.**

What surprised the researchers: a single stressful event or a bad month didn't predict flares. **Stress that went on for months or years did.**

**Stress and symptoms.** A 2015 study of 478 people with IBD found that higher stress went along with more symptoms in both UC and Crohn's. Stress turns up how much your gut hurts and reacts, and those symptoms are just as real as the ones inflammation causes.

## What this means

A nervous system that has been on alert for a long time changes how your gut works. In fight or flight, your body pulls resources away from digestion and repair. The "rest and digest" side, which helps your gut heal and keeps inflammation in check, has less of a say.

That's why long-term stress matters more than one bad day. It isn't the event. It's a body that never got the signal that it was safe to stand down.

## Signs your nervous system may be part of your UC

- Flares seem to follow long, hard seasons rather than single events
- Your gut reacts before you leave the house, travel, or face a big day
- You can't fully relax even when your UC is in remission
- You plan everything around bathrooms, even on good days
- You feel calmer and your gut feels better on vacation

Never stop or change your UC medication without your gastroenterologist. Heavy bleeding, severe belly pain, fever, or signs of dehydration need medical care right away.
