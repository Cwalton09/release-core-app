How do you calm anxiety fast? One of the quickest, best-studied tools is slowing your breathing, especially your exhale. In a Stanford study, people who did five minutes a day of "cyclic sighing," a breathing pattern with long exhales, had bigger improvements in mood and lower breathing rates than people who meditated.

It works because slow, long exhales activate your body's calming system.

## When to get help

If you have chest pain, trouble breathing, or fainting, call 911. If anxiety is frequent or makes daily life hard, talk to a doctor or therapist. If you're in crisis, call or text 988.

## What research shows

A randomized study in *Cell Reports Medicine* compared three daily five-minute breathing practices with mindfulness meditation over one month. Cyclic sighing, which focuses on long exhales, led to the greatest improvement in mood and the biggest drop in breathing rate.

A systematic review in *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* found that slow breathing is linked to more activity in the calming branch of the nervous system and to less anxiety and arousal.

## How to do cyclic sighing

1. Breathe in through your nose.
2. Take a second short breath in to fill your lungs the rest of the way.
3. Breathe out slowly through your mouth until your lungs are empty.
4. Repeat for one to five minutes.

## Other ways to calm down fast

- Put your feet flat on the floor and notice something solid under you.
- Name three things you can see.
- Put a hand on your chest and feel it rise and fall.

## Common questions

**What is the fastest way to calm anxiety?** Slow, exhale-focused breathing is one of the fastest, research-backed options.

**How long does it take to work?** Many people feel calmer within a minute or two.

**Why does anxiety keep coming back after I calm down?** Breathing calms your body in the moment. It doesn't change what your body still treats as danger.
