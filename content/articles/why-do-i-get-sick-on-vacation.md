You made it through the busy season. The deadlines, the holidays, the long weeks. You finally have time off, and on day one, you wake up with a headache, a sore throat, or a body that just won't move.

It happens every time. And it can feel like your body is punishing you for resting.

## What the research found

Dutch researchers at Tilburg University studied this in 2002 and called it **"leisure sickness."** Their study, published in *Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics*, surveyed nearly 1,900 people.

- About **3%** of people said they regularly got sick on weekends or vacations, a number the researchers called "relatively common"
- The most common symptoms were **headaches and migraines, fatigue, muscle pain, and nausea**
- Colds and flu-like illnesses often showed up around vacations
- Most people had been dealing with it for **more than 10 years**, and it often started during a stressful period of their lives
- The people it happened to had **heavier workloads, a strong sense of responsibility, and trouble relaxing**

## Why your body waits until you stop

When you're under pressure, your nervous system keeps you in go-mode. Stress hormones keep you running, push symptoms into the background, and get you through.

When the pressure finally lifts, your body drops out of that mode, sometimes all at once. Everything it was holding back comes up. Headaches researchers call this the "let-down" pattern: the migraine doesn't hit during the hard week, but right after.

For some people, there's another layer. If your body learned that it's only safe to stop when everything is done, or that resting is dangerous, it may treat downtime as unfamiliar territory. And unfamiliar can feel like a threat.

## Signs this is your pattern

- You get sick at the start of almost every vacation
- Headaches or migraines show up on weekends
- You crash the moment a big project is over
- You feel guilty or restless when you rest
- You don't feel tired until you finally stop
- Your holidays are often spent recovering
