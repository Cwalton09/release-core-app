Can anxiety cause overactive bladder? Anxiety and overactive bladder are closely linked. Research finds that people with overactive bladder have higher rates of anxiety, and the connection seems to go both ways. Your bladder is run by your nervous system, which decides when it feels full and when it's time to go. When your body is on alert, those signals can get louder and more urgent.

You might feel a sudden, strong need to pee, go much more often than usual, wake up at night to go, or feel urgency get worse when you're stressed or in places without a bathroom nearby.

## See a doctor if…

Please see a doctor if you have pain or burning when you pee, blood in your urine, fever, back pain, leaking, trouble emptying your bladder, or a sudden change in your bladder habits. Infections, diabetes, prostate problems, pelvic floor issues, and some medications can cause bladder symptoms too.

## What research shows

A 2017 systematic review and meta-analysis in the *International Neurourology Journal* looked at 11 studies with thousands of participants. It found a positive link between overactive bladder and anxiety, as well as depression. Women with overactive bladder were more likely than men to have anxiety.

## How anxiety affects your bladder

- **Your nervous system controls the bladder.** It decides when the bladder muscle squeezes and when it stays relaxed.
- **Alert mode makes signals louder.** When you're anxious, you notice body signals more, including fullness.
- **Tense pelvic muscles.** Many people hold tension in the pelvic floor when stressed.
- **A worry loop.** Fear of not reaching a bathroom adds urgency, which adds worry.

## Common questions

**Can stress make you feel like you have to pee all the time?** Yes. Stress can make bladder signals feel more urgent.

**Is overactive bladder all in my head?** No. It's a real condition. Your nervous system is part of how it works.

**Can calming my nervous system help my bladder?** It can help alongside bladder training and medical care.
