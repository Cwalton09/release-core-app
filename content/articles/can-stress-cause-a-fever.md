Can stress cause a fever? Yes, for some people. Doctors call it psychogenic fever, or stress-induced hyperthermia. Research by Japanese physician Takakazu Oka and others shows that psychological stress can raise body temperature through your nervous system, in a different way than an infection does. Some people run a low-grade temperature for long stretches during stress. Others spike during intense stress.

You feel warm and flushed. You take your temperature, and it's up. But you don't feel sick, and tests don't find an infection.

## Always rule out infection first

A fever is usually a sign of infection or another medical issue, so please see a doctor, especially if it's high, lasts more than a few days, or comes with other symptoms. Stress fever is something doctors consider after other causes are ruled out.

## How stress raises your temperature

When you have an infection, your immune system sends inflammatory signals that turn up your body's thermostat.

Stress fever works differently. Research suggests that psychological stress activates a pathway in the brain that switches on your sympathetic nervous system, your fight-or-flight system. That raises heat production, including through a type of fat called brown fat, and your temperature goes up.

Studies describe a few patterns:

- A quick temperature spike during acute, intense stress
- A rise in anticipation of stressful events
- A long-lasting low-grade temperature during ongoing stress
- Fever that returns in situations that remind the body of a past fear

That last one is a strong example of how the nervous system learns: the body can react to a reminder of an old fear as if it's happening again.

## Signs your temperature may be stress-related

- It rises during stressful times and settles when you rest
- Fever reducers don't help much
- Tests don't find an infection or other cause
- You also feel wired, flushed, or exhausted

## Common questions

**Can anxiety cause a fever?** Anxiety and stress can raise body temperature in some people. Other causes should be ruled out first.

**What temperature is a stress fever?** It's often low-grade, though some people spike higher during intense stress.

**Can a child get a stress fever?** Yes, it's been described in children and teens, especially around school stress. A pediatrician should check it first.
