Can stress make you lose weight? Yes, for some people. Stress affects eating in two directions. Some people eat more, and others lose their appetite and eat much less. When your nervous system is in fight-or-flight, digestion and hunger get turned down, because your body is focused on getting through the threat. Over weeks, that can show up on the scale.

You might forget to eat, feel full after a few bites, feel sick at the sight of food, or notice your clothes getting looser during a hard season.

## See a doctor if…

Unexplained weight loss should always be checked, even if you're under stress. Please see a doctor if you lose more than about 5% of your body weight without trying, or if weight loss comes with fever, night sweats, pain, changes in your bowels, trouble swallowing, or extreme tiredness. Thyroid problems, diabetes, digestive conditions, and other illnesses can cause weight loss too. If you're restricting food on purpose or afraid of eating, please reach out for eating disorder support.

## What research shows

A review in *Nutrition* found that stress appears to change food intake in two ways, leading to either undereating or overeating. The direction may depend partly on how severe the stress is.

A review of unintentional weight loss in *American Family Physician* notes that weight loss can have many causes, from medical conditions to medications to social factors like isolation, which is why a checkup matters.

## Why stress turns off your appetite

- **Fight-or-flight slows digestion.** Your body moves energy away from your gut.
- **Nausea and a tight stomach.** Anxiety often makes food unappealing.
- **Your mind is elsewhere.** Many people simply forget to eat.
- **Poor sleep.** It changes hunger hormones and energy use.

## Common questions

**How much weight can you lose from stress?** It varies. Any weight loss you can't explain, especially more than 5% of your body weight, is worth checking with a doctor.

**Why do some people gain weight from stress and others lose it?** Stress can push eating either way, depending on the person and how intense the stress is.

**Will the weight come back when the stress ends?** Often, as appetite returns. If it doesn't, see a doctor.
