Why do I feel worse before I feel better? When you start healing, feelings and sensations your body has been holding back can come up. As your nervous system stops bracing, you may feel more tired, more emotional, or more aware of discomfort for a while. For many people this is temporary. But feeling worse isn't always part of healing, so it's important to know when to get checked.

You might cry more, feel exhausted, have vivid dreams, or notice old memories. It can feel like you're going backward, even though something is moving.

## When to get help

Please see a doctor or mental health professional if you feel much worse for more than a couple of weeks, if new physical symptoms appear, or if you're struggling to function. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 in the US, or your local emergency number, right away.

"Healing crisis" is sometimes used to explain away real problems. Your body deserves to be taken seriously.

## Why it can feel worse at first

**You stop running on adrenaline.** When your body has been on alert for a long time, stress hormones can mask exhaustion. As your nervous system settles, you may finally feel how tired you are.

**Held-back emotions come up.** If your body learned to shut feelings down to stay safe, those feelings are still there. As you feel safer, they can surface as tears, anger, or grief. See [why I cry when healing](/articles/why-do-i-cry-when-healing).

**You notice more.** When you start paying attention to your body, you notice sensations you used to tune out. That can feel like more symptoms, even when nothing new is happening.

**Old patterns push back.** Your nervous system follows rules it learned to keep you safe. When those rules start to change, it can briefly hold on tighter.

## What research shows

Research on relaxation shows that some people feel *more* anxious when they first try to relax. A 2019 study in the *Journal of Affective Disorders* found this "relaxation-induced anxiety" was strongest in people who are very sensitive to sudden shifts in emotion. Their bodies had learned that letting their guard down could lead to a bigger drop. See [why I feel anxious when I relax](/articles/why-do-i-feel-anxious-when-i-relax).

## How to tell it apart from going backward

- **It eases with time.** Healing waves come and go. A true decline keeps getting worse.
- **You feel more, not less able.** You may feel emotional but also more present.
- **There are small wins too.** Better sleep some nights, a softer reaction to an old trigger.

## What helps

- Rest more than usual
- Let emotions move without needing to understand them right away
- Keep your days simple
- Talk to someone you trust
- Remember that one hard day isn't the whole story

You may also find [signs your nervous system is healing](/articles/signs-your-nervous-system-is-healing) and [vivid dreams after healing work](/articles/vivid-dreams-after-healing-work) helpful.

## Common questions

**Is it normal to feel more emotional after healing work?** Yes, it's common. Your body may be letting out feelings it held back for a long time.

**How long does feeling worse last?** For many people it lasts a few days. If it lasts longer than a couple of weeks or keeps getting worse, get support.

**Should I stop if I feel worse?** Slow down and rest. If you feel much worse, talk with a professional before continuing.

## Why your body held it in

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the feeling that's coming up now. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *It's not safe to feel this* or *I have to be strong*, and your nervous system has been following it for years.

When feelings come up as you heal, your body is starting to let go of what it was guarding. Most of us don't know what that was.
