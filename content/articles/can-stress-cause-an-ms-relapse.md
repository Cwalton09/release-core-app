Can stress cause an MS relapse? Research shows that stressful life events are linked to a higher risk of MS relapses. Stress doesn't cause MS, and it isn't your fault if you flare. But a large review found a consistent link between stressful events and the relapses that followed them. That's why calming your nervous system can be one part of caring for MS.

Many people with MS describe the same pattern. There's a move, a divorce, a loss, or months of pressure, and then a flare.

## Keep working with your neurologist

MS is a serious condition, and your treatment matters. Tell your neurologist about any new or worsening symptoms, such as vision changes, numbness, weakness, or trouble walking, especially if they last more than a day. Working with your nervous system goes alongside your medical care, never instead of it.

## What research shows

A 2004 review in the *BMJ* combined 14 studies on stress and MS. It found a consistent link between stressful life events and a higher rate of relapses afterward. The effect was moderate, and the studies agreed with each other.

Researchers think stress may affect MS partly through the immune system. Stress hormones like cortisol help control inflammation, and when stress goes on for a long time, that control can work less well.

The research shows a link. It doesn't prove that stress alone caused any one relapse. Many relapses happen with no stressful event, and many stressful events don't lead to relapses.

## Why your nervous system matters

Your nervous system and immune system are closely connected. When your body stays in fight-or-flight for months, it affects sleep, inflammation, pain, and fatigue, which all matter in MS.

Many people with MS also live with constant worry about the next relapse. That fear can keep the body on alert too.

## Signs stress may be part of your flares

- Your relapses or worse days have followed hard seasons
- Your fatigue, pain, or numbness get worse when you're stressed
- You feel on guard much of the time, waiting for the next flare
- You struggle to rest even when you're exhausted

## Common questions

**Can stress trigger MS for the first time?** Research on this is limited. Most studies looked at relapses in people who already had MS, and stress isn't considered a cause of MS.

**What's the difference between a relapse and a pseudo-relapse?** A pseudo-relapse is a short-term worsening of old symptoms without new damage, often brought on by heat, infection, or stress. Your neurologist can help you tell the difference.

**Does lowering stress prevent relapses?** It may help. A clinical trial of stress-management therapy for people with MS found fewer new brain lesions during the treatment period, though the effect faded afterward and relapses didn't change. It doesn't replace your MS medication.

## Why it keeps coming back

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the stressful event itself. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *I have to keep going no matter what* or *It's not safe to let my guard down*, and today's stress is just turning up its volume.
