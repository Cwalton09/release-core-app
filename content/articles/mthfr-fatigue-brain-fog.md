Is MTHFR causing your fatigue and brain fog? Possibly in part, if your folate or B12 is actually low. But for most people with a common MTHFR variant, the gene alone isn't enough to explain constant exhaustion and fog. A nervous system that has been on guard for months or years causes the same symptoms, and it's often the bigger piece.

You wake up tired. You lose words mid-sentence. You walk into rooms and forget why. Coffee barely touches it. Somewhere along the way, someone told you it might be your MTHFR.

## What MTHFR does and doesn't do

MTHFR helps your body process folate. The common variants reduce how well that enzyme works, but the CDC says people with them can still process all types of folate, and their blood folate levels are only slightly lower on average.

Real B12 or folate deficiency can cause fatigue and trouble thinking. That's worth checking. But having the gene is not the same as having a deficiency. Many people with MTHFR variants have normal levels and no symptoms at all.

## How a stressed nervous system causes fatigue and fog

When your nervous system stays in protection mode, it spends a lot of energy staying ready for danger. Over time, that shows up as:

- **Wired-but-tired exhaustion:** worn out all day, then alert at night (see [why can't I sleep when I'm exhausted?](/articles/why-cant-i-sleep-when-exhausted))
- **Brain fog:** when your body is focused on survival, careful thinking takes a back seat (see [brain fog and the nervous system](/articles/brain-fog-nervous-system))
- **Crashes:** pushing through, then collapsing once things calm down (see [why do I crash after stress?](/articles/why-do-i-crash-after-stress))
- **Unrefreshing sleep:** sleeping, but never waking up rested

None of those require a gene. They're what happens when a body never gets the signal that it's safe to recover.

## What to ask your doctor

Fatigue and brain fog have many possible causes, so it's worth ruling things out:

- B12, folate, and homocysteine levels
- Iron and a complete blood count
- Thyroid function
- Vitamin D
- Sleep problems like sleep apnea

If those come back normal and you still feel exhausted and foggy, your nervous system is a strong place to look.

## Signs it's your nervous system

- The fog gets worse when you're stressed or overwhelmed
- You feel sharper on vacation or after real rest
- You're tired but can't relax
- It started during a demanding season of life
- Vitamins didn't change much

## Why it keeps coming back

Your nervous system doesn't keep you on guard for no reason. It's following a rule it learned, often something like *I can't rest until everything is done* or *if I slow down, something bad will happen*. Most of the time, we don't know which rule is running. We only feel the exhaustion it causes.
