Can stress cause back pain? Stress can trigger and worsen back pain. When your nervous system is under stress, your muscles tighten and brace, and your brain becomes more sensitive to pain. Research has also found that emotional distress and low mood are linked to back pain becoming long-lasting. So even when a scan looks fine, stress can be a big part of why your back keeps hurting.

A stressful week, and your lower back is screaming. Or the injury healed months ago, and the pain is still there.

## What research shows

In a 2002 systematic review, Tamar Pincus and colleagues looked at studies that followed people with low back pain over time. They found that distress, depressed mood, and somatization (feeling stress through the body) were linked to back pain moving from short-term to chronic.

That doesn't mean the pain is "in your head." It means your nervous system plays a real role in how pain develops and how long it lasts.

## How stress affects your back

- **Muscle bracing**: stress makes your back and core muscles tighten, as if preparing for danger
- **Pain sensitivity**: a stressed nervous system turns pain signals up
- **Shallow breathing** stiffens the muscles around your ribs and spine
- **Poor sleep** slows healing and lowers your pain threshold
- **Fear of movement** can keep you guarded, which keeps the muscles tight

Many people brace in their back without noticing, especially if they've carried a lot for a long time. That holding pattern can become your body's default. ## When to see a doctor

Get medical care right away if back pain comes with numbness in the groin or legs, trouble controlling your bladder or bowels, fever, unexplained weight loss, or pain after a fall or injury. Please also see a doctor for back pain that is severe, getting worse, or not improving.

## Common questions

**Where do you feel stress back pain?** Many people feel it in the lower back, upper back, or between the shoulder blades.

**Can anxiety cause back pain?** Yes. Anxiety keeps muscles tense and makes the nervous system more sensitive to pain.

**Why does my back hurt more when I'm stressed?** Stress increases muscle tension and turns up pain signals.
