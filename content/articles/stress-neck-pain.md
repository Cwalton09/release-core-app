Your neck is stiff when you wake up. By the afternoon, your shoulders are up by your ears. You rub the knots, you stretch, you get a massage, and a few days later it's right back.

You haven't lifted anything heavy. You didn't sleep wrong. So why does your neck hurt so much?

## What the research found

The trapezius is the big muscle that runs from the back of your head down your neck and across your shoulders. It's one of the places researchers look when they study how stress shows up in the body.

Across many lab studies, researchers have found that **mental stress alone**, like doing hard math under time pressure or being put on the spot, **increases activity in the trapezius**, even when people are sitting completely still.

A 2018 study in *The Journal of Headache and Pain* measured neck muscle activity in people with and without migraines. In every group, neck muscle activity went up during mental stress compared with rest.

Other research has found that people who already have neck and shoulder pain tend to show **even higher** trapezius activity during stressful tasks than people without pain.

## Why your body carries stress in your neck

When your body senses danger, it braces. Your shoulders rise, your neck stiffens, and your head pulls in, almost like a turtle protecting itself. That's a built-in protective reflex.

In a short emergency, that's useful. But if your nervous system stays on alert all day, those muscles never get the signal to let go. Over time, that low-level bracing can turn into stiffness, knots, headaches, and pain.

## Signs your neck pain is tied to stress

- Your neck and shoulders tighten during stressful days
- You notice your shoulders creeping up toward your ears
- The pain comes with tension headaches or a clenched jaw
- Massage helps, but the tightness comes right back
- It eases on vacation

Neck pain can also come from injuries, arthritis, nerve problems, and other medical causes. See a doctor for neck pain with numbness, weakness, fever, a severe headache, or after an injury.
