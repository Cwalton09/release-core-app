You walk into a room and forget why. You read the same sentence three times. Words are on the tip of your tongue and won't come out. Your mind feels slow, cloudy, and far away.

That's brain fog, and it's one of the most frustrating things to live with.

## What brain fog is

Brain fog isn't a diagnosis. It's a description of how it feels when thinking gets harder: trouble focusing, forgetfulness, slow thinking, and feeling mentally tired or detached.

It has many possible causes, including poor sleep, hormone changes, thyroid problems, low vitamin B12, illness, and medications. That's why it's important to talk to your doctor, especially if your brain fog is new or getting worse.

## How stress causes brain fog

Stress is one of the most common causes of brain fog, because chronic stress keeps your nervous system on high alert.

When your body is in protection mode, it puts its energy into survival: watching for danger, bracing, getting ready to act. Calm, focused thinking moves down the priority list. Your body isn't trying to make you forgetful. It's just busy keeping you safe.

## Signs your brain fog is connected to stress

- It gets worse during hard seasons or stressful weeks
- It shows up after conflict, overwhelm, or big emotions
- You feel foggy and far away when things get to be too much
- It lifts on calm days or when you feel safe
- You also feel tired, tense, or on edge

## Fog can be a form of shutdown

For some people, brain fog is part of a shutdown response. When something feels like too much, the nervous system can turn everything down, including your clarity, to get you through it. You can read more about [why you shut down when overwhelmed](/articles/why-do-i-shut-down-when-overwhelmed).
