Why aren't my affirmations working? Usually it's because your nervous system doesn't believe them yet. You can say "I am safe" or "I am worthy" over and over, but if your body has learned the opposite, it quietly rejects the words, and nothing changes in how you feel.

If you've ever stood in front of the mirror saying an affirmation while a little voice in the back of your head said "no you're not," you know exactly what I mean.

That little voice isn't you failing. It's your nervous system telling you the truth about what it currently believes.

## Your brain has a filter

Deep in your brain there's a network called the reticular activating system, or RAS. One of its jobs is to help decide what gets your attention out of everything coming at you every second.

It's often described as your brain's filter. It lets through what matches what you already expect and believe, and it tunes out the rest. That's why, once you decide you want a certain car, you suddenly see it everywhere. The car was always there. Your filter just started letting it through.

Here's the important part: the filter isn't set by what you say. It's set by what your nervous system has learned is true.

## Why repeating words doesn't reset the filter

When you say "I am successful" but your body learned long ago that "success isn't safe for people like me," the two are in conflict. Your words say one thing. Your nervous system's rules say another.

And your nervous system wins. Every time.

So the filter keeps showing you evidence for the old belief: the rejection, the bill, the person who didn't call back. Meanwhile the affirmation slides right off, because there's nothing in your body for it to attach to.

That's why you can repeat an affirmation for months and feel exactly the same. You're not doing it wrong. You're talking to the wrong part of you.

## Signs your affirmations are bouncing off

- You feel a "yeah, right" or a tightening in your body when you say them
- They feel like lying to yourself
- You feel good while saying them, then nothing changes in your life
- You notice more evidence for the opposite of what you're affirming
- You quit, then feel guilty that you "couldn't stick with it"

## Your nervous system responds to questions, not orders

An affirmation is a statement. And when your nervous system doesn't agree with a statement, it argues with it.

A question works differently. A question doesn't ask your body to accept something it doesn't believe. It invites your body to go looking for an experience.

That's why in Release Core we ask the nervous system questions like:

"Can you show me what it feels like to be completely relaxed while I'm speaking in front of a room?"

There's nothing to argue with there. Your body simply goes searching for what that would feel like. And when it finds even a little bit of it, the filter starts updating, because now there's a real experience in your body for the new belief to attach to. ## Affirmations still have a place

Affirmations aren't useless. In Release Core, we use them too. The difference is the order.

First, we find the old rule your nervous system is still following and where it started. Then we rewire it with "Can you show me what it feels like..." questions, so your body actually experiences the new way. Only then do the affirmations come in, and now they land, because your body already has a feeling to match them to.

Your nightly script is built the same way. It uses that filter on purpose, so the new beliefs are what your subconscious takes in as you fall asleep.

## Common questions

**Why don't affirmations work for me?** Most often, it's because your nervous system holds an older belief that contradicts the affirmation. Repeating the words doesn't change that belief, so your body rejects them and nothing shifts.

**What is the reticular activating system?** It's a network in your brainstem involved in alertness and attention. It's often described as a filter that helps decide what you notice, and what you notice tends to match what you already believe.

**Can affirmations make you feel worse?** They can. If an affirmation feels like a lie, saying it over and over can highlight the gap between where you are and where you want to be, and that can feel discouraging.

**What works better than affirmations?** Finding the belief your nervous system is actually running, then asking your body questions that let it experience the new belief, works better for most people. Affirmations work best after that, not instead of it.
