You've probably noticed it. A stressful month at work, a hard season at home, and suddenly you're catching every cold that goes around.

That's not just bad luck. Your nervous system and your immune system are closely connected.

## What the research found

One of the most important studies on this question is a 2004 review by Suzanne Segerstrom and Gregory Miller, published in Psychological Bulletin. They looked at nearly 300 studies, spanning more than 30 years and almost 19,000 people.

What they found depended on the kind of stress:

- Short bursts of stress, lasting minutes, actually boosted some parts of the immune system. That makes sense. Your body gears up to protect you.
- Brief real-life stressors, like exams, dampened some immune responses.
- Long-term, chronic stress was linked to lower immune function across the board.

They also found that older people and people who were already sick were more vulnerable to these effects.

## Why long-term stress matters

Your stress response is designed for short emergencies. When the stress is over, your body is supposed to settle and recover.

But when your nervous system stays on alert for months or years, your body doesn't get that recovery time. Over time, that ongoing activation is what the research links to weaker immune function.

## Signs stress may be affecting your health

- You catch colds and bugs more often during hard seasons
- It takes you longer to recover when you're sick
- You get sick right after a stressful stretch ends
- You feel run down, tired, and depleted most of the time

Getting sick often can have many causes, so talk to your doctor if it keeps happening. You can also read about [why you crash after stress is over](/articles/why-do-i-crash-after-stress).
