You've been doing everything right. Then a stressful week hits, and your joints ache, your skin flares, your gut acts up, or the fatigue comes back hard.

If you've noticed that your autoimmune symptoms get worse when life gets harder, you're not imagining it. It's one of the most common things people with autoimmune conditions report.

## What the research found

**Stress before onset and before flares.** A review in Autoimmunity Reviews by Stojanovich and Marisavljevich looked at the research on stress and autoimmune disease. They reported that in many studies, up to 80% of patients described unusual emotional stress before their disease began. They also noted that stress can worsen the disease once it's there, and that the disease itself creates more stress, a cycle that feeds itself. The authors recommended that care for autoimmune disease should include support for stress.

**Why stress turns up inflammation.** In 2012, Sheldon Cohen and his team at Carnegie Mellon published a study in PNAS that helps explain the "how." People who had been through long-term, threatening stress developed something called glucocorticoid receptor resistance. In plain terms, their bodies became less responsive to cortisol, the hormone that normally helps calm inflammation down.

When cortisol can't do its job, inflammation runs higher. In that study, the people with this resistance produced more inflammatory chemicals when they were exposed to a virus. For someone whose immune system already overreacts, higher inflammation can mean more flares.

## What these studies do and don't show

Much of the early research relies on people remembering stress after the fact, so it shows a pattern, not proof. Stress isn't the only flare trigger. Infections, hormones, sleep, food, and medication changes all matter too. But the link between stress and flares shows up again and again.

## What keeps the nervous system in fight or flight

Here's the part that often gets missed. You don't have to feel stressed for your nervous system to be in stress mode.

If your body learned somewhere along the way that it had to stay on guard, it can keep running that pattern in the background: tight, braced, scanning, ready. You might call it "just how I am." But to your body, it's a constant low-level emergency. And a body in constant emergency is a body with its stress hormones and inflammation turned up.

That's why a "small" stressful event can set off a big flare. Your body isn't only reacting to this week. It's reacting on top of everything it has been holding for years.

## Signs stress may be part of your flares

- Your flares often follow a stressful event, conflict, or deadline
- You flare right after a stressful stretch ends, when you finally relax
- You feel on edge or braced even on calm days
- Your symptoms got worse during a hard season of life
- You feel like your body never fully comes down

Keep tracking flares with your doctor and stay on your treatment plan. Stress support works alongside medical care, not instead of it.
