Can stress affect fertility? Research shows a modest link, not a simple cause. One large study found that women with the highest levels of a stress marker took longer to get pregnant. But another large review found that emotional distress did not lower the chances of pregnancy during IVF. So stress may play a role for some people, but it's never your fault, and "just relax" is not a fertility treatment.

You might be trying to conceive and feel like stress is making it harder, or feel pressure to stay calm because you've been told stress will hurt your chances.

## Get support

If you're under 35 and have been trying for a year, or 35 or older and trying for six months, please see a doctor. See a doctor sooner if your periods are irregular or you have a known condition. Infertility has many medical causes, in both partners, and many are treatable. A counselor who specializes in fertility can also help with the emotional strain.

## What research shows

The LIFE study, published in *Human Reproduction*, followed 501 couples trying to get pregnant. Women with the highest levels of salivary alpha-amylase, a marker of the body's stress response, had about a 29% lower chance of getting pregnant each cycle. They had about twice the risk of infertility compared with women with the lowest levels.

A 2011 meta-analysis in the *BMJ* looked at 14 studies of more than 3,500 women going through IVF and similar treatments. Emotional distress before treatment was not linked to whether they got pregnant. The authors said the findings should reassure women that their stress during treatment won't lower their chances.

## How stress may affect fertility

- **Shared hormone pathways.** The brain areas that respond to stress also help control ovulation.

## Common questions

**Can stress stop you from getting pregnant?** Stress is linked to taking longer to conceive in some studies, but it's rarely the only factor. See a doctor if it's taking a while.

**Does stress affect IVF success?** A large review found that emotional distress before treatment did not lower pregnancy rates.

**Is it my fault if I'm stressed?** No. Trying to conceive is stressful. Blaming yourself only adds more weight.
