Can stress make you age faster? Research suggests it can, at the level of your cells. A well-known study found that women with the highest levels of stress had telomeres, the protective caps on the ends of their DNA, that looked about a decade older than those of women with low stress. Telomeres naturally shorten with age, and stress appears to speed that up.

You might feel like a hard few years aged you, notice more gray hair, lines, or tiredness during stressful seasons, or worry about what stress is doing to your body long term.

## A note on aging

Aging is natural, and looking or feeling older is not a sign that you've failed. If you're worried about fatigue, memory, or other changes, please see a doctor, since many causes are treatable.

## What research shows

A 2004 study in the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* looked at healthy women, some of whom were caring for a child with a chronic illness. Both feeling stressed and being under stress for longer were linked to higher oxidative stress, lower telomerase activity, and shorter telomeres. Women with the highest perceived stress had telomeres shorter by the equivalent of at least ten years of extra aging compared with women with low stress.

## How stress affects aging

- **Oxidative stress.** Long-term stress raises cell-damaging molecules.
- **Less repair.** Telomerase, the enzyme that helps protect telomeres, was lower in stressed women.
- **Inflammation.** Stress keeps inflammation higher, which is linked to aging. See [does stress cause inflammation](/articles/does-stress-cause-inflammation).
- **Sleep.** Poor sleep affects repair throughout the body.

## The good news

Telomeres respond to how you live. Research suggests that sleep, movement, nourishing food, supportive relationships, and lower stress are linked to healthier cells. It's not too late to support your body.

## Common questions

**Can stress age your face?** Stress affects sleep, skin, and inflammation, which can show on your face.

**Can you reverse aging from stress?** Some changes may improve as stress comes down. Research on telomeres is still developing.

**What is a telomere?** A protective cap on the end of each chromosome, like the plastic tip on a shoelace.
