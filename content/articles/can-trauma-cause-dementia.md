Can trauma cause dementia? Trauma itself doesn't directly cause dementia, but research links PTSD with a higher risk of developing dementia later in life. A large review found that people with PTSD were about 1.6 times as likely to develop dementia. This is a link, not proof that one causes the other, and most people with PTSD will not develop dementia. Researchers call PTSD a potentially modifiable risk factor, which means it's something that can be treated.

If you've lived through trauma and notice memory problems, it's understandable to worry. Many memory problems after trauma are related to stress, poor sleep, and an overloaded nervous system, and they can improve.

## See a doctor if…

Please see a doctor if memory problems keep getting worse, if you get lost in familiar places, have trouble with everyday tasks, or if family members notice changes. Many causes of memory problems are treatable, like thyroid issues, vitamin B12 deficiency, sleep apnea, depression, and medication side effects. If you're struggling with PTSD, a trauma-informed therapist can help. If you're in crisis, call or text 988 in the US.

## What research shows

A 2020 meta-analysis in the *British Journal of Psychiatry* combined studies following more than 1.6 million people. PTSD was linked to a higher risk of all-cause dementia, with a hazard ratio of 1.61. The link held in veterans and in the general population. The authors noted that most studies were looking back in time and results varied a lot between studies, so more research is needed on whether treating PTSD lowers the risk.

## Why PTSD may affect the aging brain

Researchers think several things may play a role: long-term stress hormones, poor sleep, inflammation, and higher rates of heart and blood vessel problems in people with PTSD. All of these can affect the brain over time.

## What helps protect your brain

- Getting support for trauma and PTSD
- Good sleep
- Moving your body
- Staying socially connected
- Looking after blood pressure and heart health

## Common questions

**Does PTSD mean I'll get dementia?** No. It raises the risk on average, but most people with PTSD don't develop dementia.

**Can trauma-related memory problems get better?** Often, yes. Memory problems from stress and poor sleep can improve as your nervous system calms.

**Does treating PTSD lower dementia risk?** Researchers don't know yet. They suspect it may, which is one more reason to get support.
