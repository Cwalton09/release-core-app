Why can't you stop worrying something is wrong with your body? Health anxiety happens when your nervous system treats normal body sensations as signs of danger. Every twinge, headache, or flutter feels like it could be something serious. You search, check, and seek reassurance, and the relief never lasts. It's not that you're dramatic. Your nervous system has learned to scan your body for threat, and the more it scans, the more it finds.

You read about a symptom, and now you're sure you have it. The doctor says you're fine, and a week later you're worried about something new.

## Real symptoms still deserve care

Health anxiety doesn't mean nothing is ever wrong. Please keep seeing your doctor, and get urgent care for emergency symptoms. A good doctor can help you find a balance between checking things and getting stuck in the worry loop.

## How health anxiety works

Your nervous system constantly reads signals from inside your body, like your heartbeat, breathing, and digestion. This is called interoception. When your nervous system is on high alert, it pays more attention to those signals and is more likely to read them as dangerous.

Then a loop forms:

1. You notice a sensation
2. Your nervous system reads it as a threat
3. Anxiety rises, which creates more sensations, like a racing heart or tight chest
4. Those sensations feel like proof something is wrong
5. You search or check for reassurance, which calms you briefly
6. Your body learns that checking is how you stay safe, so you check more

You can read more in [hypervigilance: always scanning for danger](/articles/hypervigilance-always-scanning-for-danger).

## Why reassurance doesn't stick

Reassurance calms the thought, but not the nervous system's rule underneath. Your mind hears "you're fine." Your body still believes *something bad could be hiding* or *if I stop watching, I'll miss it*. So the worry comes back, often about a new symptom.

## Signs of health anxiety

- Googling symptoms often
- Checking your body, pulse, or skin repeatedly
- Needing reassurance that doesn't last
- Avoiding doctors, or seeing them very often
- One worry replacing another
- Feeling worse after reading about health

If health anxiety is taking over your life, a mental health professional can help. It's very treatable.

## Common questions

**What causes health anxiety?** It often starts after a health scare, an illness in someone close to you, a stressful season, or a nervous system that learned to stay on guard.

**Why do I always think I'm dying?** When your nervous system reads sensations as danger, your mind fills in the worst-case explanation.

**Is health anxiety the same as hypochondria?** Health anxiety is the more current term for what used to be called hypochondria.

## Why it keeps coming back

The worry keeps finding new targets because the rule underneath hasn't changed.

The real root is often very different from what you think. It's rarely about the symptom itself. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *I have to stay alert or something bad will happen* or *I can't relax until I know for sure*, and today's sensations are just turning up its volume.
