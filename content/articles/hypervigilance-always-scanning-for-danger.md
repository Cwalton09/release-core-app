You notice every sound in the house. You read the mood of a room the second you walk in. You sit where you can see the door. You replay conversations, looking for what you missed.

People might call you observant, sensitive, or a worrier. But on the inside, it can feel exhausting. Like you can never fully put your guard down.

That's called hypervigilance.

## What hypervigilance is

Hypervigilance is when your nervous system stays on high alert, constantly scanning for danger. It's not a choice or a personality flaw. It's a protective state your body learned to stay in.

## Where it comes from

Hypervigilance usually develops when, at some point, staying watchful actually helped you stay safe. Maybe you grew up needing to read an adult's mood to know what was coming. Maybe something happened that you didn't see coming, and your body decided never to be caught off guard again.

Your nervous system learned: "If I watch everything, I'll be safe." And it's still following that rule.

## Signs of hypervigilance

- You startle easily at sounds or sudden movement
- You constantly read people's faces and tone
- You notice small changes others miss
- You have trouble relaxing in new places
- You feel uneasy when things are quiet
- You plan for every possible thing that could go wrong
- You're exhausted from always being "on"

## Why you can't just turn it off

You can't talk yourself out of hypervigilance, because it isn't a thought. It's a body-level rule your nervous system believes is keeping you safe. Telling it to relax can make it feel even less safe. You can read more about [why you can't relax](/articles/why-cant-i-relax).

## Why it keeps coming back

Most of the time, we don't know the real reason behind what's driving the trigger. We can guess, but the actual trigger is usually something we would never think of on our own. And until it's found, your body will keep scanning for danger.

That's why you do a session. In a Release Core session, you gently activate the original trigger so it can be rewired, and your body communicates what the actual trigger is and where it started. You don't have to guess.
