Can trauma cause physical symptoms years later? Yes, research shows that trauma, especially in childhood, is linked to more physical health problems in adulthood. The large Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE) Study found that the more hard experiences people had as children, the higher their risk of heart disease, lung disease, and other serious conditions as adults. Trauma can keep your nervous system on alert for years, and that ongoing stress affects the whole body.

Many people never connect their symptoms with what happened. The pain, gut trouble, or exhaustion started long after, and nobody asked about their past.

## See a doctor for your symptoms

Physical symptoms always deserve a medical check, whatever your history. Trauma can be part of the picture without being the whole picture. If you're struggling with memories of trauma, a trauma-informed therapist can help. If you're in crisis, call or text 988 in the US.

## What research shows

**The ACE Study.** In this 1998 study of more than 9,500 adults, people were asked about seven kinds of hard childhood experiences, such as abuse, violence at home, or living with someone with addiction or mental illness. The more of these experiences people had, the higher their risk of many leading causes of death in adulthood, including heart disease, lung disease, and liver disease.

**A large review.** A 2017 review in *The Lancet Public Health* combined 37 studies with more than 250,000 people. People with four or more ACEs had higher risks of every health outcome studied, including heart disease, respiratory disease, cancer, and poor overall health.

Part of this link comes from coping behaviors like smoking or drinking. But researchers also point to long-term stress itself, which affects inflammation, hormones, and the nervous system.

## How trauma stays in the body

When something overwhelming happens, your nervous system learns from it. It may decide the world isn't safe and stay on alert to protect you. Years later, it can still be running that protection: tense muscles, a sensitive gut, poor sleep, high stress hormones, and a body that never fully rests.

Over time, that wear and tear can show up as physical symptoms. See [is trauma stored in the body](/articles/is-trauma-stored-in-the-body) and [where trauma is stored in the body](/articles/where-is-trauma-stored-in-the-body).

## Signs past trauma may be part of your symptoms

- Your symptoms started or worsened during a stressful time or after a reminder of the past
- You've had many symptoms in different parts of your body
- Tests haven't fully explained how you feel
- You feel on guard, startle easily, or struggle to relax
- You had a hard childhood, even if it felt "normal" at the time

## Common questions

**Can trauma you don't remember affect your body?** Your body can carry learning from experiences you don't consciously remember, especially from early childhood. This is called implicit memory.

**Does everyone with trauma get physical symptoms?** No. ACEs raise risk, but many people with hard childhoods stay healthy. Support and safety make a real difference.

**Can these symptoms improve?** Yes. Your nervous system can keep learning throughout your life.

## Why it keeps coming back

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the trauma story you can tell. It's the rule your body learned during it, like *I'm not safe* or *I have to handle everything alone*, and your nervous system keeps following that rule today.

When your body keeps reacting years later, your nervous system is likely still protecting you from that time. Most of us don't know what it's still reacting to.
