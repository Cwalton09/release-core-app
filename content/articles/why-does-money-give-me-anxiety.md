You open your banking app and your stomach drops before the numbers even load. A bill shows up and your whole body tenses. You avoid checking your balance for days, then feel worse for avoiding it.

If money makes you anxious, you're far from alone. But for a lot of people, the reaction is much bigger than the numbers on the screen.

## What the research found

The American Psychological Association has run its Stress in America survey every year since 2007. In its 2014 survey of 3,068 adults, money was the top source of stress, as it had been every year since the survey began.

- **64%** of adults said money was a somewhat or very significant source of stress
- **72%** said they had felt stressed about money at some point in the past month
- **22%** said they had felt extreme stress about money
- Parents, millennials, and Gen Xers reported the highest money stress, around **75%**

The APA also found that people with high money stress were more likely to report physical symptoms of stress and were more likely to skip health care they needed.

## When the reaction doesn't match the numbers

Some money anxiety makes sense. Bills are real, and so is a tight month.

But many people feel the same dread when there's plenty in the account. The panic shows up even when nothing is wrong. That's a clue that your body isn't reacting to today's balance. It's reacting to what money *meant* the first time you learned about it.

If you grew up hearing fights about bills, watching a parent panic over money, or feeling like there was never enough, your nervous system may have connected money with danger. Now any money moment sets off the same alarm.

## Signs your nervous system is involved

- Your heart races or your stomach knots when you check your accounts
- You avoid opening bills, statements, or emails about money
- You feel guilty or uneasy when you spend on yourself
- Even good financial news doesn't feel safe
- You expect something bad to happen whenever you get ahead
- Money conversations make you freeze or get defensive
