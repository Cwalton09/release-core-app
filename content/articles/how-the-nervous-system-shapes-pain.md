Most of us think about pain like a wire. Something gets hurt, a signal travels up to the brain, and you feel it.

But pain is more complicated than that, and understanding it can change how you see your own body.

## Your nervous system decides how loud pain feels

When something happens in your body, nerves send signals up through your spinal cord to your brain. But those signals don't arrive at a fixed volume.

Your nervous system acts like a mixing board. It can turn the signal up or down depending on what it believes is happening. Is this dangerous? Has this hurt before? Am I safe right now?

That's why the same injury can feel very different from one day to the next, or from one person to another.

## Pain is protection

Pain's job is to protect you. It tells you to stop, rest, and pay attention.

So when your nervous system believes you're in danger, it can turn pain up to make sure you listen, even if the tissue itself isn't in much danger at all.

## What can turn the volume up

- Stress and anxiety
- Poor sleep
- Fear about what the pain means
- Feeling alone, unsupported, or trapped
- Past experiences of pain or injury
- A nervous system that has been on alert for a long time

And what can turn it down? Feeling safe, supported, rested, and understood.

## This doesn't mean it's in your head

Your pain is real. A nervous system that turns pain up is producing real pain, not imagined pain. This is about how pain works, not whether it's real.

Please see your doctor about pain that's new, severe, getting worse, or comes with other symptoms like numbness, weakness, or fever.
