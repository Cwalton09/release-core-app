What happens when you ask your body a question instead of saying an affirmation? Your nervous system stops arguing and starts searching. An affirmation asks your body to accept something it may not believe. A question invites it to go find out what that would feel like, and that's when real change starts.

Try this for a second.

Say to yourself: "I am completely calm."

Notice what your body does. For a lot of people there's a little pushback. A "no I'm not." A tightening.

Now try: "Can you show me what it feels like to be completely calm?"

Notice the difference. Instead of pushing back, something in you gets curious. It starts looking.

That small shift is the heart of how Release Core works.

## Statements create an argument

When you say an affirmation your nervous system doesn't believe, you've set up a debate. You say "I'm safe." Your body, which has years of evidence that it isn't, says "no, you're not."

And your body doesn't lose that debate. It has more evidence than you do, and it's been practicing its side for a long time. I go deeper into why in [why your affirmations aren't working](/articles/why-affirmations-dont-work).

## Questions create a search

A question doesn't ask your body to agree with anything. It gives it a job.

When you ask "Can you show me what it feels like to be completely relaxed while I'm talking to my boss?", your nervous system does what brains love to do with a question: it goes looking for the answer. It searches for even a small experience of that feeling.

There's nothing to resist, because you haven't claimed anything. You've just asked.

## What people notice when they start asking

- A softening somewhere, often the shoulders, jaw, chest, or belly
- A deeper breath they didn't try to take
- A picture, memory, or feeling that comes up out of nowhere
- Sometimes resistance, which is useful too, because it shows exactly what still needs attention

And every time your body finds even 10% of the feeling, it's having a real experience of the new way, not just hearing about it. That's the difference between knowing something and your body believing it.

## Why we start small

Sometimes the full feeling is too big of a jump. If your body has been on guard for 20 years, "show me what it feels like to be completely relaxed" can feel impossible.

So we make the question smaller: "Can you show me what it feels like to soften just 10%?" A small step your body can actually take is worth more than a big one it refuses.

## How the questions fit into a session

In a Release Core session, the questions come after we find what's really going on.

First, your body shows you the rule it's still following, through simple yes and no answers. Then you find where that rule started and what your nervous system learned back then.

Then comes the rewire, and that's where the questions come in:

- "Can you show me what it feels like to be safe even when someone is upset with me?"
- "Can you show me what it feels like to rest without having to earn it?"
- "Can you show me what it feels like to receive money and feel calm about it?"

After that, the affirmations finally land, because your body already has a feeling to match them to. And your nightly script closes with a few of these questions, so your nervous system keeps practicing the new feeling as you fall asleep.

## Common questions

**Why do questions work better than affirmations?** A statement your body doesn't believe creates resistance. A question doesn't claim anything, so there's nothing to resist. Your nervous system simply goes looking for the experience you asked about.

**What kind of questions should I ask my body?** Questions that invite a felt experience work best, like "Can you show me what it feels like to..." followed by the state you want. Keeping them small and specific makes them easier for your body to answer.

**What if nothing happens when I ask?** That's information too. It often means there's an older belief in the way, and finding that belief is usually the next step.

## Why it keeps coming back

Most of the time, we don't know the real reason our body pushes back. We can guess, but the actual belief is usually something we would never think of on our own. And until it's found, your body will keep resisting the change you want.

That's why you do a session. In a Release Core session, you gently activate the original trigger so it can be rewired, and your body communicates what the actual trigger is and where it started. You don't have to guess.
