Why are your muscles twitching all over? Small, harmless twitches, called fasciculations, are very common, and stress and anxiety are among the most frequent triggers. When your nervous system is on high alert, your nerves fire more easily, so muscles can flicker in your arms, legs, eyelids, or all over your body, often when you're resting. Caffeine, poor sleep, and exercise can make it worse.

The twitching itself is rarely dangerous. But it can be scary, especially if you start searching online and land on frightening conditions.

## When to see a doctor

Most twitching is benign. Please see a doctor if twitching comes with muscle weakness, muscle wasting, trouble swallowing or speaking, cramping that's getting worse, or loss of coordination. Those signs need evaluation.

If your doctor examines you and finds no weakness, that's very reassuring.

## How stress causes twitching

When your nervous system senses danger, it gets your muscles ready to move. Nerves become more excitable, and adrenaline keeps your body primed. Some of those nerve signals fire on their own, and you feel them as little flickers or jumps under your skin.

If your nervous system stays on alert for weeks or months, the twitching can keep going. Doctors sometimes call ongoing harmless twitching benign fasciculation syndrome, and anxiety is commonly linked to it.

## Why the worry makes it worse

Twitching often sets off a loop. You notice a twitch, you worry about what it means, the worry raises your stress, and the stress causes more twitching. Then you notice more, because you're watching for it.

This is how a nervous system on alert works: it pays more attention to body sensations, and attention makes them feel bigger. More on that in [hypervigilance](/articles/hypervigilance-always-scanning-for-danger).

## Common triggers

- Stress and anxiety
- Caffeine and energy drinks
- Poor sleep
- Intense exercise
- Dehydration or low magnesium
- Some medications

## Common questions

**Can anxiety cause muscle twitching all over?** Yes. Anxiety is one of the most common causes of harmless twitching.

**Why do my muscles twitch when I'm resting?** At rest, you notice twitches you'd miss when moving, and a revved-up nervous system often fires more when you're still.

**Is eye twitching from stress?** Very often. See [eye twitching and stress](/articles/eye-twitching-stress).
