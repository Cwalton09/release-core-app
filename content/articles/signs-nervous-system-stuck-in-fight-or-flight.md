Fight or flight is supposed to be temporary. Something feels threatening, your body gears up to handle it, and when it's over, you settle back down.

But for a lot of people, the "settle back down" part never really happens. The alarm just stays on. Not always loud, but always running in the background.

If you've been feeling wired, tense, or on edge for so long that it just feels like your personality, this might be what's going on.

## Common signs

- You feel on edge even when nothing is wrong
- It's hard to relax, and rest can actually make you feel more anxious
- Your shoulders, jaw, or stomach are tight most of the time
- You startle easily or feel jumpy
- Small things make you irritable or snappy
- Your mind races, especially at night
- You wake up already feeling wired or dreading the day
- You feel like you always have to be doing something
- You scan rooms, people, or conversations for what might go wrong
- You feel like you can never fully let your guard down

None of these on their own means something is wrong with you. But if several of them feel familiar, and they've been going on for a while, your body may be stuck in protection mode.

## Why it gets stuck

Your nervous system stays in fight or flight when it still believes there's something to protect you from.

Sometimes that's obvious. You're under a lot of pressure, or something hard is happening in your life right now.

But often, the threat your body is responding to isn't really about today. It's about something it learned earlier. Maybe it learned that you had to stay alert to stay safe. Maybe it learned that relaxing meant something bad would happen. Maybe it learned that it was your job to hold everything together.

Your body doesn't keep that alarm on to make your life harder. It keeps it on because, at some point, it decided that staying ready was what kept you okay.

## Why "just relax" doesn't work

If you've ever been told to just relax, you know how unhelpful that is.

You can't relax your way out of something your body believes is necessary. If it thinks staying on guard is keeping you safe, it's going to resist letting go, no matter how much you want it to.

That's also why some people feel worse when they finally slow down. Taking a break, going on vacation, or lying down to rest can make your system feel exposed, because it's being asked to stop doing the thing it thinks is protecting you.
