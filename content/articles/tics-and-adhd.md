If your child has tics, there's a good chance someone has also mentioned ADHD. Or maybe it's you: easily overwhelmed, restless, and noticing the tics get louder on the hardest days.

Tics and ADHD show up together so often that researchers have studied the overlap closely.

## What the research found

In 2007, Roger Freeman and colleagues published findings in European Child and Adolescent Psychiatry from a worldwide clinical database of 6,805 people with Tourette syndrome.

**About 55% of them also had ADHD.** Earlier work from the same international database found that only around 12% of people with Tourette's had no other condition at all. ADHD, anxiety, and OCD were the most common companions.

People with both tics and ADHD also tended to have more challenges overall, like more anger and behavior struggles, than those with tics alone.

## Why tics and ADHD overlap

Both tics and ADHD involve the brain's systems for controlling movement, attention, and impulses. When those systems are under strain, it gets harder to filter out urges, stay focused, and stay calm.

Stress affects both. Overwhelm makes it harder to focus, harder to sit still, and harder to hold tics back. Many families notice the same pattern: on the hardest days, everything gets louder at once.

## What keeps the nervous system in fight or flight

A nervous system that runs hot gets overloaded faster. Noise, demands, transitions, and big emotions can push it past what it can handle, and when that happens, the tics and the ADHD symptoms both turn up.

For many kids and adults, there's also a layer underneath: years of being told to sit still, be quiet, or stop ticking. A body that learned it was "too much" or always in trouble can stay braced all the time, and a braced body has even less room to cope.

## Signs overwhelm is turning tics and ADHD up

- Tics get worse on days with more demands or transitions
- Focus and tics both fall apart when tired or stressed
- Big emotional reactions come right before tic flares
- Holding it together at school or work leads to a crash at home
- Feeling like you're always "too much" or always in trouble

Tics and ADHD both benefit from professional support. Talk to your doctor about evaluation and options, including behavioral approaches with strong research behind them.
