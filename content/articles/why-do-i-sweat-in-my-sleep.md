You wake up and the sheets are damp. Your pajamas are stuck to you. Maybe your heart is pounding, or you woke up from a bad dream. It's not hot in the room, so why are you drenched?

Night sweats can have a lot of causes. But one that people rarely talk about is the nervous system.

## What the research found

In 2002, researchers led by James Mold published a study in the *Journal of Family Practice* that surveyed **2,267 adults** visiting primary care doctors.

- **41%** said they'd had night sweats in the past month
- Night sweats were most common in people in their **40s and 50s**
- In women, night sweats were linked with **hot flashes and panic attacks**
- In men, they were linked with **sleep problems**
- Most people had **never mentioned** their night sweats to their doctor

## How your nervous system makes you sweat

Sweating is controlled by your sympathetic nervous system, the "fight or flight" side. When your body senses danger, it gets you ready to run, and part of that is sweating to cool you down.

That system doesn't turn off just because you're asleep. A nightmare, a nighttime panic attack, or a body that's stuck on high alert can all trigger it.

Panic attacks during sleep are more common than many people realize. Reviews of people with panic disorder have found that **44% to 71%** have had at least one panic attack that woke them up.

## Other causes to rule out

Night sweats can also come from:

- Menopause and perimenopause
- Infections
- Medications, including some antidepressants
- Low blood sugar
- Alcohol
- Thyroid problems
- Sleep apnea
- Rarely, cancers like lymphoma

**See your doctor**, especially if night sweats come with fever, weight loss you can't explain, or swollen glands.

## Signs your nervous system may be involved

- You wake up sweaty with a racing heart
- You wake from nightmares or with a sense of dread
- Night sweats get worse in stressful seasons
- You have panic attacks or a lot of anxiety in the day
- Your doctor hasn't found another cause
