Why does your heart race when you lie down? Often because night is when you finally notice it. The British Heart Foundation explains that for most people, palpitations feel worse at night simply because there's less noise and distraction, so you're more aware of your body. Anxiety, caffeine, alcohol, and some medications can also cause palpitations. And when your nervous system is still on alert from the day, lying down in the quiet can bring that activation to the surface.

You get into bed, the room goes quiet, and you feel your heart pounding in your chest or your ears. Then you start worrying about it, and it beats even faster.

## Please check with a doctor first

Palpitations can sometimes be caused by a heart condition. If your heart racing is new, happens often, or comes with chest pain, shortness of breath, dizziness, or fainting, get medical care. Chest pain or fainting needs urgent care right away. See also [can stress cause chest pain?](/articles/can-stress-cause-chest-pain)

## Why you notice it more at night

During the day, your attention is pulled in a hundred directions. At night, it's just you and your body. Lying still in a quiet room, you can feel your heartbeat in a way you never notice at your desk. Lying on your left side can also make your heartbeat easier to feel.

## The anxiety loop

Feeling your heart race can be scary. That fear tells your nervous system something is wrong, which releases more adrenaline, which makes your heart beat faster. Soon the worry and the racing are feeding each other.

This is why heart racing at night is so common for people who are stressed or anxious. See [heart palpitations and anxiety](/articles/heart-palpitations-anxiety-nervous-system) and [signs your nervous system is stuck in fight or flight](/articles/signs-nervous-system-stuck-in-fight-or-flight).

## Other things that can play a part

- Caffeine, energy drinks, or nicotine
- Alcohol, especially as it wears off
- Some medications and decongestants
- Dehydration or eating a big meal late
- Hormone changes, like around your period, pregnancy, or menopause
- Thyroid problems

## Common questions

**Why does my heart race when I lie down to sleep?** You're more aware of your heartbeat in the quiet, and a nervous system still on alert can make it beat faster.

**Can anxiety make your heart race at night?** Yes. Anxiety releases adrenaline, which speeds up your heart, and worrying about it can keep the cycle going.

**Why does my heart race when I wake up?** Waking suddenly, a dream, or [waking up with anxiety](/articles/why-do-i-wake-up-with-anxiety) can all set off a quick surge of adrenaline.

## Why it keeps coming back

When your heart keeps racing as soon as you lie down, your nervous system may be treating rest as the moment to stay on guard.

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the bed, the bedtime, or the day you had. It's often an old rule your body learned long before, like *nighttime isn't safe* or *I can't let my guard down*, and today's stress is just turning up its volume.
