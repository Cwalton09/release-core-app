Can stress cause shingles? It may play a role, but the research is mixed. Shingles happens when the chickenpox virus, which stays in your nerves for life, wakes back up. That usually happens when your immune system is weaker. Stress can affect your immune system, and some studies have found more shingles after stressful life events, while other large studies have not. Age and a weakened immune system remain the biggest risk factors.

A lot of people say the same thing: "I got shingles right after the hardest month of my life." That experience is real, even if science is still sorting out how big a role stress plays.

## See a doctor quickly

If you think you have shingles, a painful rash, often in a band on one side of your body, see a doctor as soon as possible. Antiviral medicine works best when it's started early, and it can lower the risk of long-lasting nerve pain. Get urgent care if the rash is near your eye.

The shingles vaccine is the best way to lower your risk. Ask your doctor if it's right for you.

## How stress affects your immune system

When your nervous system is under long-term stress, it keeps stress hormones like cortisol high. Over time, that can dampen parts of the immune system that keep viruses in check, including the T cells that keep the chickenpox virus asleep.

You can read more in [does stress weaken your immune system?](/articles/does-stress-weaken-your-immune-system)

## What the research shows

- Some studies have linked stressful life events, high perceived stress, and low sense of purpose with a higher chance of shingles.
- Other large studies, including one of about 40,000 people after sudden major stressors like losing a spouse, found no clear increase in shingles.

So stress is a plausible factor for some people, but not a proven, consistent trigger. Being honest about that matters, because shingles is never your fault.

## Signs stress may be part of your picture

- You've been under heavy stress for a long time
- You're getting sick more often than usual
- You feel run down, wired, or exhausted
- You haven't had a real chance to rest or recover

## Common questions

**Can anxiety cause shingles?** There's no strong evidence that anxiety directly causes shingles. Long-term stress may affect your immune system, which is one of the things that keeps the virus dormant.

**Who is most at risk for shingles?** People over 50 and people with weakened immune systems are at highest risk.

**Can shingles come back with stress?** Shingles can come back, though it's less common. Talk to your doctor about vaccination.
