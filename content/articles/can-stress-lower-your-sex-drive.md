Can stress lower your sex drive? Yes. Desire and arousal depend on the rest-and-digest side of your nervous system. When you're stressed, your body shifts into fight-or-flight, which is the opposite state. Stress hormones, a distracted mind, and a body on guard can all turn desire down. A study in women found that chronic stress was linked to lower physical arousal, higher cortisol, and more distraction.

You might feel too tired, too tense, or too "in your head" to want sex, even with a partner you love.

## See a doctor if…

Low desire can come from hormone changes, birth control, antidepressants and other medications, thyroid problems, pain during sex, depression, and relationship stress. Please see a doctor if it's a sudden change, if sex is painful, or if it's causing distress. Low sex drive is common and treatable.

## How your nervous system shapes desire

Arousal needs your body to feel safe enough to relax, open, and receive. That happens through the parasympathetic nervous system. Fight-or-flight moves blood and attention toward survival and away from pleasure.

When your nervous system stays on alert, your body may treat intimacy as one more demand instead of something good.

## What research shows

In a 2013 study in *The Journal of Sexual Medicine*, women with high chronic stress had lower genital arousal while watching an erotic film than women with average stress. They also had higher cortisol and reported more distraction. Distraction was the strongest predictor of lower arousal. Their reported mental arousal was similar, which shows how stress can disconnect the body from what the mind wants.

## Signs stress may be behind it

- Your desire dropped during a stressful season
- You want to want it, but your body doesn't respond
- Your mind races or drifts during intimacy
- You feel touched-out or tense

## Common questions

**Can anxiety kill your sex drive?** It can. Anxiety keeps your body on alert, which makes it hard to relax into desire.

**Will my sex drive come back after stress?** For many people, yes, as stress and sleep improve. If it doesn't, talk with a doctor.

**Can stress cause erectile dysfunction?** Yes, stress and anxiety are common causes. A doctor can also check for physical causes.
