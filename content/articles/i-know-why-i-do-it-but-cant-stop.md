Why can't you stop a pattern even when you understand it? Because understanding happens in your thinking mind, and the pattern lives in your nervous system. Knowing why you do something doesn't automatically update the rule your body learned. And there's a second problem: the reason you've figured out may not be the rule that's actually running.

You can explain your patterns perfectly. You've read the books. You can say exactly how your childhood shaped you. And you still do the thing.

## Insight is a start, not a switch

Understanding your patterns isn't useless. It can bring relief, reduce shame, and make you more curious about yourself. Therapy and self-reflection can be genuinely valuable.

But insight is information. Your nervous system doesn't change because it received information. It changes because it has a new experience. Your mind can know you're safe while your body still believes you're not.

## Your explanation might be a guess

Here's something research has shown for decades. In a well-known 1977 paper titled "Telling More Than We Can Know," psychologists Richard Nisbett and Timothy Wilson reviewed studies showing that people often can't accurately report what's actually driving their behavior. When asked, they give confident, sensible explanations, and those explanations are often wrong.

We don't have direct access to the processes behind our reactions. So the mind does what it always does: it builds a story that fits.

That means your "I know why I do it" may be a very good theory. But it may not be the rule your body learned.

## What's really running is usually hidden

A lot of what drives your reactions is stored as [implicit memory](/articles/what-is-implicit-memory), lessons your nervous system learned without a conscious memory attached. Those rules don't sound like explanations. They sound like:

- If I rest, something bad will happen
- If I need something, I'll be too much
- If I relax, I'll get blindsided
- If I'm seen, I'll be judged
- If I let go, I'll lose it forever

You might think you people-please because your mom was critical. Your body might be running *if someone is upset, it's my job to fix it, or I'm not safe*. Close, but not the same. And only one of them is the rule that has to change.

In one Release Core session, a boy who always wanted the next new thing and always followed his friends' lead thought he just liked new stuff and liked going along. His body showed two rules he had never noticed: *getting something new means I'm loved and important*, and *if I make my own choice, I'll be judged, so it's safer to let someone else decide*. Nobody would have guessed that from the outside, including him.

## Why the wrong reason keeps you stuck

If you're working on the wrong rule, nothing shifts. You understand more and more, and the pattern keeps running, which can make you feel broken or hopeless. You're not broken. You're just aiming at a target that isn't there.

In Release Core sessions, the most useful moments are often when the body says no. Someone is sure the issue is X, and their body answers no. That no is the clue. It points away from the story and toward the real rule.

## Common questions

**Why do I keep doing things I know are bad for me?** Because the behavior is likely protecting you from something your nervous system still sees as a threat. See [why do I self-sabotage?](/articles/why-do-i-self-sabotage)

**Is insight enough to change behavior?** Often it isn't. Many people understand their patterns deeply and still repeat them, because understanding doesn't by itself update what the nervous system learned.

**What is the difference between knowing and healing?** Knowing is your mind understanding the pattern. Healing is your body learning it doesn't need the pattern anymore.

## Why it keeps coming back

Most of the time, we don't actually know the pattern that's running. We have a theory, and the theory feels right because it's logical. But your body learned its rules through experience, not logic.

That's why you do a session. In a Release Core session, you don't have to guess. Your body shows you the exact rule it's following, the age it started, and what it was protecting you from.
