You've heard the stories. A husband dies, and weeks later his wife of 50 years follows. People say she died of a broken heart.

It sounds like poetry. But doctors now know there's a real medical condition behind it. It's called takotsubo cardiomyopathy, or **broken heart syndrome**, and it shows just how powerfully your nervous system can affect your heart.

## What broken heart syndrome is

Broken heart syndrome happens when part of the heart muscle suddenly weakens and stops pumping normally. It can cause chest pain and shortness of breath that feel exactly like a heart attack.

But when doctors look, the arteries aren't blocked. Instead, the heart has been stunned, most likely by a sudden flood of stress hormones like adrenaline.

The name comes from Japan. On imaging, the weakened heart balloons into a shape that looks like a *takotsubo*, a traditional pot used to catch octopus.

## What the research found

In 2015, researchers published the largest study of the condition at the time in the *New England Journal of Medicine*. They followed **1,750 patients** from 26 hospitals in Europe and the United States.

- **About 90% were women**, with an average age of 67
- Physical stress, like surgery, an asthma attack, or a serious illness, came before about **36%** of cases
- Emotional stress, like grief, fear, or a fight, came before about **28%**
- In about **28%**, there was no clear trigger at all
- During the hospital stay, serious complications like shock and death happened about as often as they did in people having a typical heart attack

People with broken heart syndrome were also more likely to have a history of neurological or mental health conditions, like anxiety or depression.

A later study from the same registry found that even **happy events**, like a wedding or the birth of a grandchild, can sometimes trigger it. It's not just sadness. It's an overwhelming surge.

## Where the nervous system comes in

Your heart is wired directly to your nervous system. When your body senses a major threat, your "fight or flight" system releases a wave of stress hormones to get you ready to act.

Usually, that surge passes. But in broken heart syndrome, it seems to overwhelm the heart muscle itself.

Most people recover their heart function within weeks. But the condition shows something important: emotions aren't just "in your head." Your body feels them, sometimes with real force.

## Signs to take seriously

- Sudden chest pain or pressure
- Shortness of breath
- A racing or irregular heartbeat
- Fainting or nearly fainting
- Symptoms that start after a shock, a loss, or extreme stress

**Chest pain is always an emergency. Call 911.** Doctors can't tell broken heart syndrome apart from a heart attack without tests.
