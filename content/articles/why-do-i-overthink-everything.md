You send a text and immediately start wondering if it came across wrong. You replay a conversation from three days ago. You lie awake running through every way tomorrow could go badly.

People tell you to "just stop overthinking." If only it were that easy.

Overthinking isn't a personality flaw, and it isn't a sign that something is wrong with your brain. More often, it's your nervous system trying to keep you safe.

## Overthinking is a form of protection

When your body feels unsafe, your mind goes to work. It tries to predict what's coming so you can be ready. It tries to figure out what you did wrong so it won't happen again. It tries to solve problems before they even exist.

That's what overthinking is: your mind trying to create safety by staying one step ahead.

The problem is, it never feels finished. There's always one more scenario to plan for, one more thing to double-check. Because the thinking isn't really the root. The feeling of not being safe is.

## Common types of overthinking

- Replaying conversations and analyzing what you said
- Imagining worst-case scenarios
- Second-guessing decisions, even small ones
- Reading into other people's tone, texts, or facial expressions
- Needing to know exactly how things will turn out
- Thinking through every possible option before you can act

## Where it often comes from

A lot of overthinkers learned early that they had to stay alert to stay okay.

Maybe you had to read the room to know what kind of mood someone was in. Maybe mistakes got a big reaction. Maybe things felt unpredictable, so your mind learned to plan for everything. Maybe love felt conditional, so you learned to watch closely for signs you'd done something wrong.

Your mind became really good at scanning and predicting. It was protecting you. It just never got the message that it can stop.

## Why overthinking gets worse at night

When everything gets quiet, there's nothing to distract your mind. And if your body is still holding tension from the day, your mind goes looking for a reason. ## What helps in the moment

- Notice it without judging it: "My mind is trying to protect me right now."
- Bring your attention into your body. Where do you feel the tension?
- Breathe out slowly, longer than you breathe in.
- Write the thoughts down so your mind doesn't have to hold them.
- Ask, "Is there anything I actually need to do about this right now?" If not, you're allowed to set it down.

## What helps long term

You can't think your way out of overthinking. The thinking is a symptom. What quiets it is helping your body feel safe enough that your mind doesn't have to stand guard.

Underneath most overthinking there's a core belief, like "If I make a mistake, something bad will happen," "I have to have everything figured out," or "People will leave if I get it wrong." When you find that belief and give your body what it needed back then, the overthinking often loosens on its own.
