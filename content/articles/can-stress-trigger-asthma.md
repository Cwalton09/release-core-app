Can stress trigger asthma? Stress can make asthma worse. It doesn't cause asthma on its own, but research shows stressful events can raise the risk of asthma attacks in the weeks that follow. Your nervous system controls how open your airways are, and stress changes how strongly your airways react to triggers like allergens, smoke, and colds.

You might notice more wheezing or chest tightness during hard weeks, flares after an upsetting event, or needing your rescue inhaler more often.

## Get help right away if…

Asthma attacks can be serious. Call 911 if you're struggling to breathe, can't speak in full sentences, your lips or fingernails look blue or gray, or your rescue inhaler isn't helping. Keep taking your asthma medications as prescribed and follow your asthma action plan. Talk with your doctor if your asthma is flaring more often.

## What research shows

A study in *The Lancet* followed children with asthma for 18 months. A severe negative life event raised the risk of a new asthma attack in the following weeks. When that event happened on top of high ongoing stress, the risk rose sharply and much sooner.

A review in *Brain, Behavior, and Immunity* explains how. Stress doesn't act alone. It changes how strongly the airways react to irritants, allergens, and infections, through the stress hormone system and both branches of the autonomic nervous system.

## How stress affects your airways

- **Stronger inflammation.** Stress can turn up the airway's reaction to triggers.
- **Changes in breathing.** Fast or shallow breathing during stress can irritate sensitive airways.
- **Nervous system signals.** The nerves that tighten and relax your airways respond to stress.
- **Harder to manage.** Stress can make it harder to keep up with medications and avoid triggers.

## Common questions

**Can anxiety feel like asthma?** Yes. Anxiety can cause chest tightness and shortness of breath. Only a doctor can tell the difference, so get new breathing symptoms checked.

**Can emotional stress cause an asthma attack?** It can help trigger one, especially with other triggers present.

**Can calming down help my asthma?** It can support your asthma care, but it never replaces your medications.
