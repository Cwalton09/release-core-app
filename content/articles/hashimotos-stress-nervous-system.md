Hashimoto's thyroiditis is the most common autoimmune thyroid condition. It can leave you exhausted, foggy, cold, anxious, or flat, often all at once.

A lot of women with Hashimoto's say the same thing: it started, or got worse, during the hardest season of their life. So is there a real connection to stress?

## What the research found

The honest answer is that the research is mixed, and that's worth knowing.

**One study found no clear link.** A study of 759 people with normal thyroid function compared those who had thyroid antibodies with those who didn't. Their levels of daily hassles and stressful life events weren't meaningfully different. Part of the challenge is that Hashimoto's often develops quietly for years before anyone notices, which makes it hard to line up with a specific stressful event.

**A trial found stress support helped.** In 2019, researchers in Greece ran a randomized controlled trial with 60 women who had Hashimoto's. Half followed an 8-week stress management program, and half received standard care. The women in the stress program had a significant drop in one thyroid antibody (anti-TG), along with lower stress, depression, and anxiety, compared with the women who didn't.

**The bigger picture.** Large studies have linked stress-related disorders with a higher risk of autoimmune disease in general, and long-term stress is known to keep inflammation running higher. Hashimoto's sits within that larger picture, even if the thyroid-specific research is still catching up.

## What this does and doesn't mean

Stress doesn't cause Hashimoto's on its own. Genetics, iodine, hormones, and other factors matter. But how your body handles stress may affect how you feel day to day, and possibly how active the autoimmunity is. That's a reason to take stress seriously, alongside your thyroid care.

## What keeps the nervous system in fight or flight

Many women with Hashimoto's are the strong ones. They hold everything together, take care of everyone, and keep going when they're running on empty.

That kind of strength is often something the body learned early: if I don't hold it together, no one will. A body living by that rule stays braced and on duty, sometimes for decades. It may not feel like "stress." It just feels like life. But underneath, the nervous system is still running in protection mode.

## Signs your body may be stuck on duty

- You feel exhausted but can't fully relax
- You're the one who holds everything together
- Rest makes you feel guilty or anxious
- Your symptoms got worse during a stressful season
- You feel wired at night and drained during the day

Keep working with your doctor on your thyroid levels and medication. Stress support is something you add, not something you swap in.
