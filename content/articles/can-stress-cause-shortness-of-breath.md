Can stress cause shortness of breath? Yes, stress and anxiety can cause shortness of breath. When your nervous system is on alert, your breathing gets faster and shallower, and your chest and neck muscles tighten. You can end up breathing off more carbon dioxide than your body needs, which can make you feel like you can't get enough air, even when your oxygen is fine.

You might notice it as a need to sigh or yawn to get a "full" breath, a tight chest, or breathing that speeds up when you're worried. It can also come with lightheadedness or tingling in your hands or lips.

## Get emergency help if…

Shortness of breath can be a sign of serious problems with your heart or lungs. Call 911 if it comes on suddenly or severely, or comes with chest pain, pain spreading to your arm or jaw, fainting, blue lips, coughing up blood, or swelling in one leg. If you have asthma or a lung or heart condition, follow your doctor's action plan. Don't assume it's stress until a doctor has checked.

## What research shows

Doctors use the term "dysfunctional breathing" for breathlessness with no medical cause, or breathlessness that's stronger than any medical problem explains. A 2020 medical update notes that hyperventilation syndrome very often comes with, or comes from, anxiety. It also notes that missing this can lead to many unneeded tests and even more anxiety.

A review on panic disorder describes a strong two-way link between breathing and anxiety. Fast breathing can bring on panic symptoms, and anxiety can change how you breathe.

## How stress changes your breathing

In fight-or-flight, your body gets ready to run, so you breathe faster and higher in your chest. Over time, that can become a habit. Your diaphragm doesn't move as freely, your chest and shoulder muscles work harder, and taking a breath starts to feel like work.

When you notice it, it's natural to worry, and worry makes the breathing pattern stronger.

## Signs stress may be part of your breathlessness

- It happens at rest or when you're worried, not mainly with exercise
- You sigh or yawn a lot to get a satisfying breath
- Your heart and lung tests were normal
- It comes with a tight chest, a racing heart, or tingling
- It eases when you're distracted or relaxed

## Common questions

**How do I know if it's anxiety or something serious?** You can't always tell on your own. If it's new, severe, or comes with chest pain, get checked right away. Once your doctor rules out other causes, stress becomes more likely.

**What helps stress breathlessness?** Slow breathing with a longer exhale, breathing low into your belly, and relaxing your shoulders can help. Breathing retraining with a physical therapist helps many people.

**Can stress cause shortness of breath for days?** Yes. During long stressful periods, the breathing pattern can stay with you most of the day.
