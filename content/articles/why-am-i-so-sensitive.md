Why am I so sensitive? Some people's nervous systems are simply built to notice more. Researchers call this sensory processing sensitivity, and people high in it are often called "highly sensitive people." It's a normal trait, not a disorder. It means you take in more detail, feel emotions more deeply, and get overwhelmed more easily. On top of that, a nervous system that learned it wasn't safe can turn sensitivity up even higher.

You might cry easily, feel drained after crowds, notice every sound in a room, take criticism to heart, or pick up on other people's moods before they say a word.

## When to get support

Sensitivity itself isn't a problem. But if you feel overwhelmed most of the time, can't cope with daily life, or feel hopeless, please talk with a doctor or therapist. Sensory overload can also be part of autism, ADHD, anxiety, migraines, or PTSD, which deserve their own care. If you're in crisis, call or text 988.

## What research shows

Psychologists Elaine and Arthur Aron first described sensory processing sensitivity in a 1997 study in the *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*. They found it's a distinct trait, separate from being introverted. People high in it process information more deeply and react more strongly to their environment.

Later research suggests sensitive people are more affected by their surroundings in both directions. Hard environments affect them more, and supportive environments help them more.

## How your nervous system shapes sensitivity

Your nervous system decides how much input to let in and how strongly to react. If you grew up needing to read the room to stay safe, your body may have learned to scan constantly: tone of voice, facial expressions, small changes in mood. That watchfulness can feel like being "too sensitive," when really it's your body protecting you.

When your nervous system feels safe, sensitivity can become a strength: empathy, creativity, intuition, and noticing what others miss.

## Signs your nervous system may be turning sensitivity up

- You feel on edge in busy or loud places
- You can tell someone's upset before they speak
- Criticism stays with you for days
- You need lots of time alone to recover
- You cry easily or feel emotions intensely

## Common questions

**Is being highly sensitive a mental illness?** No. It's a personality trait found in a large share of people.

**Why am I so emotionally sensitive lately?** Stress, poor sleep, hormones, and burnout can all make you more sensitive than usual.

**Can I become less sensitive?** Your trait may not change, but you can feel far less overwhelmed when your nervous system feels safe.

## Why it can feel like too much

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the noise or the comment itself. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *I have to know how everyone feels to stay safe* or *If someone's upset, it's my job to fix it*, and your sensitivity is working overtime for that rule.
