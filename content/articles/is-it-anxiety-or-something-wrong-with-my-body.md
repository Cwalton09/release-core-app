How do I know if it's anxiety or something wrong with my body? You often can't tell on your own, because anxiety causes real physical symptoms that can feel exactly like medical problems. The safest path is to get checked by a doctor first, especially for new or severe symptoms. If your tests come back normal and your symptoms rise and fall with stress, your nervous system may be a big part of what's going on. It can also be both: anxiety and a medical condition often happen together.

This is one of the most common questions people have, and the uncertainty itself can keep the anxiety going.

## Always get emergency help for…

Call 911 for chest pain or pressure, trouble breathing, fainting, sudden weakness or numbness on one side, a drooping face, slurred speech, sudden vision loss, the worst headache of your life, or thoughts of harming yourself (call or text 988 in the US). Don't wait to figure out if it's anxiety.

## Why anxiety feels so physical

Anxiety isn't just worry. When your nervous system senses danger, it changes your heart rate, breathing, muscles, digestion, and blood flow. That can cause a racing heart, chest tightness, shortness of breath, dizziness, tingling, nausea, shaking, and more. These symptoms are real. They come from your nervous system, not your imagination. See [why anxiety feels physical](/articles/why-does-my-anxiety-feel-physical).

## Clues it may be anxiety

- Symptoms rise with stress or worry and ease when you're distracted or calm
- They come in waves or during panic, then pass
- Many different symptoms show up in different parts of your body
- Your doctor's exam and tests are normal
- You check your body often and search your symptoms a lot

## Clues something else may be going on

- Symptoms are new, getting steadily worse, or wake you from sleep
- You have weight loss, fever, blood in your stool or urine, or a lump
- Symptoms happen with exercise, such as chest pain when walking uphill
- One side of your body is affected
- You have a family history of a related condition

These clues are a guide, not a diagnosis. When in doubt, get checked.

## If your tests are normal

Normal tests are good news, but they can also feel frustrating when you still feel awful. It doesn't mean it's "all in your head." It often means your nervous system is stuck on alert. See [feel off but labs normal](/articles/feel-off-but-labs-normal).

Doctors call ongoing symptoms without a clear medical cause "medically unexplained symptoms." A 2022 review in *General Hospital Psychiatry* found that psychological treatments made these physical symptoms less severe compared to usual care.

## Common questions

**Can anxiety cause symptoms every day?** Yes. When your nervous system stays on alert, symptoms can show up daily.

**Why do my symptoms get worse when I think about them?** Paying attention to a symptom makes your brain treat it as more important, and worry adds more stress signals.

**Should I keep getting tests?** Work with one doctor you trust. Repeat testing without new symptoms can increase anxiety without giving new answers.

## Why it keeps coming back

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the symptom itself. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *My body isn't safe* or *I have to watch for what could go wrong*, and your symptoms are part of that alarm.
