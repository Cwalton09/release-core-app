If you've spent any time on wellness TikTok or Instagram, you've probably heard about the vagus nerve. Interest in nervous system regulation has exploded, and the vagus nerve is at the center of it.

So what is it, and why does it matter?

## What the vagus nerve is

The vagus nerve is one of the longest nerves in your body. It runs from your brainstem down through your neck and chest into your belly, connecting your brain to your heart, lungs, and digestive system.

It's a big part of your parasympathetic nervous system, the "rest and digest" side that helps your body calm down after stress.

## It mostly listens

Here's something many people don't know: most of the vagus nerve's fibers carry information from your body up to your brain. It's constantly reporting how your heart, lungs, and gut are doing.

That's one reason your body can feel anxious before your mind knows why. Your brain is getting signals from your body all the time.

## What the vagus nerve affects

- Your heart rate, including how it slows down after stress
- Breathing
- Digestion and how your gut moves
- How quickly you recover after being upset
- The connection between your [gut and your brain](/articles/gut-brain-connection-nervous-system)

## What "vagal tone" means

You may hear people talk about "vagal tone." It's a way of describing how well your body can shift back into a calm state after stress. Researchers study it in many areas, from anxiety to digestion to pain.

## Why hacks only go so far

There are lots of vagus nerve "hacks" online, like cold water or humming. Some can help your body calm down in the moment.

But if your nervous system still believes there's something to protect you from, it will keep pulling you back into alert, no matter how many hacks you try. The vagus nerve responds to safety, and safety comes from what your body believes, not just what you do.
