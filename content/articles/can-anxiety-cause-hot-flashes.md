Can anxiety cause hot flashes? Yes. Anxiety can cause sudden waves of heat, flushing, sweating, and then chills. Your nervous system controls body temperature through blood flow and sweating, and adrenaline can switch both on quickly. During perimenopause and menopause, research shows anxiety is strongly linked to more frequent and more severe hot flashes.

You might feel heat rush up your chest and face, start sweating, then feel cold and shivery a few minutes later.

## See a doctor if…

Hot flashes and chills can come from menopause, thyroid problems, infections, medications, low blood sugar, and other conditions. See a doctor if you have a fever, night sweats that soak your sheets, unexplained weight loss, or hot flashes with a racing heart that doesn't settle. Get emergency care for heat or sweating with chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting.

## How your nervous system controls temperature

Your brain's temperature center works closely with your stress system. In fight-or-flight, adrenaline can widen blood vessels in your skin (flushing and heat) and trigger sweating to cool you down. Once the sweat evaporates and the adrenaline fades, you can feel chilled and shaky.

That's why a panic attack can feel like a hot flash, followed by chills.

## What research shows

In a 2005 study in the journal *Menopause*, researchers followed women through the menopausal transition for six years. Women with higher anxiety had more hot flashes, and their hot flashes were more frequent and more severe, even after accounting for hormone levels, menopause stage, and smoking. Anxiety came before the hot flashes in this group, which suggests anxiety may play an active role.

## Signs anxiety may be part of it

- Heat waves come with worry, panic, or stressful moments
- You flush and sweat, then feel chilled
- Your heart races at the same time
- Hot flashes got worse during a stressful season

## Common questions

**Can anxiety make you feel hot and cold?** Yes. Adrenaline can cause flushing and sweating, then chills as your body cools down.

**Are anxiety hot flashes the same as menopause hot flashes?** They feel similar. In perimenopause, anxiety and hormone changes can make each other worse.

**How do I stop a hot flash from anxiety?** Slow your breathing, cool your face and wrists, and let the wave pass. It usually fades within minutes.
