Does stress cause inflammation? Long-term stress can keep inflammation higher than it should be. Normally, the stress hormone cortisol helps turn inflammation off. But research shows that when stress goes on for a long time, your immune cells can stop listening to cortisol. Without that brake, inflammation stays switched on.

You might feel achy, stiff, puffy, or tired all the time, notice more flare-ups of a chronic condition during hard seasons, or be told your inflammation markers are high.

## See a doctor if…

Inflammation has many causes, including infections, autoimmune conditions, and other illnesses. Please see a doctor for joint swelling, ongoing pain, fevers, unexplained weight loss, rashes, or fatigue that won't go away. Keep taking any medications you've been prescribed.

## What research shows

A 2012 study in the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* tested this idea in healthy volunteers. People who had recently gone through a long-term, threatening stressful experience showed glucocorticoid receptor resistance, which means their immune cells were less responsive to cortisol. Those people were more likely to develop a cold after being exposed to a cold virus. In a second study, greater resistance predicted more inflammatory signals in people who got infected.

The authors suggest that because inflammation plays a role in so many diseases, this may help explain how stress affects health so broadly.

## How stress keeps inflammation going

- **Cortisol stops working as a brake.** Your body makes it, but your cells stop responding.
- **Your alarm system stays on.** Stress keeps sending signals that prepare the body for injury.
- **Poor sleep adds fuel.** Short sleep raises inflammatory signals.
- **The loop.** Inflammation can make you feel worse, which adds stress.

## What helps lower inflammation

- Regular sleep
- Moving your body
- Whole foods, plenty of plants
- Time with people who feel safe
- Calming your nervous system

## Common questions

**Can stress raise CRP?** Stress is linked to higher inflammation markers in many studies, but CRP has many causes. Talk to your doctor about your results.

**Can anxiety cause inflammation in the body?** Ongoing anxiety keeps your stress system active, which can keep inflammation higher.

**How long does it take to lower inflammation?** It depends on the cause. Lifestyle changes often help over weeks to months.
