Why can't you talk yourself out of anxiety? Because anxiety doesn't start in the part of your brain that uses words and logic. It starts in your nervous system, which reacts automatically to what it has learned is dangerous. You can't argue with it, because it isn't listening to arguments. And often, the thing you're trying to talk yourself out of isn't even the real cause.

If you've ever said "I know this is irrational" while your heart pounds anyway, this is why.

## Your nervous system reacts first

Your nervous system scans for danger constantly and automatically. Researchers call this [neuroception](/articles/what-is-neuroception). When it detects something it learned to fear, it sets off the stress response: heart rate up, muscles tight, breath short, mind racing.

That happens fast, and it happens before conscious thought. By the time you start reasoning with yourself, your body is already mid-reaction.

## Logic and fear live in different places

Your thinking brain is good at reasons, plans, and facts. But it doesn't run your alarm system. Under stress, the parts of your brain involved in careful reasoning tend to have less say, and the faster survival systems take over.

So you're trying to calm a smoke alarm by explaining to it that there's no fire. It can't hear you. It was never built to take that kind of input.

## Why the pep talk sometimes makes it worse

Telling yourself "stop it, this is ridiculous" can add a second layer: now you're anxious *and* frustrated at yourself for being anxious. Your nervous system can read that inner pressure as another threat.

And repeating reassurances over and over can become its own loop, like [rehashing a problem](/articles/does-talking-about-your-problems-keep-you-stuck) without anything changing.

## You're probably arguing with the wrong thing

Here's the part almost nobody talks about. When you try to talk yourself out of anxiety, you're arguing with the reason your mind *thinks* you're anxious.

"I'm nervous about the presentation." So you tell yourself it'll go fine.

But your body might not be scared of the presentation at all. It might be running a rule like *if people see me, they'll find something wrong with me*, or *if I'm not perfect, I'll be in trouble*. That rule was learned long before the presentation, and your mind may not know it's there.

In Release Core sessions, this happens again and again. Someone comes in sure they know what's wrong. Then their body answers differently. What looked like fear of a test result turns out to be *I can't rest until I know for certain*. What looked like stress about one person turns out to be a pattern that started years before they met.

You can't talk yourself out of a pattern you can't see.

## Common questions

**Why does my anxiety ignore logic?** Because anxiety is driven by your nervous system's learned threat responses, which happen automatically and outside conscious reasoning. Logic doesn't change what your nervous system learned.

**Does positive self-talk help anxiety?** Kind self-talk is better than harsh self-talk. But on its own, it usually can't override a deep, learned pattern, for the same reason [affirmations often don't work](/articles/why-affirmations-dont-work).

**Why do I feel anxious for no reason?** There's almost always a reason. It's just not one your conscious mind can see. See [why do I feel anxious for no reason?](/articles/why-do-i-feel-anxious-for-no-reason)

## Why it keeps coming back

The anxiety keeps returning because the rule underneath it hasn't changed. Most of the time, we don't know which rule is running. We guess, and our guess is usually a story about the present, not the rule our body learned in the past.
