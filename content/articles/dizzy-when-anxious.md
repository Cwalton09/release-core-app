The room isn't spinning, exactly. But you feel off. Unsteady. Floaty. Like the floor isn't quite solid. It gets worse in busy stores, scrolling on your phone, or standing in a crowd.

Dizziness is one of the most unsettling symptoms of anxiety, and one of the most searched.

## What the research found

In 2017, an international committee of the Bárány Society, the leading organization for balance disorders, published official criteria for a condition called **persistent postural-perceptual dizziness**, or PPPD, in the Journal of Vestibular Research.

Here's what they described:

- Dizziness, unsteadiness, or non-spinning vertigo on most days for at least three months
- Symptoms that get worse when standing upright, moving, or looking at busy visual scenes, like screens, patterns, or crowds
- It's often set off by something that first disrupted balance, like an inner ear problem, an illness, a panic attack, or a period of intense stress

PPPD is described as a functional disorder. That means the balance system is structurally okay, but the way the brain and nervous system process balance has shifted. It's real, it's common, and it's not "all in your head."

## Why anxiety and dizziness feed each other

When your body senses danger, it goes on high alert, including your balance system. Your brain starts monitoring every small movement more closely, your muscles stiffen to brace, and you may breathe faster than you need to. All of that can make you feel lightheaded and unsteady.

Then the dizziness itself feels scary, which makes your body brace even more. The loop keeps going, even after the original trigger is gone.

## What keeps the nervous system in fight or flight

A body that has learned it has to stay on guard doesn't relax its balance system either. It keeps watching, keeps bracing, keeps preparing to catch you before you fall.

Often what keeps the nervous system in that state isn't the dizziness itself. It's something the body learned a long time ago about not being safe, and the dizziness is one of the ways that protection shows up.

## Signs your dizziness may be tied to an on-alert nervous system

- You feel unsteady most days, but tests keep coming back normal
- It gets worse in busy places, scrolling, or watching movement
- It started after an illness, a panic attack, or a stressful season
- You hold your body stiff or tense to feel steady
- You feel on edge or braced most of the time

Dizziness can have many medical causes, including inner ear, heart, and neurological problems. See a doctor to get checked, and get emergency care for sudden severe dizziness, fainting, slurred speech, or weakness.
