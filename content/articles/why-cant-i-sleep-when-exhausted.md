You've been dragging all day. You can barely keep your eyes open on the couch. Then you get into bed, and suddenly you're wide awake.

Your mind starts racing. Your body feels restless or wired. And the harder you try to sleep, the more awake you feel.

## Sleep needs a nervous system that feels safe

Falling asleep isn't something you can force. It happens when your nervous system feels safe enough to let its guard down.

If part of your body is still on alert, scanning, planning, or bracing, it won't fully let go. From a survival point of view, that makes sense. Your body doesn't want to fall asleep if it thinks something might need its attention.

## What researchers call hyperarousal

Sleep researchers have a name for this: hyperarousal. A well-known 2010 review by Dieter Riemann and colleagues in Sleep Medicine Reviews describes insomnia as linked to higher arousal, both at night and during the day, across many measures in the body and brain.

Researchers are still debating exactly how it works. A later 2017 review by Kay and Buysse found the physical evidence is mixed and suggested insomnia may involve several systems being out of balance rather than one simple "switched on" state. But mental and emotional arousal, like worry and racing thoughts, have strong support as part of the picture.

## Signs your sleep trouble is nervous system related

- You're exhausted but feel wired at bedtime
- Your mind races as soon as your head hits the pillow
- You wake up at 2 or 3 AM and can't get back to sleep
- Sleep is worse during stressful seasons
- You sleep better on vacation or away from home
- You start dreading bedtime because you expect to struggle

## When bed becomes the trigger

Over time, your body can start linking your bed with being awake and frustrated. Then just getting into bed can switch your nervous system on. That's one reason insomnia can keep going even after the original stress is gone.

Talk to your doctor about ongoing sleep problems, since conditions like sleep apnea can also cause poor sleep. You can also read about [calming your nervous system before bed](/articles/how-to-calm-your-nervous-system-before-bed).
