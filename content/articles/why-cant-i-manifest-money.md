You're doing everything you're supposed to. Working hard. Saying the money affirmations. Picturing the number in your bank account.

But money keeps slipping through your fingers. An unexpected bill shows up every time you get ahead. You undercharge, avoid looking at your accounts, or feel strangely uneasy when you finally have extra.

That may not be a mindset problem. It may be your nervous system.

## My story with money

When I was younger, I watched my parents lose everything when the economy crashed in 2008. They worked hard, and it still wasn't enough to keep what they had built.

My nervous system took a lesson from that: you can work hard for your money and still lose it.

I didn't know I was carrying that. I just knew that no matter what I did, something held me back with money. It wasn't until I did a session on it that I found where it started. Ever since clearing that, manifesting has become so easy for me.

## How money blocks form

Money blocks often form in childhood, from what we see and feel around money long before we can understand it. Your body may have learned money means:

- Stress and fighting
- Fear and scarcity
- Losing everything, no matter how hard you work
- Being judged or resented
- Never having enough
- Something only other people get

Research supports a connection here. A 2022 study in the Journal of Financial Therapy found that adverse childhood experiences were linked to insecure attachment in adulthood, and insecure attachment was linked to unhealthy money beliefs and behaviors. Small studies of young people in families facing serious financial hardship have described lasting effects on their emotions and beliefs about money.

## Why money can feel unsafe

If your body connected money with fear or loss, then having more money can actually feel threatening. More to lose. More to protect. More attention, more responsibility, more risk.

So your nervous system may quietly keep things at a level that feels familiar, even if familiar means struggling.

## Signs your nervous system is involved

- You feel anxious checking your bank account
- Money comes in and goes right back out
- You feel guilty spending on yourself
- You undercharge or avoid asking for more
- You feel uneasy when things are financially good
- You expect the other shoe to drop
