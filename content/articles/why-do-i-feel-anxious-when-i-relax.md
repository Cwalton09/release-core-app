Why do I feel anxious when I try to relax? It's a real, studied experience called relaxation-induced anxiety. For some people, slowing down feels unsafe to the nervous system. If your body learned that letting your guard down leads to something bad, or that staying busy keeps hard feelings away, then rest can set off an alarm instead of calm.

You might feel it when you sit down at the end of the day, on vacation, during a massage, or when you try breathing exercises or meditation. Instead of melting, your heart speeds up, your mind races, or you feel an urgent need to get up and do something.

## What research shows

Researchers have studied relaxation-induced anxiety for decades. A 2019 study in the *Journal of Affective Disorders* looked at people with anxiety, people with depression, and people with neither. Those with anxiety and depression had more anxiety during relaxation exercises.

The study tested an idea called the Contrast Avoidance Model. It suggests some people stay worried because it protects them from a sudden drop into a bad feeling. Staying tense feels safer than relaxing and then getting hit by bad news. The more sensitive people were to those sudden shifts, the more anxiety they felt during relaxation.

## Why your nervous system resists rest

- **Rest once meant danger.** If bad things happened when you weren't watching, your body learned to stay alert.
- **Busy keeps feelings away.** When you stop, feelings you've been outrunning can catch up.
- **Your worth got tied to doing.** If you learned you're valuable only when you're useful, rest can feel like failing.
- **You can finally feel your body.** When you stop moving, you notice tension, a racing heart, or tiredness you were tuning out.

## What helps

- **Go slow.** Start with a few minutes of rest instead of an hour.
- **Rest with movement.** A slow walk, rocking, or gentle stretching can feel safer than sitting still.
- **Keep your eyes open.** Looking around the room can tell your nervous system you're safe.
- **Skip the forced deep breathing.** If breathing exercises make you more anxious, breathe normally and let your exhale be a little longer.
- **Notice, don't fight.** Let the anxiety be there without forcing yourself to calm down.

## Common questions

**Why do I get anxious on vacation or weekends?** When the busyness stops, your nervous system has room to feel what it's been holding, and it may treat the slowdown as unsafe.

**Why does meditation make my anxiety worse?** Focusing inward can make you notice uncomfortable sensations and thoughts. Shorter practices with eyes open, or movement-based practices, often feel better.

**Is relaxation-induced anxiety dangerous?** It's uncomfortable but not dangerous. If it comes with chest pain or fainting, see a doctor.

## Why it keeps coming back

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the bath or the couch. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *If I relax, something bad will happen* or *I only matter when I'm doing something*, and rest sets that rule off.
