Why does my body ache all the time? Ongoing body aches can come from many things, including poor sleep, illness, low vitamin D, thyroid problems, and conditions like fibromyalgia. Stress is a common piece too. A nervous system on alert keeps muscles tense, can make your body more sensitive to pain, and can raise inflammation. Researchers call stress turning up pain "stress-induced hyperalgesia."

You might wake up sore, ache like you have the flu without a fever, or feel tender in places you didn't hurt.

## See a doctor if…

Body aches that last should be checked. Please see a doctor for aches with fever, weight loss, joint swelling, rash, weakness, or numbness, or if they started after a new medication. Ongoing aches can come from autoimmune conditions, infections, thyroid problems, low vitamin D, and other causes that need treatment.

## How stress turns up pain

Your nervous system decides how loud pain signals are. When it senses danger, it can turn up the volume, so normal sensations hurt more. At the same time, stress keeps your muscles braced, especially in the neck, shoulders, jaw, and back, which leads to soreness.

Long-term stress can also affect sleep and inflammation, and both make aches worse.

## What research shows

A 2014 review in *Progress in Neurobiology* describes stress-induced hyperalgesia: studies in people and animals show that long-term or repeated stress can make the body more sensitive to pain. That's different from the short-term pain relief you can get in a sudden emergency. It's a slow turning-up of pain over weeks and months.

Research on inflammation also shows that immune signals can cause achiness and fatigue, the same feeling you get with the flu.

## Signs stress may be part of your aches

- They got worse during a hard season
- You ache more after stressful days or poor sleep
- The pain moves around instead of staying in one place
- Tests haven't fully explained it
- You also have tension, a tight jaw, or trouble relaxing

## Common questions

**Can stress cause body aches without a fever?** Yes. Muscle tension, poor sleep, and a more sensitive nervous system can all cause aches without illness.

**Why do I ache when I wake up?** Poor sleep, clenching your jaw or tensing at night, and inflammation can make mornings achy.

**Can anxiety make your whole body hurt?** It can. Anxiety keeps muscles tense and makes pain signals louder.
