First, the most important part: palpitations can sometimes be a sign of a heart rhythm problem. Get emergency care right away if they come with chest pain, trouble breathing, fainting, dizziness, or confusion. Talk to your doctor about any palpitations that are frequent or don't go away within a few minutes.

Once your doctor has checked your heart, many people find their palpitations are connected to their nervous system.

## What palpitations feel like

Palpitations can feel like your heart is racing, pounding, fluttering, skipping a beat, or beating too hard. You might feel it in your chest, throat, or neck.

## How your nervous system causes them

When your nervous system senses danger, it activates fight or flight. Adrenaline is released, and your heart beats faster and harder to get your body ready to act. Cleveland Clinic describes anxiety as one of the common triggers for palpitations for exactly this reason.

Palpitations from anxiety tend to start suddenly and end fairly quickly.

## Other common triggers

- Caffeine, alcohol, or nicotine
- Poor sleep
- Dehydration
- Some medications
- Hormone changes
- Intense exercise

## The loop that keeps it going

Noticing your heartbeat can make you more anxious. And more anxiety means more adrenaline, which makes your heart pound harder. Before long, you're stuck in a loop: you check your heart, it feels off, you worry, and it races again.

For many people, the fear of their heartbeat becomes just as hard as the palpitations themselves. You can also read about [why your chest feels tight when you're anxious](/articles/why-does-my-chest-feel-tight-anxiety).
