Why do I have muscle twitching all over my body every day? For most people, everyday twitching all over the body is benign, meaning harmless. Stress, anxiety, caffeine, poor sleep, and hard exercise can all make muscles twitch. When twitching shows up all over and keeps going, doctors sometimes call it benign fasciculation syndrome. Anxiety and twitching often feed each other: twitching makes you worry, and the worry makes you twitch more.

You might feel it in your calves, thighs, arms, eyelids, feet, or back. It can feel like popcorn under your skin or a tiny flicker that won't stop, and it's often worse when you finally sit still or lie down.

## See a doctor if…

Please see a doctor about twitching that lasts, especially if it comes with muscle weakness, trouble gripping, tripping, muscles getting smaller, trouble swallowing or speaking, or numbness. Twitching *with* weakness is different from twitching alone, and it should be checked.

## What research shows

In a 2018 study in *Muscle & Nerve*, 35 people with benign fasciculation syndrome were followed for two years. None of them went on to develop motor neuron disease (ALS). Many of them said they felt anxious, though few had an anxiety disorder.

A 2019 review of eight studies found that people with benign twitching often had strong health anxiety, usually a fear of ALS. Talk therapy and, in some cases, medication helped with that fear.

## How stress makes muscles twitch

When your nervous system is on alert, it sends more signals to your muscles and keeps them ready to move. Stress hormones, poor sleep, caffeine, and low magnesium or potassium can make nerves fire more easily. The result can be small, random twitches anywhere in the body.

Many people notice the twitching started during a stressful season, or after they read something scary online and began checking their body constantly.

## Signs stress may be part of your twitching

- It started during a hard time or after a health scare
- It's worse when you're tired, anxious, or have had caffeine
- It moves around the body instead of staying in one spot
- You don't have weakness, and your doctor's exam was normal
- You check your muscles and search symptoms often

## Common questions

**How long does anxiety twitching last?** It can come and go for weeks or months. It often eases as your stress and worry about the twitching ease.

**Is twitching all over a sign of ALS?** Twitching alone, without weakness, is very rarely a sign of ALS. Your doctor can check your strength and reflexes and tell you whether more tests are needed.

**Why is twitching worse at night?** When you lie still, you notice small sensations more. Many people also feel their stress more when the day goes quiet.

## Why it keeps coming back

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the twitch itself. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *I have to watch for danger* or *Something bad will happen if I relax*, and the twitching is part of a body that can't stand down.
