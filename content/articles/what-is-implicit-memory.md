What is implicit memory? Implicit memory is memory that shapes your behavior and reactions without you consciously remembering it. It's how you ride a bike without thinking, and it's also how your body can react to a situation based on something you don't remember learning. You don't recall it. You just feel it.

This is one of the main reasons you can have a strong reaction and honestly have no idea where it came from.

## Two kinds of memory

Researchers often divide long-term memory into two broad types.

- **Explicit memory** is what you can consciously recall. Your address. Your tenth birthday. What you had for lunch.
- **Implicit memory** works without conscious recall. Skills, habits, conditioned reactions, and emotional responses all live here.

Explicit memory says, "I remember that." Implicit memory just acts. It doesn't announce itself.

## How implicit memory shows up

You don't experience implicit memory as a memory. You experience it as a feeling, a reaction, or a sense that something is off.

- Your chest tightens when someone sighs, but you don't know why
- You feel guilty resting, even with nothing left to do
- You shrink when someone is disappointed in you
- You feel uneasy in a certain kind of house or room
- Your body braces when things are going well

Each of those can be an old lesson playing out, without the original memory attached.

## Why so much of it starts early

Very young children don't form lasting explicit memories the way adults do. Most people remember little or nothing from their first few years. But their nervous systems are learning constantly in those years: whether people come when they cry, whether it's safe to need things, what happens when someone gets upset.

Those lessons can be stored implicitly. So you can carry a rule from age two, or age six, with no memory of learning it. You just know what it feels like.

## Why you can't find it by thinking

When you try to explain a reaction, your mind goes looking through explicit memory, the stuff you can recall. If the pattern was stored implicitly, it isn't there to find.

So your mind fills the gap with a reasonable-sounding story. That story might even be partly true. But it often isn't the rule your body is actually running. More on that in [I know why I do it, so why can't I stop?](/articles/i-know-why-i-do-it-but-cant-stop)

## Common questions

**Is implicit memory the same as the subconscious?** They overlap. Implicit memory is a research term for memory that works without conscious recall. People often use "subconscious" to describe the same kind of hidden influence. See [how to change subconscious beliefs](/articles/how-to-change-subconscious-beliefs).

**Is trauma stored as implicit memory?** Many researchers believe parts of traumatic experience, like body sensations and emotional reactions, can be held implicitly, which is why reactions can show up without a clear memory. Read more in [is trauma stored in the body?](/articles/is-trauma-stored-in-the-body)

**Can implicit memories change?** Research on memory reconsolidation suggests that when a memory is activated, there's a window where it can be updated. That's covered in [why old situations still run your life](/articles/why-old-situations-still-run-your-life).
