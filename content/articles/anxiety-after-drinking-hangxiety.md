Why do you get anxiety after drinking? It's often called "hangxiety." Alcohol calms the nervous system at first, then as it wears off, your brain swings the other way and becomes more alert and on edge. Research also found that shy and socially anxious people feel this rebound the most: drinking eased their anxiety that night, but the next morning their anxiety rose significantly.

You wake up the morning after, and the dread hits before you're even out of bed. Your heart is racing. You're replaying every conversation, sure you said something wrong.

## What research shows

In a 2019 study from University College London and the University of Exeter, researchers followed about 100 social drinkers. Some drank as usual, others stayed sober. For people who were very shy, drinking slightly lowered their anxiety that night. But the next morning, their anxiety went up significantly. Those with stronger hangxiety also scored higher on a screening test for alcohol use problems.

## What's happening in your body

Alcohol boosts the brain's calming signals and dampens its alerting ones. When it wears off, your nervous system rebounds, swinging toward alertness and stress. Add poor sleep, dehydration, and low blood sugar, and your body feels like it's under threat. Your mind then looks for a reason, and it usually finds one: *what did I say last night?*

## Why shy and anxious people feel it most

If your nervous system is already on guard around people, alcohol can feel like relief. It quiets the scanning. But when it wears off, the scanning comes back stronger. That's part of why some people come to rely on drinking to feel okay in social settings. ## When to get support

If you drink to cope with anxiety, or hangxiety keeps happening, it may be worth talking with your doctor. The SAMHSA National Helpline (1-800-662-4357) is free and confidential, 24/7.

## Common questions

**How long does hangxiety last?** It often eases within a day, as your body rebalances and you sleep.

**Why do I feel guilty after drinking?** The anxious rebound makes your mind search for something you did wrong, even when you didn't.

**Why do I feel more anxious the day after drinking than before?** Your nervous system rebounds as alcohol leaves your system, swinging toward alertness.

## Why it keeps coming back

If the morning-after dread keeps showing up, your nervous system is still on guard underneath, and alcohol is only quieting it for a while.

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the drinks or what you said. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *I'm not safe being fully myself around people* or *I'll be judged if I'm not careful*, and the morning after is just turning up its volume.
