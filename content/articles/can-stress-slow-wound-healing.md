Can stress slow wound healing? Yes. Some of the clearest research on stress and the body comes from wound healing. In one study, small wounds took about 40% longer to heal in students during exam time than during a calm summer break. In another, caregivers under long-term stress took about nine days longer to heal than people who weren't caregiving. When your nervous system stays on alert, your body puts less energy into repair.

You might notice cuts, scrapes, or blisters taking longer to heal during hard seasons, slower recovery after procedures, or mouth sores that hang on when you're stressed.

## See a doctor if…

Please see a doctor if a wound is red, hot, swollen, draining pus, or getting worse, if you have a fever, or if a wound hasn't healed in two weeks. Slow healing can also be a sign of diabetes, poor circulation, or other conditions that need care. If you're recovering from surgery, follow your care team's instructions.

## What research shows

A study in *The Lancet* gave a tiny skin wound to women caring for a family member with dementia and to women who weren't caregiving. The caregivers took an average of about 49 days to heal, compared with about 39 days for the others. Their immune cells also made less of a key healing signal.

A study in *Psychosomatic Medicine* gave dental students a small wound in the mouth twice, once during summer vacation and once before major exams. During exams, the wounds took about three days longer to heal, which was 40% longer, and a healing signal dropped by 68%.

## Why stress slows healing

Healing depends on a careful sequence of inflammation and repair. Stress hormones like cortisol change the immune signals that guide that process. Poor sleep and running on empty slow it further. Your body is in protection mode, and repair waits. See [does stress weaken your immune system](/articles/does-stress-weaken-your-immune-system).

## What supports healing

- Sleep, even short naps
- Protein and nourishing food
- Gentle movement if your doctor allows it
- Rest instead of pushing through
- Calming your nervous system

## Common questions

**Can stress slow healing after surgery?** Research suggests it can. Talk with your care team about ways to rest and recover.

**Why do my cuts take so long to heal?** Stress, poor sleep, diabetes, and circulation problems can all slow healing. Get it checked if it keeps happening.

**Does calming down help you heal faster?** Lower stress supports the conditions healing needs, like sleep and a balanced immune response.
