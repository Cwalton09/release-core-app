How is fibromyalgia connected to the nervous system? Fibromyalgia is a condition of widespread pain, fatigue, sleep problems, and brain fog. Many researchers believe it involves central sensitization, where the brain and spinal cord turn pain signals up, so the body hurts more than the tissue damage would explain. Stress and trauma are commonly reported before it starts, and they may influence how the nervous system handles pain in people who are already susceptible.

It can be exhausting just to explain. Everything hurts, your labs look normal, and people don't always believe you.

## Fibromyalgia is real and deserves care

Fibromyalgia is a real medical condition, diagnosed by a doctor. It affects an estimated 2% to 3.5% of people and is about five times more common in women than men. Please work with your doctor or a rheumatologist. Treatment can include medication, movement, sleep support, and therapy.

## The volume knob on pain

Pain isn't just a signal from your body. It's something your brain and spinal cord process and decide how loud to make. In fibromyalgia, researchers have found signs that the system that usually turns pain down works less well. Brain imaging studies have shown changes in the pain-control pathways.

That's what central sensitization means: the nervous system's volume knob on pain is stuck high. Pressure, touch, and normal aches can feel much more painful. You can read more in [how the nervous system shapes pain](/articles/how-the-nervous-system-shapes-pain).

## Stress, trauma, and fibromyalgia

In a large survey, people with fibromyalgia most often named chronic stress (42%) and emotional trauma (31%) as what set it off. A 2025 study found women with fibromyalgia reported more trauma and higher rates of PTSD than comparison groups.

Researchers caution that stress and trauma probably don't cause fibromyalgia on their own. But in people who are already susceptible, long-term stress and early trauma may shape how the brain handles pain. That's the nervous system piece.

## Why fibromyalgia brings more than pain

A nervous system stuck on high alert affects much more than pain:

- Unrefreshing sleep
- Exhaustion (see [why am I always tired?](/articles/why-am-i-always-tired-nervous-system))
- Brain fog, sometimes called "fibro fog"
- Sensitivity to noise, light, and temperature
- Anxiety or low mood

## Common questions

**Is fibromyalgia a nervous system disorder?** Many researchers consider it a disorder of pain processing in the nervous system, though its exact cause is still being studied.

**Can stress cause a fibromyalgia flare?** Many people notice their symptoms flare during stressful times.

**Is fibromyalgia in your head?** No. The pain is real. The nervous system is part of the body, and how it processes pain is physical.
