A tight band around your head. Pressure behind your eyes. A throbbing migraine that wipes out your whole day.

If your headaches seem to show up during stressful times, there's a reason research keeps pointing to stress.

## Stress is the top reported trigger

A 2018 meta-analysis in the journal Cephalalgia looked at 85 studies on headache triggers. Stress was the trigger people reported most often, followed by sleep problems.

Surveys of people with both migraines and tension-type headaches have found the same thing: stressful life events were the most commonly reported trigger across headache types.

## How stress affects headaches

Researchers are still working out exactly how stress triggers headaches. But some of the pieces include:

- Muscle tension in your jaw, neck, shoulders, and scalp
- Stress hormones and changes in pain sensitivity
- A more sensitive, reactive nervous system
- Poor sleep that often comes with stress

Some research suggests stress may make the brain more sensitive over time, which can make headaches easier to trigger.

## The let-down headache

Some people notice their migraines don't hit during the stress, but right after. The weekend after a hard week. The first day of vacation. That pattern is so common it has its own name: the let-down headache. You can read more about [why you crash after stress](/articles/why-do-i-crash-after-stress).

## When to see a doctor

Get emergency care for a sudden, severe "worst headache of your life," or a headache with confusion, weakness, trouble speaking, vision loss, or a stiff neck and fever. Talk to your doctor about frequent or changing headaches. You can also read about [jaw clenching](/articles/why-do-i-clench-my-jaw), which often comes with tension headaches.
