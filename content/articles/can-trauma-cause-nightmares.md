Can trauma cause nightmares? Yes. Nightmares are one of the most common effects of trauma, and repeated, distressing dreams are one of the core symptoms of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). After something frightening, your nervous system can keep processing it at night, sometimes replaying the event, sometimes showing the fear in other forms. Many people wake up with a racing heart, sweating, and feeling like it just happened.

You might dread going to sleep, wake up several times a night, or have dreams about being chased, trapped, or unable to help someone.

## Get support

Trauma nightmares are treatable. Please talk with a doctor or trauma therapist if nightmares are frequent, disrupt your sleep, or come with flashbacks, avoidance, or feeling on edge. Treatments like imagery rehearsal therapy, trauma-focused therapy, and some medications help many people. If you're in crisis, call or text 988 in the US.

## What research shows

A 2023 review of PTSD in *American Family Physician* lists distressing dreams about the trauma among the main symptoms doctors look for, along with intrusive memories, avoidance, and feeling constantly on alert. Sleep problems and nightmares are among the most common reasons people with PTSD seek help.

## Why trauma shows up in dreams

During sleep, especially REM sleep, your brain processes emotional memories. After trauma, your nervous system may stay so alert that the fear breaks through into dreams. Instead of softening the memory, the dream can replay the alarm. ## Signs your nightmares are tied to trauma

- They started after a frightening or painful event
- They replay the event, or carry the same feeling
- You wake up with your heart racing and sweating
- You feel on edge during the day too
- You avoid sleep because of the dreams

## Common questions

**Can old trauma cause nightmares years later?** Yes. Nightmares can start or return years later, often during stressful times or after reminders.

**Do trauma nightmares go away?** With treatment and a calmer nervous system, they often become less frequent and less intense.

**Why do I dream about the same thing over and over?** Repeated dreams often mean your nervous system is still working on the same fear.

## Why it keeps coming back

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't only the event itself. It's the rule your body learned during it, like *I'm not safe* or *I can't stop bad things from happening*, and your nervous system keeps running that rule every night.
