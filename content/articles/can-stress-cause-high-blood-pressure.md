Can stress cause high blood pressure? Stress raises your blood pressure in the moment, every time. Your nervous system's fight-or-flight response speeds up your heart and narrows your blood vessels. And long-term stress is linked to a higher risk of developing high blood pressure over time. Stress isn't the only cause, but a nervous system that never stands down keeps your blood pressure working harder.

Maybe your reading is always higher at the doctor's office. Or it started creeping up during a stressful year.

## High blood pressure needs medical care

High blood pressure often has no symptoms, and it raises the risk of heart attack and stroke. Please keep up with your doctor, take any medication as prescribed, and never stop it without talking to them. Everything below is meant to work alongside your care, not replace it.

## What stress does to your blood pressure

When your nervous system senses a threat, it releases adrenaline and noradrenaline. Your heart beats faster and harder, and your blood vessels tighten. Blood pressure rises so your body can fight or run.

That's helpful in a real emergency. The problem is when the stress response stays switched on, day after day.

## What happens with long-term stress

Researchers think ongoing stress can:

- Keep stress hormones like cortisol elevated
- Make blood vessels more reactive and less flexible over time
- Increase inflammation
- Affect sleep, eating, drinking, and exercise, which all affect blood pressure

In the Jackson Heart Study, which followed more than 1,800 adults, people who reported high stress were 22% more likely to develop high blood pressure over about seven years than people with low stress. In another study, higher levels of stress hormones in urine predicted who later developed high blood pressure.

These studies show a strong link, though researchers are still working out exactly how much stress causes high blood pressure on its own.

## Signs stress may be part of your picture

- Your readings are higher on stressful days or at the doctor's office
- Your blood pressure rose during a hard season of life
- You often feel tense, wired, or on edge
- You have other stress signs like poor sleep, jaw clenching, or a racing heart

## Common questions

**Can anxiety cause high blood pressure?** Anxiety can raise blood pressure in the moment. Long-term anxiety and stress are linked to a higher risk of developing high blood pressure.

**What is white coat hypertension?** It's when your blood pressure is higher at the doctor's office than at home, often because of stress about the visit. Your doctor may suggest home readings.

**Can lowering stress lower blood pressure?** It can help as part of an overall plan with your doctor.
