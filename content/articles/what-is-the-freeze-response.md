What is the freeze response? Freezing is one of your body's built-in reactions to threat, along with fight and flight. Instead of running or fighting, your body goes still and watchful. Research shows humans freeze too: when people simply looked at angry faces, their bodies swayed less and their heart rate slowed. Freezing isn't weakness or laziness. It's your nervous system pausing to assess danger.

You might notice your mind going blank, feeling stuck or unable to start things, going quiet in conflict, or feeling heavy and unable to move when you're overwhelmed.

## When to get help

If you often lose time, feel detached from your body, or freeze in ways that affect your safety, please talk to a doctor or trauma-informed therapist. If you're in crisis, call or text 988.

## What research shows

A study in *Psychological Science* had people stand on a balance platform while they looked at angry, neutral, or happy faces. Angry faces made people sway less and slowed their heart rate. That's a freeze-like response, similar to what animals show. The more anxious people felt, the more they froze.

A review by neuroscientist Karin Roelofs explains that freezing isn't passive. It's a "brake" on movement that helps the body watch and prepare to act. Problems come when someone gets stuck in it instead of shifting back out.

## Signs of the freeze response

- Your mind goes blank when you're put on the spot
- You go quiet or still during conflict
- You feel stuck, heavy, or unable to start tasks
- You procrastinate even on things you care about
- You feel numb or far away when overwhelmed

## Common questions

**Is freezing a trauma response?** It's a normal survival response everyone has. It can become a habit after experiences where fighting or running wasn't possible.

**Why do I freeze instead of fighting back?** When escape or fighting seems impossible, freezing can be the body's safest option. It isn't a choice or a failure.

**Can you stop the freeze response?** You can help your body shift out of it, and you can change what your body treats as danger.
