Why am I so tired after crying? Crying takes energy. Your breathing, heart rate, and muscles all work hard while you cry. Research also suggests crying may help soothe you by switching on your body's "rest and digest" side afterward. So the heavy, sleepy feeling after a good cry is often your nervous system shifting from stress into rest.

You might feel wiped out, sleepy, puffy, or strangely calm after crying, sometimes with a headache.

## When to get help

If you cry most days, feel hopeless, or lose interest in things you used to enjoy, please talk to a doctor or therapist. These can be signs of depression, which is treatable. If you're having thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988.

## What research shows

A review in *Frontiers in Psychology* looked at what science knows about crying as "self-soothing." The researchers found growing evidence that crying can calm us down directly, not only by bringing comfort from other people. One of the proposed ways is through the parasympathetic nervous system, the calming branch that slows your heart and helps you rest.

They also note that crying doesn't always improve mood right away. How you feel afterward depends on what made you cry and whether you felt supported.

## Why crying makes you tired

- **Your body works hard.** Sobbing changes your breathing and tightens muscles.
- **The shift to calm.** After stress, your body may switch into rest mode, which feels sleepy.
- **Emotional release.** Letting out what you held in can leave you drained.
- **Headache and puffiness.** Tension and swelling can add to the tired feeling.

## Common questions

**Is it normal to feel sleepy after crying?** Yes. It's very common and often a sign your body is calming down.

**Why do I get a headache after crying?** Muscle tension, swollen sinuses, and dehydration can all play a part.

**Is crying good for you?** Research suggests it can help you self-soothe, especially when you feel supported.
