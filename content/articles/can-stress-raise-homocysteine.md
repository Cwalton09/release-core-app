Can stress raise homocysteine? Research suggests it can, at least in the short term. In one study, women's homocysteine levels rose during a stressful task and returned to normal afterward. In another, homocysteine rose after stress in middle-aged and older men, though not in younger men. That matters if you have MTHFR, because homocysteine is the marker people watch most.

If you're in the MTHFR world, you've probably heard a lot about homocysteine. But stress rarely comes up, even though it may be part of the picture.

## What homocysteine is

Homocysteine is an amino acid your body makes as part of normal metabolism. Your body uses folate, vitamin B12, and vitamin B6 to break it down.

When homocysteine stays high over time, it's linked to a higher risk of heart and blood vessel problems. That's why doctors sometimes check it, and why it gets so much attention in MTHFR groups. MTHFR variants, especially two copies of C677T, can nudge homocysteine up, particularly when folate is low.

## What the stress studies found

**Women and acute stress.** In a 1999 study published in *Life Sciences*, researcher Catherine Stoney measured homocysteine in women before, during, and after a psychologically stressful task. Homocysteine rose significantly during the stress and returned to baseline during recovery. The researchers suggested the rise may be linked to the body's fight-or-flight response.

**Men and age.** In a later study from the University of Bern, researchers compared younger men with middle-aged and older men. After a stressful task, homocysteine rose in the older group but not in the younger group. They concluded that stress affected homocysteine in combination with age.

These were short-term lab studies with small groups. They don't prove that ongoing stress causes high homocysteine. But they show that your nervous system's stress response can move the same marker people often blame entirely on a gene.

## Why this matters if you have MTHFR

If your homocysteine is a little high, it's easy to assume the gene is the reason. Sometimes it's part of it. But a nervous system that's always on guard is also working against you:

- The stress response itself may push homocysteine up
- Stress can affect digestion and how you absorb nutrients like B12 and folate (see [can stress affect how your body absorbs nutrients?](/articles/stress-and-nutrient-absorption))
- Stress often comes with poor sleep, skipped meals, and less recovery

So two people with the same MTHFR variant can have very different numbers depending on what their bodies are carrying.

## What to ask your doctor

- Can we check homocysteine, B12, and folate levels, not just the gene?
- Could anything else be raising my homocysteine, like kidney function, thyroid, or medications?
- How often should we recheck it?

Never stop or start supplements or medication without talking to your doctor. High homocysteine deserves real medical care.
