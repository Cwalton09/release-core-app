Your stomach turns before a big meeting. You feel queasy every morning. You lose your appetite during stressful weeks, or feel sick after eating even small meals. Sometimes the nausea shows up out of nowhere.

You've ruled out pregnancy, a stomach bug, and food poisoning. So why do you keep feeling sick?

## What the research found

In 2002, researchers in Norway published a study using health surveys from about **62,000 people** in the Nord-Trøndelag Health Study (HUNT).

- About **1 in 8** people had dealt with nausea in the past year
- People with anxiety were about **3.4 times more likely** to have nausea
- Depression was also linked to nausea, but less strongly than anxiety
- Among people with major nausea complaints, about **41% had an anxiety disorder**

## Why anxiety makes you feel sick

Your gut and your brain are in constant conversation through your nervous system, especially the vagus nerve.

When your body senses danger, it shifts resources away from digestion. Your stomach can slow down or tighten, stress hormones rise, and your gut sends uncomfortable signals back to your brain. From a survival point of view, that makes sense: if you're running from danger, you don't need to digest breakfast.

But if your nervous system stays on alert, that queasy, unsettled feeling can become your everyday normal.

## Signs your nausea may be coming from your nervous system

- Nausea before or during stressful events
- Feeling queasy in the morning, then better later in the day
- Losing your appetite when you're worried
- Nausea with a racing heart, shakiness, or dread
- It gets better on vacation or during calm seasons

Nausea can also come from pregnancy, medications, migraines, reflux, gallbladder problems, infections, and other medical causes. See a doctor for ongoing nausea, vomiting, weight loss, or severe pain.
