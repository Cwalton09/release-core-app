You fall asleep fine. Then, like clockwork, your eyes open at 3 AM. Your mind is suddenly wide awake, running through tomorrow, replaying yesterday, worrying about things that seemed fine at dinner.

By the time you drift off again, the alarm is about to go off.

If this happens to you most nights, your body isn't broken. It may be doing exactly what it's been trained to do.

## What happens in your body at night

Your stress hormone, cortisol, follows a daily rhythm. It's lowest around the start of the night, then begins climbing in the early morning hours to help you wake up.

At the same time, your sleep gets lighter in the second half of the night. You spend less time in deep sleep and more time in lighter sleep, which is easier to wake from.

For most people, that's fine. But if your stress system is already running hot, that early-morning rise can be enough to tip you awake.

## What the research found

In 2001, researchers at Penn State published a study in the *Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism* on people with chronic insomnia.

They found that people with insomnia had **higher overall levels of cortisol and ACTH**, the hormone that tells your body to make cortisol, across the 24-hour day. The biggest increases were in the evening and the first part of the night.

The more disturbed their sleep, the higher their stress hormones. The researchers described insomnia as a disorder of **hyperarousal**: a body whose stress system doesn't fully switch off at night.

Not every later study has found exactly the same results, but the idea that insomnia is tied to an over-active stress system has strong support.

## Why the same time every night

When you wake at the same time over and over, your body starts to expect it. The waking becomes a habit, and the worry about waking becomes part of the pattern: you wake, check the clock, think "not again," and your body gets even more alert.

## Signs your nervous system is waking you up

- You wake at the same time most nights
- Your heart is racing or you feel a jolt when you wake
- Your mind starts planning or worrying right away
- You're exhausted in the day but wired at night
- You sleep better on vacation

Waking at night can also come from sleep apnea, reflux, needing to pee, hormone changes, pain, alcohol, or medications. Talk to your doctor, especially if you snore or wake up gasping.
