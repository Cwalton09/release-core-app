A stressful week, and your skin flares. Itchy patches, redness, breakouts, or a rash that seems to come out of nowhere.

If your skin seems to react to stress, you're not imagining it. Researchers have a name for the link between your nervous system and your skin: the brain-skin axis.

## The brain and skin are connected

Your skin and your nervous system actually develop from the same tissue before you're born. They stay closely connected for life.

A 2025 review of 159 studies describes a two-way connection between the brain and the skin. When you're stressed, your brain and stress glands release hormones and chemical messengers that are linked to skin inflammation. And signals from inflamed skin can travel back to the brain too.

## What research says about flares

Reviews describe stress as a common trigger for psoriasis flares, along with infections, injury, and medications. Researchers also describe it working in both directions:

- Stress can come before a flare
- A flare can cause more stress, embarrassment, and poor sleep
- That stress can then make the skin worse

That can turn into a vicious circle. Much of the detailed research on how this works is still being done, some of it in animals, so there's still a lot to learn.

## Signs your skin is reacting to stress

- Flares during hard seasons or after big events
- Itching that gets worse when you're anxious
- Skin that calms down on vacation
- Breakouts or rashes before deadlines or conflict
- Scratching or picking more when you're stressed

## Your skin deserves care

Skin conditions are real medical conditions. Please work with your doctor or dermatologist on treatment. Understanding the nervous system side can be one more piece of the picture, alongside the care you already have.
