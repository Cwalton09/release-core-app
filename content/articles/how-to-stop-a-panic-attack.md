How do you stop a panic attack? You can't always stop one instantly, but you can help it pass faster. Panic attacks usually peak within about 10 minutes and then fade. What helps most is not fighting them: slow your exhale, plant your feet, and remind yourself that the feeling is intense but not dangerous. Then later, look at what set it off.

During a panic attack you might feel a pounding heart, chest tightness, shaking, numbness, dizziness, or a sense that you're dying or losing control.

## Get help right away if…

If it's your first episode, or you have chest pain, pain spreading to your arm or jaw, trouble breathing, or fainting, call 911. Panic and heart problems can feel similar, so get checked. If panic attacks keep happening, talk to your doctor. Panic disorder is very treatable.

## What research shows

A review in *The Lancet* notes that panic disorder affects up to 5% of people at some point in their lives, and that there's strong evidence for effective treatments, including cognitive behavioral therapy and some medications. Panic attacks themselves are sudden surges of intense fear that build quickly, usually peaking within about 10 minutes.

A Stanford study found that cyclic sighing, a breathing pattern with long exhales, improved mood and lowered breathing rate more than meditation did. Slow exhales help switch on your body's calming system.

## What to do in the moment

1. **Slow your exhale.** Breathe in through your nose, then breathe out longer through your mouth.
2. **Plant your feet.** Press them into the floor and notice something solid.
3. **Name what's happening.** "This is panic. It peaks and passes."
4. **Don't fight it.** Fighting the feeling usually makes it stronger.
5. **Look around.** Name three things you can see.

## Common questions

**How long does a panic attack last?** Most peak within about 10 minutes and fade after that.

**Can a panic attack hurt you?** It feels terrifying, but a panic attack itself isn't dangerous. Still, get any first episode or chest pain checked.

**Why do panic attacks come out of nowhere?** Your body may be reacting to something it learned to treat as danger, even if your mind doesn't notice it.
