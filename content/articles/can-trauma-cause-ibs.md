Can trauma cause IBS? Trauma isn't the only cause of irritable bowel syndrome, but research shows a strong link. A 2014 meta-analysis of 71 studies found that people who had been through trauma were about 2.7 times more likely to have a functional somatic syndrome, a group that includes IBS, fibromyalgia, and chronic fatigue. Your gut is wired directly to your nervous system, and a body that learned the world isn't safe can keep the gut on alert for years.

You might notice your gut reacts to stress before you even feel stressed, or that symptoms started during or after a hard season of life.

## See a doctor first

IBS is diagnosed after ruling out other conditions. Please see a doctor for gut symptoms, and get checked right away for blood in your stool, weight loss, fever, vomiting, waking at night with symptoms, anemia, or a family history of colon cancer, celiac disease, or IBD.

## What research shows

The 2014 meta-analysis in *Psychosomatic Medicine* combined 71 studies. People who reported trauma, including emotional, physical, or sexual abuse in childhood or adulthood, combat, or PTSD, were 2.7 times more likely to have a functional somatic syndrome such as IBS. The link was strongest with PTSD.

The research shows a link, not that trauma is behind every case of IBS.

## How trauma reaches the gut

Your gut has its own nervous system and is in constant contact with your brain through the vagus nerve and stress hormones. After trauma, the nervous system can stay on alert, which can:

- Make gut nerves more sensitive to normal sensations
- Speed up or slow down digestion
- Change gut bacteria and the gut lining
- Keep belly and pelvic muscles braced

## Signs trauma may be part of your IBS

- Symptoms started during or after a hard time
- Your gut flares with stress, conflict, or reminders
- Diets and medications only partly help
- You also have other symptoms, like anxiety, poor sleep, or chronic pain

## Common questions

**Can childhood trauma cause IBS?** Childhood adversity is linked to a higher risk of IBS in adulthood.

**Can therapy help IBS?** Yes. Gut-directed hypnotherapy and cognitive behavioral therapy have good evidence for IBS.

**Why does my stomach react before I feel stressed?** Your gut often picks up your nervous system's alarm before your mind does.
