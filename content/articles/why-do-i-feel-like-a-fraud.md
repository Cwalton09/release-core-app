You got the promotion. The client said yes. People tell you you're good at what you do. And still, a voice in the back of your mind says: *they're going to figure out you don't belong here.*

That's impostor syndrome. And for a lot of people, it isn't really about their skills at all.

## What the research found

In 2019, researchers published a systematic review in the *Journal of General Internal Medicine* that pulled together 62 studies with more than 14,000 people.

They found:

- Impostor feelings were reported by anywhere from **9% to 82%** of people, depending on the group and how it was measured
- It showed up in men and women, and at every career stage, from students to senior professionals
- It often came alongside **depression and anxiety**
- It was linked to **burnout**, lower job satisfaction, and weaker job performance

The authors also noted that no published studies had tested a treatment for it.

## Why success can feel unsafe

Impostor syndrome looks like a confidence problem. But for many people, it's a safety problem.

If you grew up being criticized, compared, or only praised when you were perfect, your nervous system may have learned that being seen is dangerous. Success means more people watching. More chances to get it wrong. More to lose.

So instead of feeling proud, your body braces. It overprepares, overworks, and waits to be exposed.

## Signs it's coming from your nervous system

- You credit your success to luck, timing, or other people
- You overprepare for everything, then feel drained
- Praise makes you uncomfortable instead of happy
- You avoid going for things you're qualified for
- One small mistake feels like proof you're a fraud
- You feel anxious right after a win
