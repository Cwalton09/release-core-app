Why do you feel like everyone hates you? Often because your nervous system is primed to expect rejection. It reads neutral moments, like a short text, a quiet coworker, or a friend who's slow to reply, as signs that someone is upset with you. That feeling is real, but it usually comes from what your nervous system learned in the past, not from what other people actually think.

You replay conversations. You read tone into every message. You walk into a room and feel like everyone is judging you.

## Your nervous system is scanning for rejection

Humans are wired to care about belonging. For most of history, being rejected by the group was dangerous. So your nervous system watches closely for signs that you might be pushed out.

If it learned early that love could be taken away, that people got upset easily, or that you were often in trouble, it may become very sensitive to any hint of disapproval. Researchers sometimes call this rejection sensitivity. It runs through your nervous system's automatic threat detection, called [neuroception](/articles/what-is-neuroception).

## Why neutral feels negative

A nervous system on guard fills in blanks with the worst-case story:

- A one-word reply means they're annoyed
- A serious face means they don't like you
- Not being invited means you're unwanted
- Silence means you did something wrong

Most of the time, the other person is just tired, busy, or dealing with their own day. Other people's reactions are often about their own nervous systems, not you.

## When it gets worse

- When you're tired, stressed, or overwhelmed
- Before your period (see [why am I so anxious before my period?](/articles/why-do-i-feel-anxious-before-my-period))
- After a conflict or rejection
- In new places or around new people

## Signs it's your nervous system

- You feel disliked in very different groups
- You need lots of reassurance that people aren't upset
- You apologize often (see [why do I apologize so much?](/articles/why-do-i-apologize-so-much))
- You feel like an outsider (see [why do I feel like I don't fit in?](/articles/why-do-i-feel-like-i-dont-fit-in))
- The feeling fades when you feel truly safe with someone

If you feel hopeless or like people would be better off without you, please reach out to a mental health professional, or call or text 988.

## Common questions

**Why do I always think people are mad at me?** A nervous system primed for rejection reads neutral signals as anger or disapproval.

**Is it normal to feel like everyone hates you?** Many people feel this, especially during stress. When it's constant, it's often an old protective pattern.

**Why do I feel like everyone hates me at work?** Work stirs up feelings about being judged and evaluated, which can trigger rejection sensitivity.

## Why it keeps coming back

The feeling keeps returning because your nervous system is still following a rule it learned about people.

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't what's happening with these people now. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *I have to keep everyone happy to be safe* or *people will leave if I'm not careful*, and today's moments are just turning up its volume.
