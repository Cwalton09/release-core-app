Can grief cause anxiety? Yes. Anxiety is one of the most common, and least talked about, parts of grief. Losing someone shakes your nervous system's sense of safety. Suddenly the world feels less predictable, you may worry about losing others, and your body stays on alert. Some people have panic attacks for the first time after a loss.

You might feel restless, jumpy, or tight in your chest, worry constantly about the people you still have, or feel a wave of panic when you're reminded of the loss.

## Get support if…

Please reach out to a doctor or grief counselor if anxiety or panic is affecting your daily life. Chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting should always be checked, so call 911 if they're severe. If grief feels unbearable or you think about harming yourself, call or text 988 in the US.

## Why grief brings anxiety

- **Your sense of safety is shaken.** Loss shows your nervous system that terrible things can happen, and it may stay on guard.
- **Fear of more loss.** Many people start worrying about their own health or the people they love.
- **Your role changed.** Losing someone can mean new responsibilities, money worries, or loneliness.
- **Your body is under strain.** Stress hormones, poor sleep, and exhaustion all make anxiety worse.

## What research shows

A review in *The Lancet* describes how bereavement brings a wide range of psychological reactions, and how a smaller group of people go on to develop longer-lasting problems. Anxiety is a common one, alongside depression and prolonged grief.

Research on grief and inflammation also shows the body is under real stress during bereavement, which can feed anxiety.

## Signs grief is driving your anxiety

- The anxiety started or worsened after the loss
- It spikes around reminders, dates, or places
- You worry constantly about losing someone else
- You feel unsafe in a way you didn't before

## Common questions

**Is it normal to have anxiety after someone dies?** Yes. It's a very common part of grief.

**Can grief cause panic attacks?** Yes, especially in the early months or around strong reminders.

**When does grief anxiety get better?** For many people it eases as the nervous system adjusts. If it stays intense, support can help.

## Why it can stay stuck

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't only the loss. It's an older rule the loss woke up, like *People I love will leave* or *I have to hold everyone together*, and your nervous system is bracing for the next blow.
