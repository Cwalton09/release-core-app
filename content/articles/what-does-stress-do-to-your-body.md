What does stress do to your body? When you're stressed, your nervous system switches on fight-or-flight. Your body releases adrenaline and cortisol, your heart beats faster, your muscles tighten, your breathing speeds up, and digestion slows down. That's helpful for a short emergency. But when stress goes on for weeks or years, those same changes can lead to headaches, stomach problems, poor sleep, high blood pressure, fatigue, and a weaker immune system.

Your body can't always tell the difference between a lion and an overflowing inbox. It reacts to whatever your nervous system reads as a threat.

## What happens in the moment

Within seconds of a stressful moment:

- **Heart:** beats faster and harder, and blood pressure rises
- **Lungs:** breathing gets faster and shallower
- **Muscles:** tighten, ready to move
- **Gut:** digestion slows, and you may feel butterflies or nausea
- **Eyes:** pupils widen
- **Blood sugar:** rises for quick energy
- **Mind:** gets sharp, focused, and scanning

Once the threat passes, a healthy nervous system switches back to rest-and-digest, and these changes ease.

## What long-term stress does

Stress researcher Bruce McEwen called the cost of long-term stress "allostatic load": the wear and tear when stress hormones stay switched on too long. In his 1998 review in the *New England Journal of Medicine*, he explained that the same hormones that protect us in the short term can harm the body when they don't switch off.

Over time, long-term stress is linked to:

- **Heart and blood vessels:** higher blood pressure and a higher risk of heart disease and stroke
- **Gut:** bloating, heartburn, IBS flares, and changes in appetite
- **Muscles:** neck, shoulder, back, and jaw tension, and headaches
- **Immune system:** getting sick more often and slower healing
- **Sleep:** trouble falling asleep, or waking at 3 a.m.
- **Skin:** flares of acne, eczema, psoriasis, and hives
- **Mind:** anxiety, low mood, brain fog, and trouble concentrating
- **Hormones:** changes to periods and to weight

## Why some people feel stress more in their bodies

Your nervous system learns from your life. If you grew up needing to stay on guard, your body may have learned to treat many situations as threats. That's why two people can face the same stress and have very different reactions.

## Common questions

**Can stress make you physically sick?** Yes. Long-term stress can trigger or worsen many physical symptoms and conditions. It's still important to see a doctor, because stress isn't always the only cause.

**What are the first signs stress is affecting your body?** Common early signs are tension, headaches, poor sleep, stomach changes, and feeling tired but wired.

**How long does it take your body to recover from stress?** After a single stressful moment, your body can settle within minutes to hours. After long-term stress, recovery can take longer, but your body has a real ability to heal.

## Why it keeps coming back

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the job, the bills, or the stress in front of you. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *I have to hold everything together* or *I'm only safe if I'm in control*, and today's stress is just turning up its volume.
