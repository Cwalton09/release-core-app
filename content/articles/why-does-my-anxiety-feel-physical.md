Maybe you don't even feel "worried." You just feel it in your body. A tight chest. A heavy stomach. Shaky hands, a racing heart, a lump in your throat.

A lot of people are searching for this right now: why does my anxiety feel physical? The answer is that anxiety was always physical. It starts in the body.

## What the research found

In 2014, researchers in Finland published a study in PNAS called "Bodily maps of emotions." They asked 701 people to color in a body outline to show where they felt sensations getting stronger or weaker during different emotions.

The maps were strikingly consistent. Each emotion showed up in its own pattern in the body. Fear and anxiety lit up the chest and upper body. The patterns looked the same across people from Western Europe and East Asia, which suggests they come from biology, not culture or habit.

In other words, emotions aren't just thoughts. Your body feels them first, in specific places.

## Why it can feel more physical over time

When your body senses danger, your nervous system shifts into fight or flight. Your heart speeds up. Your breathing gets shallow. Your muscles brace. Your digestion slows down. That's your body getting ready to protect you.

If that happens once in a while, it passes. But when your nervous system stays on alert for a long time, those physical changes stop being occasional. They become your normal. Your chest stays tight. Your stomach stays on edge. And because the worry isn't always loud in your mind, the body is the only place you notice it.

## What keeps the nervous system in fight or flight

Your nervous system is always scanning. When something in your life matches an old experience, a tone of voice, a feeling of being left out, a time of year, it reacts before your thinking mind even notices.

Often, the reaction isn't about today at all. It's connected to something your body learned a long time ago, and it's still following that rule. That's why you can know you're safe and still feel your body bracing.

## Signs your anxiety is living in your body

- A tight or heavy chest
- A knot or flutter in your stomach
- Shaky hands or a trembling feeling inside
- Shallow breathing or not being able to take a full breath
- A racing heart when nothing is happening
- Tension in your jaw, shoulders, or neck

Physical symptoms can have medical causes, so check with your doctor, especially for chest pain, fainting, or trouble breathing.
