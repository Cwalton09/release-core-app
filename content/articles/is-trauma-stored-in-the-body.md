You may have heard the phrase "the body keeps the score," from Bessel van der Kolk's well-known book. Or that trauma is "stored in the body."

It's one of the most talked-about ideas in healing right now. But what does it actually mean?

## What people mean by "stored in the body"

Trauma isn't stored in your body like a file in a drawer. What researchers and clinicians mean is that hard experiences can change how your nervous system responds afterward.

After something overwhelming, the nervous system can stay more alert, more reactive, or more shut down, even long after the danger has passed. That can show up in your body as tension, a racing heart, stomach trouble, startling easily, or feeling numb.

The general idea is widely accepted. Exactly how it works, and which explanations are best supported, is still being studied and debated.

## How it can show up

- Reacting strongly to things that remind your body of the past
- Tension that never fully lets go
- Feeling on guard, jumpy, or watchful
- Feeling numb, far away, or disconnected
- Stomach issues or pain that flares with stress
- Knowing you're safe but not feeling safe

## It doesn't have to be a "big T" trauma

Many people think trauma only means major events like accidents, abuse, or disasters. Those can absolutely affect the nervous system.

But the nervous system can also be shaped by experiences that didn't seem "big enough" to count, like feeling unseen, criticized, alone, or unsafe at home over and over. Your body doesn't compare your story to anyone else's. It just learns from what happened.

## Your body learned for a reason

If your nervous system is still reacting, it's because at some point, those reactions helped protect you. You can read more about [why your body reacts when you know you're safe](/articles/why-body-reacts-when-you-know-you-are-safe).

## Why it keeps coming back

Most of the time, we don't know the real reason behind what's driving the trigger. We can guess, but the actual trigger is usually something we would never think of on our own. And until it's found, your body will keep reacting like it's still happening.

That's why you do a session. In a Release Core session, you gently activate the original trigger so it can be rewired, and your body communicates what the actual trigger is and where it started. You don't have to guess.
