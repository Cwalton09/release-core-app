The injury healed. The scans look fine. Maybe your doctor even told you there's nothing left to fix.

But it still hurts.

If that's you, it can feel lonely and confusing. But there's a well-studied explanation for it, and it has a lot to do with your nervous system.

## The alarm can keep ringing

When you first get hurt, pain is your body's alarm. It protects the injured area while it heals. Normally, as the tissue heals, the alarm quiets down.

But sometimes, the nervous system stays on high alert even after the danger is gone. Researchers call this central sensitization. The nerves in the spinal cord and brain become more reactive, so they amplify signals they would normally filter out.

That can lead to:

- Pain from things that shouldn't hurt, like light touch
- More pain than expected from small bumps or movement
- Pain that spreads or moves around
- Pain that flares with stress, poor sleep, or worry

## Old loops keep the pain intact

Think of it like a path through the grass. The more often a signal travels the same route, the easier that route becomes. Over time, the nervous system can get very good at producing pain.

And the loop often feeds itself:

- Pain makes you brace and guard
- Bracing keeps your body tense and on alert
- Worry about the pain keeps your nervous system scanning
- Scanning makes you notice every sensation
- Your nervous system reads that as danger and turns the pain up again

The original injury may be long gone. But the loop it started can keep running.

## Why the loop often started before the injury

For many people, the nervous system was already on alert before the pain began. A hard season, an old fear, or a belief like "I have to push through" or "I can't let my guard down" can make it much easier for the alarm to get stuck.

Sometimes the pain is connected to what was happening in your life when it started, not just to the injury itself.

## Your pain is real

This doesn't mean your pain is in your head. Sensitized pain is real pain, produced by a real nervous system. Keep working with your doctor, and get checked for any new, worsening, or different pain.
