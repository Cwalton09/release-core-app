You catch yourself with your teeth pressed together. Your dentist mentions you're grinding at night. You wake up with a sore jaw, a headache, or tight cheeks.

Jaw clenching is one of the most common ways stress shows up in the body.

## Why stress goes to your jaw

When your nervous system senses a threat, your muscles tighten to get ready. Your jaw is one of the places that tightens first. Clenching is part of bracing, the same way your shoulders creep up toward your ears.

For a short burst of stress, that's normal. But when your nervous system stays on alert, your jaw can stay clenched for hours, even while you sleep.

## Holding things in

For many people, the jaw holds more than physical tension. It's where we hold back words we didn't say, anger we didn't feel safe showing, or the effort of keeping it together.

If you grew up needing to stay quiet, keep the peace, or "bite your tongue," your jaw may have learned that holding tight is how you stay safe.

## Signs your jaw is holding stress

- Waking up with a sore jaw or headache
- Teeth grinding at night
- Clenching while you concentrate, drive, or scroll
- Tight cheeks, temples, or neck
- Clicking or tension around your jaw joint
- Clenching more during stressful weeks

Talk to your dentist or doctor about jaw pain, clicking, or grinding, since it can damage your teeth and may need its own care.

## Why you can't just "relax your jaw"

You can unclench on purpose, but a few minutes later, it's back. That's because the clenching isn't a decision. It's your nervous system bracing for something it still believes is coming.
