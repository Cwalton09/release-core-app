Can childhood trauma cause autoimmune disease? Research links childhood trauma with a higher risk of autoimmune disease in adulthood. A study from the CDC's Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE) research found that people with more difficult childhood experiences were more likely to be hospitalized with an autoimmune disease decades later. Genetics, infections, hormones, and environment matter too, so childhood stress isn't the only cause. But it may be one piece of the puzzle.

You might have lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, thyroid disease, MS, or another autoimmune condition, and wonder whether what you lived through as a child is connected.

## Keep working with your doctor

Autoimmune diseases need medical care. Please keep working with your doctor and keep taking your medications. Looking at childhood stress doesn't replace treatment. It can work alongside it.

## What research shows

A 2009 study in *Psychosomatic Medicine* followed 15,357 adults in the ACE Study. Childhood adversities included abuse, witnessing domestic violence, and growing up with household substance use, mental illness, divorce, or an incarcerated family member. The more of these people had, the higher their chance of a first hospitalization for an autoimmune disease. Compared with people who had none, people with two or more ACEs had a 70% to 100% higher risk, depending on the type of autoimmune disease.

## Why childhood stress may affect the immune system

When a child lives with ongoing stress, their stress system develops on high alert. Over time, long-term stress can change inflammation and how the immune system is regulated. Researchers think this may make the immune system more likely to misfire later in life. See [can stress cause autoimmune disease](/articles/can-stress-cause-autoimmune-disease).

## Common questions

**What is an ACE score?** It counts how many types of difficult childhood experiences you had, from 0 to 10.

**Does a high ACE score mean I'll get an autoimmune disease?** No. It raises the risk on average, but many people with high scores never develop one.

**Can healing childhood trauma help autoimmune disease?** It isn't a cure, but many people find calming their stress response helps them handle flares.
