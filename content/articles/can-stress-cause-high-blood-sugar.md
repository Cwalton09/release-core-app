Can stress raise your blood sugar? Yes. When you're stressed, your body releases cortisol and adrenaline, which tell your liver to release sugar into your blood for quick energy. That's helpful in an emergency. But when stress goes on for a long time, blood sugar can stay higher, and research links long-term stress with a higher risk of type 2 diabetes. Stress doesn't cause diabetes on its own, but it can be part of the picture.

Many people with diabetes or prediabetes notice higher readings during stressful weeks, even when their food and medicine haven't changed.

## Work with your doctor

Diabetes and high blood sugar need medical care. Never change your medication without your doctor. Get urgent care for very high blood sugar with extreme thirst, frequent urination, confusion, vomiting, fruity breath, or trouble breathing. Signs of diabetes include increased thirst and urination, blurry vision, slow-healing sores, and tiredness. Ask your doctor about testing if you notice them.

## How stress raises blood sugar

In fight-or-flight, your body wants fuel ready for your muscles. Stress hormones:

- Tell your liver to release stored sugar
- Make your cells respond less to insulin for a while
- Can increase cravings for quick-energy foods

When stress is short, levels come back down. When stress is constant, your body may keep blood sugar higher than it needs to.

## What research shows

A 2017 review in *Nature Reviews Endocrinology* looked at psychological stress and type 2 diabetes. It found growing evidence that psychological and social stress predicts new cases of type 2 diabetes and worse outcomes in people who already have it. The authors called stress a risk factor that can be changed.

Part of the link comes from how stress affects sleep, eating, and exercise. Part comes from stress hormones themselves.

## Signs stress may be affecting your blood sugar

- Your readings are higher on stressful days
- Your numbers climbed during a hard season
- You crave sugar or carbs when stressed
- Poor sleep makes your numbers worse

## Common questions

**Can stress cause diabetes?** Stress alone doesn't cause diabetes, but long-term stress is linked to higher risk, along with genetics, weight, activity, and diet.

**How much can stress raise blood sugar?** It varies from person to person. Track your readings on stressful days and share them with your doctor.

**Can lowering stress help blood sugar?** It can help, alongside your medical care. Better sleep and a calmer nervous system support steadier levels.
