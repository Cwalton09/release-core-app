Why do you feel a lump in your throat? That stuck feeling, when nothing is actually there, is called globus sensation, or globus pharyngeus. It's very common. In one survey of more than 3,000 people, about 1 in 5 had experienced it. It's linked to acid reflux and throat muscle tension, and people with globus are more likely to have anxiety, depression, or stress. Your throat is one of the first places your body braces when you hold back emotion.

It feels like a pill that won't go down, or a ball sitting in your throat. You keep swallowing to clear it. Food goes down fine, but the feeling stays.

## When to see a doctor

Please see a doctor if you have trouble or pain when swallowing food, food getting stuck, weight loss, hoarseness that lasts, a lump you can feel on your neck, coughing up blood, or symptoms getting worse. Those need evaluation.

Globus is usually diagnosed after other causes, like reflux, are checked.

## How stress and the throat are connected

Your throat is surrounded by muscles controlled by your nervous system. When you feel emotion you can't express, like sadness you're holding back, anger you're swallowing, or words you can't say, those muscles can tighten. That's the "lump in your throat" feeling people get right before they cry.

If your nervous system stays on guard, that tension can stick around. Stress can also worsen reflux, which irritates the throat.

## What research shows

- In a survey of 3,006 people in China, about 21.5% had experienced globus at some point. Anxiety, depression, and sleep problems were all more common in people with globus.
- In a hospital study in Nepal, about 73% of people with globus had psychological distress, compared with about 40% of people without it.

These studies show a strong link, not proof of cause. But they match what so many people feel: the throat tightens with what we hold in.

## Signs it may be stress-related

- It gets worse during stress or emotional moments
- You can eat and drink normally
- It comes and goes
- You often feel like you're holding something in
- Your doctor hasn't found another cause

## Common questions

**Can anxiety cause a lump in your throat?** Anxiety is strongly linked to globus sensation, often through throat muscle tension.

**Why do I feel like something is stuck in my throat?** Often it's globus, from tight throat muscles or reflux. A doctor can rule out other causes.

**How long does globus last?** It can come and go for weeks or months, often easing as stress settles.

## Why it keeps coming back

When the lump keeps coming back, your body may still be holding back something it never felt safe to say or feel.
