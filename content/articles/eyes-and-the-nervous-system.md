How does the nervous system affect your eyes? Your autonomic nervous system, the part that runs on its own, controls your pupils, how your eyes focus, your tears, and the blood flow inside your eyes. Fight-or-flight widens your pupils and gets your eyes ready to scan for danger. Rest-and-digest narrows your pupils and helps your eyes focus up close. When your nervous system is stuck on alert, your eyes can feel strained, sensitive to light, dry, or slightly blurry.

Many people with anxiety or long-term stress say their vision just feels "off." Lights seem too bright, screens are harder to look at, their eyes feel tired by noon, or the world looks a little unreal.

## See an eye doctor first

Vision changes can come from many things, including needing glasses, dry eye, migraines, eye disease, and other medical conditions. Please get an eye exam for any vision change that lasts. Go to the emergency room right away for sudden vision loss, double vision, flashes of light, a curtain over part of your vision, eye pain, or vision changes with weakness, numbness, or trouble speaking.

## How your nervous system runs your eyes

A 2015 review in *Comprehensive Physiology* describes how both branches of the autonomic nervous system work in the eye:

- **Pupils.** The sympathetic (fight-or-flight) side opens the pupil wider. The parasympathetic (rest) side makes it smaller.
- **Focus.** The parasympathetic side tightens the small muscle that lets your lens focus on things up close, like reading.
- **Tears.** Nerves control the tears that keep your eyes moist and comfortable.
- **Blood flow.** Both sides help control blood flow inside the eye.

Your eyes are also closely tied to your brain's alarm system. Your brain uses vision to scan for threats, so when you're on alert, your eyes and attention go wide and watchful.

## What stress does to your eyes

When fight-or-flight is switched on, wider pupils let in more light. That can make bright rooms and screens feel harsh and things look less sharp up close. Tension often brings less blinking, which can dry your eyes, and a tight jaw, forehead, and neck, which can add to eye strain and headaches.

Long-term stress and high cortisol are also linked to an eye condition called central serous chorioretinopathy, which can blur vision. See [can stress cause blurry vision](/articles/can-stress-cause-blurry-vision).

## Signs your nervous system may be affecting your eyes

- Light feels too bright, especially when you're anxious
- Your eyes tire quickly or feel strained by the end of the day
- Your vision feels a little blurry or "off," but your eye exam is normal
- You notice eye twitching on stressful days
- Things sometimes look unreal or far away when you're overwhelmed

## Common questions

**Can a dysregulated nervous system affect your eyesight?** It can affect how your vision feels, such as light sensitivity, strain, and dryness. It doesn't usually change your eyes' structure, but any lasting vision change should be checked.

**Why do my pupils look big when I'm anxious?** Adrenaline from fight-or-flight widens the pupils so you can take in more of your surroundings.

**Can calming my nervous system help my eyes feel better?** Many people find their eyes feel more comfortable on calmer days. Blinking breaks, rest from screens, and treating dry eye can help too.
