You stand up and your heart races. You get dizzy, shaky, or foggy. You feel exhausted after doing very little. And maybe you've been told it's "just anxiety."

If that sounds familiar, you may have heard of POTS, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome. It's real, it's more common than people think, and it's a condition of the nervous system.

## What the research says

In 2021, a group of experts brought together by the National Institutes of Health published a summary of what's known about POTS in the journal Autonomic Neuroscience.

Here's what they described:

- POTS is a chronic and often disabling disorder of the autonomic nervous system, the part that runs your heart rate, blood pressure, and digestion without you thinking about it.
- When people with POTS stand up, their heart rate jumps far more than it should, even though their blood pressure doesn't drop.
- Fatigue, trouble exercising, and digestive problems often come with it.
- It most often affects women of childbearing age, and it frequently starts in the teen years.
- It can begin after an immune stressor like a viral infection.
- There are currently no FDA-approved medications specifically for POTS, so care usually starts with education and non-drug approaches.

POTS has several possible causes and contributors, and it's not "all in your head." It needs a proper diagnosis, usually with a stand test or tilt table test.

## Where stress and the nervous system fit in

POTS isn't caused by anxiety. But it lives in the same system that runs your stress response.

Your autonomic nervous system has two main modes: the "go" side that speeds you up, and the "rest" side that slows you down. In POTS, the body struggles to keep that balance when you change position. Many people with POTS also find that their symptoms get worse during stressful periods, with poor sleep, or after a big emotional event, because all of those push the "go" side even harder.

## What keeps the nervous system in fight or flight

When your body has learned that it has to stay ready, the "go" side never fully switches off. Your heart rate, breathing, and muscle tension stay a little higher all the time, like an engine idling too fast.

For someone with POTS, that background activation can stack on top of an already sensitive system. A body that feels safe has more room to adjust. A body that's braced has less.

Often, what keeps a nervous system in that braced state isn't what's happening now. It's something the body learned a long time ago and never had the chance to let go of.

## Signs your nervous system may be on high alert

- Your heart races when you stand or after small effort
- You feel wired and exhausted at the same time
- Your symptoms get worse during stressful weeks
- You feel on edge even when nothing is wrong
- You've been told it's "just anxiety," but it feels physical

If you have fainting, chest pain, or a racing heart that won't settle, get medical care right away. If you suspect POTS, ask your doctor about testing.
