If you've been reading about leaky gut, here's a piece of the story that rarely gets talked about: your nervous system.

## Is leaky gut real?

Your gut lining is a barrier. It's supposed to let nutrients through while keeping bacteria and food particles where they belong. How easily things pass through it is called intestinal permeability, and it's real and measurable.

Permeability goes up in conditions like Crohn's disease and celiac disease. It can also go up with intense exercise, anti-inflammatory painkillers like ibuprofen, and pregnancy.

"Leaky gut syndrome" as its own diagnosis is more controversial. Doctors don't have an agreed way to diagnose it, and a 2019 review from a Mayo Clinic gastroenterologist found it hasn't been proven that fixing the barrier alone cures any disease. So be careful with anyone selling a single fix.

But one thing the research does show clearly: **stress affects your gut barrier.**

## What the research found

**A speech was enough.** In a 2014 study published in the journal *Gut*, Belgian researchers measured gut permeability in healthy volunteers under different conditions. **Giving a public speech made the small intestine measurably leakier.** These were healthy people with no gut problems. A single stressful event was enough.

**The stress hormone CRH did the same thing.** When researchers gave volunteers CRH, one of the first hormones your body releases under stress, it also increased gut permeability.

**Mast cells were the link.** When volunteers took a drug that calms mast cells, the immune cells that sit right next to your nerves, stress and CRH no longer made the gut leakier. Your stress response and your gut lining are connected through those cells.

## What this means

If one speech can open up the gut barrier in a healthy person, imagine a nervous system that has been on alert for years.

You can do everything right with food and supplements, but if your body is constantly releasing stress hormones, it keeps pushing against the very barrier you're trying to heal.

## Signs your nervous system may be part of your gut issues

- Your gut gets worse during stressful seasons, even when your diet doesn't change
- You've become sensitive to more and more foods over time
- Your symptoms ease on vacation or during calm stretches
- You react to foods sometimes but not others
- You feel wired, tense, or on guard most of the time

Gut symptoms deserve proper medical care. See your doctor before cutting out major food groups, and especially if you notice blood in your stool, weight loss, or symptoms that wake you up at night.
