Can anxiety affect your vision? Yes, anxiety can change how your vision feels. Common changes are light sensitivity, blurry or strained vision, tunnel vision during panic, and things looking unreal or far away. Anxiety can also make you notice things that were already there, like floaters or visual static. Your nervous system controls your pupils and focus, and when it's on high alert, your eyes and brain work differently.

These changes can be scary, and fear makes them easier to notice, which can make the anxiety worse.

## Get help right away if…

Go to an emergency room or call 911 for sudden vision loss, double vision, a sudden shower of new floaters, flashes of light, a curtain or shadow over part of your vision, eye pain, or vision changes with a severe headache, weakness, numbness, or trouble speaking. These can be signs of a retinal tear, stroke, or other emergency. See an eye doctor for any vision change that lasts.

## Common ways anxiety changes vision

**Light sensitivity.** Adrenaline widens your pupils, letting in more light. Bright rooms, sunlight, and screens can feel harsh.

**Tunnel vision.** During panic, your attention can narrow toward the "threat." Some people feel like the edges of their vision are fading.

**Blurry or strained vision.** Wider pupils, less blinking, and tense face muscles can make it harder to focus and leave your eyes tired.

**Things looking unreal.** Feeling like the world looks flat, foggy, or like a dream is called derealization. It's a common response to overwhelming stress. See [derealization](/articles/derealization-feeling-disconnected).

**Noticing floaters.** Floaters come from changes in the gel inside your eye. Anxiety doesn't cause them, but when you're on alert and scanning your body, you notice them much more. A *sudden* increase in floaters needs an eye exam the same day.

**Visual snow.** Some people see constant tiny dots or static across their whole vision. Visual snow syndrome is a neurological condition, and anxiety doesn't cause it on its own, but they often show up together.

## What research shows

In a 2021 study of 125 people with visual snow syndrome, rates of anxiety, depression, poor sleep, and feeling unreal were high. People who felt more detached and unreal also had more severe visual symptoms. This suggests the nervous system's state and how vision is experienced are closely linked.

Research on the eye also shows that the fight-or-flight side of the nervous system directly controls pupil size, which helps explain light sensitivity during anxiety.

## Signs anxiety may be part of it

- Your vision changes come and go with stress or panic
- They ease when you're calm, distracted, or rested
- Your eye exam was normal
- You check your vision often and worry about what it means

## Common questions

**Can anxiety cause seeing spots?** Anxiety can make you notice floaters and spots more. New or sudden spots, especially with flashes, need an eye exam.

**Why does everything look too bright when I'm anxious?** Your pupils widen during fight-or-flight, so more light gets in.

**Will my vision go back to normal?** Vision changes from anxiety usually ease as your nervous system calms. If yours don't, see an eye doctor or neurologist.
