Can anxiety make you throw up? Yes. Anxiety can cause nausea, gagging, dry heaving, and sometimes vomiting. Your gut and brain are wired together, and when your nervous system goes into fight-or-flight, it changes how your stomach moves and how sensitive it feels. For some people, that's enough to bring food back up.

You might throw up before a test, an interview, or a hard conversation, gag when you brush your teeth on stressful mornings, or feel sick every time you're anxious.

## See a doctor if…

Please see a doctor if vomiting happens often, if you can't keep fluids down, if you see blood or something that looks like coffee grounds, or if vomiting comes with severe stomach pain, a high fever, a bad headache, or signs of dehydration like dizziness and very little urine. Repeated vomiting can have many medical causes and can harm your teeth and throat over time.

## What research shows

A review in the *Journal of Physiology and Pharmacology* explains that stress changes gut function through the brain-gut axis. It changes how the gut moves, makes it more sensitive, and changes digestive secretions. These changes are linked to a wide range of digestive symptoms.

In cyclic vomiting syndrome, a condition with repeated bouts of severe vomiting, researchers have found anxiety to be very common. Anxiety measures also predicted quality of life in young people with the condition.

## Why anxiety triggers vomiting

- **Fight-or-flight slows your stomach.** Food sits longer, which can bring on nausea.
- **Your gut becomes more sensitive.** Normal sensations can feel overwhelming.
- **Stress hormones and adrenaline.** These can trigger the nausea reflex.
- **Fast breathing and swallowing air.** These add to the queasy feeling.

## What helps in the moment

- Breathe out slowly, longer than you breathe in
- Sip cold water
- Get fresh air
- Put something cool on your neck or wrists
- Let your belly soften instead of bracing

## Common questions

**Is it normal to throw up from anxiety?** It's common, especially before big events. If it happens often, see a doctor.

**Why do I gag when I'm nervous?** Your nervous system makes your throat and stomach more reactive, which sets off the gag reflex more easily.

**How do I stop throwing up from anxiety?** Calming your body in the moment helps. Finding what keeps the alarm going helps more over time.
