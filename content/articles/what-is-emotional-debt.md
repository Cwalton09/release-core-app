You've probably heard of financial debt. But what about emotional debt?

Behavior expert Chase Hughes describes emotional debt as what happens when feelings we never fully felt or processed keep building up in the nervous system, a lot like interest on a loan. The longer it goes unpaid, the more it costs.

## How emotional debt builds

Every time something hurt and you had to push it down, a little debt was created:

- The time you couldn't cry, so you held it in
- The anger you weren't allowed to show
- The fear you had to hide to keep everyone else calm
- The grief you never had time for
- The moments you were told you were "too sensitive"

You got through it. You had to. But your body didn't get to finish the feeling. So it kept it, waiting for a safe moment that never came.

## The interest you pay

Emotional debt doesn't sit quietly. You pay interest on it every day, often without realizing it:

- Overreacting to small things
- Feeling exhausted for no clear reason
- Tension, stomach trouble, or pain that won't let go
- Avoiding people, places, or conversations
- Feeling numb or far away
- Repeating the same patterns in relationships

Some of what people think of as personality may actually be built from old, unfinished emotional experiences. "I'm just an anxious person." "I'm just not a crier." "I'm just the strong one." Sometimes those aren't who you are. They're what you had to become.

## Why you can't pay it off by thinking

You can't think your way out of emotional debt, because it isn't stored as a thought. It's stored in your body, as patterns your nervous system learned to protect you. That's why understanding your story doesn't always change how you feel. You can read more about this in [why old situations still run your life](/articles/why-old-situations-still-run-your-life).

## Paying it down

Emotional debt starts to clear when your body finally gets to complete what it couldn't back then, and when the old situation is seen from a new perspective, one where you're safe now.

## Why it keeps coming back

Most of the time, we don't know the real reason behind what's driving the trigger. We can guess, but the actual trigger is usually something we would never think of on our own. And until it's found, your body will keep paying interest on feelings it never got to finish.

That's why you do a session. In a Release Core session, you gently activate the original trigger so it can be rewired, and your body communicates what the actual trigger is and where it started. You don't have to guess.

## How Release Core helps

First, you ask your nervous system whether there is an emotional connection to the feelings you've been carrying. If there is, you ask your body to lead you directly to it, so you can start the rewire. You don't have to dig through years of your life on your own.

You find the age the pattern started, what was happening in your life back then, and what your nervous system learned from it.

Then you rewire it, with questions like "Can you show me what it feels like to finally let this feeling be complete?" A question gives your nervous system something to go looking for instead of something to argue with. In my experience, that's where the shift happens, and your body can stop paying interest on the past.
