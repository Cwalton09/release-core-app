For a lot of people, celiac disease seems to show up out of nowhere. You ate bread your whole life. Then, after a pregnancy, a loss, a divorce, or a brutal year at work, your body suddenly couldn't handle it anymore.

It's a story many people with celiac disease tell. And researchers have looked into it.

## What celiac disease is

Celiac disease is an autoimmune condition. When someone with celiac eats gluten, a protein in wheat, barley, and rye, the immune system attacks the lining of the small intestine. That damage can cause bloating, diarrhea, fatigue, anemia, brain fog, and trouble absorbing nutrients.

You need certain genes to get celiac disease, and gluten is what sets off the immune reaction. **Stress doesn't cause celiac disease.** A strict gluten-free diet is the treatment.

## What the research found

**Stressful events before diagnosis.** In a 2013 study, Italian researchers compared 186 adults with celiac disease to 96 people with acid reflux. The people with celiac reported **more frequent and more severe stressful life events in the years before their diagnosis.**

**Pregnancy stood out.** About 20% of women with celiac described their pregnancy as a negative or stressful event. None of the women in the comparison group did.

The researchers suggested that stressful events may help bring celiac disease to the surface, or speed up how fast symptoms show up. It's an observational study based on what people remembered, so it shows a link, not proof. But it matches what so many people describe.

## What this means

Your nervous system and your immune system talk to each other constantly. When your body is under long-term stress, the immune system can become more reactive, and the gut lining can become more sensitive.

For someone who already carries the genes for celiac disease, a hard season may be part of what tips things over.

And after diagnosis, the stress doesn't always stop. Reading every label, worrying about cross-contamination, eating out, being the "difficult" one at family dinners: living with celiac can keep your nervous system on alert all on its own.

## Signs your nervous system may be part of the picture

- Your symptoms started or got much worse after a stressful season
- You still feel gut symptoms or fatigue even when you're strictly gluten-free
- Eating out or eating at other people's houses makes your body tense
- You feel anxious around food even when you know it's safe
- You're always bracing for the next accidental exposure

Celiac disease needs proper medical care. Don't go gluten-free before you're tested, because it can make the test results wrong. If you have symptoms even on a strict gluten-free diet, talk to your doctor.
