The TV is on. Someone's talking to you. The dog is barking. The kids are asking for snacks. And suddenly you can't take one more sound.

You might snap, need to leave the room, or feel like you're going to cry. That's sensory overload.

## What sensory overload is

Sensory overload happens when your nervous system takes in more than it can process at once. Sounds, lights, smells, textures, clutter, and crowds all pile up until your body hits its limit.

When that happens, your nervous system can shift into fight, flight, or shutdown. You might feel irritable, anxious, panicky, or numb and checked out.

## Why some people get overloaded faster

Everyone has a limit, but some people reach it much faster. Sensory overload is common with ADHD, autism, anxiety, PTSD, and chronic stress.

A nervous system that's already on alert has less room left over. If your body is spending energy scanning for danger, there's less capacity for the noise of everyday life.

## Common signs of sensory overload

- Noise feels physically painful or unbearable
- Clutter makes you anxious or irritable
- Crowds or busy stores leave you drained
- You need to escape to a quiet, dark room
- Certain textures, tags, or fabrics feel intolerable
- You snap at people when there's too much going on
- You feel exhausted after social events

## It's not you being dramatic

Sensory overload is a real nervous system response, not a character flaw. Many people who live with it have been told they're too sensitive or overreacting.

That message can become its own belief, like "I'm too much" or "my needs are a burden," which can keep your body even more on guard.
