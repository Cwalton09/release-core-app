Can stress affect your thyroid? Stress can affect how your thyroid works, and research links stressful life events with the onset of Graves' disease, an autoimmune condition that makes the thyroid overactive. Stress hormones also interact with thyroid hormones. But thyroid disease has many causes, including genetics, and stress is only one possible piece. Thyroid problems need proper testing and treatment.

You might be wondering whether a hard season set off your thyroid symptoms, or notice that fatigue, weight changes, or a racing heart got worse during a stressful time.

## Get tested if…

Please see a doctor if you have a racing heart, weight changes you can't explain, feeling very hot or very cold, hair thinning, tremor, swelling in your neck, bulging or irritated eyes, or exhaustion that won't go away. A simple blood test can check your thyroid. Keep taking any thyroid medication as prescribed.

## What research shows

A 2001 study in *Clinical Endocrinology* compared people newly diagnosed with Graves' disease to people with another thyroid condition and to healthy people. Those with Graves' had more stressful life events, and more negative ones, before their diagnosis. The authors concluded that stressful life events may be a precipitating factor in the onset of Graves' disease.

Research on Hashimoto's, the most common cause of an underactive thyroid, is less clear. Stress may play a role through the immune system, but more research is needed.

## How stress and the thyroid connect

- **Shared control center.** The brain areas that respond to stress also help regulate the thyroid.
- **Cortisol affects thyroid hormones.** Long-term stress can change how thyroid hormones are made and used.
- **Immune changes.** Stress affects the immune system, which matters in autoimmune thyroid disease.
- **Overlapping symptoms.** Anxiety and an overactive thyroid can feel very similar, which is why testing matters.

## Common questions

**Can stress cause hyperthyroidism?** Research links stressful events with the onset of Graves' disease, the most common cause of hyperthyroidism. It's one factor, not the only cause.

**Can anxiety feel like a thyroid problem?** Yes. A racing heart, shakiness, and poor sleep happen in both. A blood test can tell.

**Can lowering stress help my thyroid?** It may support your overall health, but thyroid disease needs medical treatment.
