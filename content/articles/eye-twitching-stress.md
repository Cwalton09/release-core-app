It starts with a tiny flutter in your eyelid. Then it won't stop. It comes and goes all day, sometimes for days or weeks, usually right when life is the most stressful.

An eye twitch is one of the most common ways stress shows up in the body. Here's what's going on.

## What it is

The medical name for the common eyelid twitch is **eyelid myokymia**. It's a fine, rippling contraction of the small muscle around the eye, most often in the lower lid. It's usually harmless and goes away on its own.

## What the research says

StatPearls, a medical reference used by doctors and hosted by the National Library of Medicine, describes eyelid myokymia as a condition that usually happens in healthy people. It lists the main triggers as:

- Fatigue
- Anxiety
- Stress
- Exercise
- Caffeine

The exact cause isn't fully understood, but the pattern is clear: it shows up when the body is running on overdrive.

## Why stress makes your eye twitch

When your nervous system is on alert, your whole body gets more "switched on." Muscles hold more tension, nerves fire more easily, and small muscles like the ones around your eyes can start firing on their own. Add short sleep and extra coffee to keep going, and the twitch has everything it needs.

## What keeps the nervous system in fight or flight

A twitch that comes back again and again is often a sign that your body isn't getting a real break. If your nervous system learned that it has to stay ready, it keeps your muscles primed and your nerves on edge, even when you're sitting still.

Often, the stress isn't only your schedule. It's a body that has been holding on for a long time, and the eye twitch is one of the small ways it tells you.

## Signs your eye twitch is stress-related

- It shows up during busy, stressful, or emotional weeks
- It's worse when you're short on sleep
- You're relying on more caffeine to get through the day
- It comes with jaw clenching, tight shoulders, or headaches
- It fades on vacation or after a few good nights of rest

See a doctor if the twitch lasts more than a few weeks, spreads to other parts of your face, makes your eyelid close completely, or comes with drooping, redness, or discharge.
