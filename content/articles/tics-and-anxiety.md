If you have tics, you've probably noticed they get louder when you're anxious. And if you're anxious, you may have noticed your tics give you one more thing to worry about.

You're not imagining that connection. Anxiety and tics often travel together, and they can feed each other.

## Anxiety turns up the urge

Anxiety is your nervous system on alert. Your muscles tighten, your breathing gets shallow, and your body gets ready for something to go wrong.

For many people with tics, that alert state makes the urge before a tic stronger and harder to hold back. So when anxiety rises, tics often rise with it.

## Tics can create their own anxiety

It works the other way too. Worrying about when a tic will happen, who will notice, or what people will think keeps your body braced.

Over time, that can become a loop:

- You worry about ticcing in front of people
- Your body tenses and stays on guard
- The urge gets stronger
- The tic happens, or you work hard to hold it in
- You feel embarrassed or drained
- You worry even more about next time

## Anxiety isn't the cause, but it matters

Tics have a neurological side, and anxiety doesn't cause tic disorders on its own. But anxiety is one of the biggest things that can make tics more frequent and more intense.

That's good news in a way. It means calming the nervous system can matter, even when the tics themselves have other roots too. You can read more about [how the nervous system and tics are connected](/articles/tics-and-the-nervous-system).

## Signs anxiety is driving your flare-ups

- Your tics spike before events, meetings, or social plans
- They get worse when you're worried about something specific
- They ease when you're relaxed or absorbed in something you love
- You notice tension in your body right before the urge builds
- You avoid places or people because of how you might react

If anxiety or tics are getting in the way of daily life, talk with your doctor. There are treatments that help with both.
