Can anxiety cause dry mouth? Yes. Your salivary glands are run by your autonomic nervous system. The rest-and-digest side tells them to make plenty of watery saliva. When fight-or-flight takes over, saliva flow drops and turns thicker, so your mouth feels dry and sticky. That's why your mouth goes dry before a big speech or during a panic attack. Anxious mouth-breathing makes it worse.

You might notice a dry tongue, trouble swallowing, a sticky feeling, or needing water constantly when you're stressed.

## See a doctor or dentist if…

Dry mouth that lasts can come from medications (including many antidepressants and anxiety medications), diabetes, dehydration, Sjögren's syndrome, and other conditions. Ongoing dry mouth also raises your risk of cavities and gum problems. Please see a doctor or dentist if it doesn't go away, or if it comes with dry eyes, joint pain, extreme thirst, or frequent urination.

## How your nervous system controls saliva

Saliva is part of digestion, and digestion belongs to rest-and-digest. When your body feels safe, the parasympathetic nerves keep saliva flowing. When your body senses danger, the sympathetic side shifts energy toward your muscles and away from digestion, and your mouth gets drier.

That's useful in a real emergency. But if your nervous system stays on alert, dry mouth can become an everyday thing.

## What research shows

A 2023 study of young adults measured dry mouth and saliva flow alongside stress, anxiety, and depression. It found that these psychological factors significantly affected dry mouth and were linked to lower saliva flow. It was a single study, but it matches what many people notice.

## Signs anxiety may be behind your dry mouth

- It comes on before or during stressful moments
- It's worse during panic or when you're nervous about speaking
- You breathe through your mouth when anxious
- It eases when you're relaxed

## Common questions

**Why does my mouth get dry when I'm nervous?** Fight-or-flight turns down saliva so your body can focus on getting ready to act.

**Can anxiety medication cause dry mouth?** Yes, many do. Don't stop medication on your own, but ask your doctor about options if it bothers you.

**What helps dry mouth from anxiety?** Sip water, chew sugar-free gum, breathe through your nose, and slow your exhale.
