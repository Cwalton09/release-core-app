The burning in your chest. The sour taste in your throat. Lying awake because it gets worse when you lie down. You've cut out coffee, spicy food, and late dinners, and some weeks it's still bad.

If your heartburn seems to flare when life gets hard, there may be a reason.

## What acid reflux is

Acid reflux happens when stomach acid flows back up into the esophagus, the tube that connects your throat to your stomach. When it happens often, it's called GERD (gastroesophageal reflux disease). It can cause heartburn, chest discomfort, a sour taste, coughing, or trouble swallowing.

Food, weight, pregnancy, smoking, and a hiatal hernia can all play a part. So can your nervous system.

## What the research found

**Major stress predicted worse heartburn.** In a 2004 UCLA study, researchers followed 60 people with heartburn. People who had gone through a **severe, ongoing stressor** in the previous six months had **worse heartburn over the following four months.**

Interestingly, everyday ups and downs and mood swings didn't predict it as strongly. It was the big, ongoing kind of stress that wore the body down.

**Exhaustion mattered too.** Feeling drained, worn out, and depleted, what researchers called "vital exhaustion," was strongly linked to heartburn symptoms on its own.

**Stress can make your esophagus more sensitive.** The researchers pointed to a few ways stress may do this: more acid reaching the esophagus, a stomach that empties more slowly, and a nervous system that makes the same amount of acid feel more painful. Other research has found that stress can make people with GERD feel acid more intensely.

## What this means

When your nervous system is in fight or flight, digestion slows down. Your stomach can empty more slowly, which gives acid more chances to come back up. And a nervous system on alert turns up pain signals everywhere, including your chest.

So the burning is real. But for some people, the stress their body is carrying is turning the volume up.

## Signs your nervous system may be part of your reflux

- Your heartburn gets worse during hard seasons, even when your diet doesn't change
- It flares at night when your mind is racing
- You feel tightness in your chest or throat when you're anxious
- You feel exhausted and depleted most of the time
- Your reflux eases on vacation or during calm stretches

See your doctor for ongoing heartburn. Trouble swallowing, vomiting, weight loss, or black stools need medical care. And chest pain can be a heart problem: if you have chest pain with shortness of breath, sweating, or pain spreading to your arm or jaw, call 911.
