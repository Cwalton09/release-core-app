You can know you are safe. You can know the argument is over, the person is gone, the deadline is handled, the danger has passed... and your body can still react like it's happening right now.

Your chest tightens. Your stomach drops. Your thoughts start racing. Or the opposite happens, and you go numb, foggy, and far away.

If that has ever happened to you, you are not broken, and you are not doing it wrong. There's a reason your body keeps reacting, and it usually makes a lot more sense than it feels like it does.

## Knowing and feeling are two different things

The part of you that knows you're safe is the thinking part. It can look at the facts, weigh them, and come to a conclusion.

Your nervous system doesn't work that way. It isn't asking "what are the facts?" It's asking "have I seen something like this before, and what happened last time?"

So when something today looks, sounds, or feels like something from before, your body can respond to the old experience instead of the current one. Your mind says "this is fine." Your body says "I remember this."

## Your body learned this for a reason

Most of these reactions started as protection.

At some point, your nervous system learned that a certain kind of moment wasn't safe. Maybe a raised voice meant something bad was coming. Maybe being seen meant being criticized. Maybe slowing down meant falling behind. Maybe needing something meant being disappointed.

Your body learned the lesson, and it learned it well. It's still running that lesson today, even if your life looks completely different now.

That's why you can't just think your way out of it. You're not dealing with a thought. You're dealing with something your body believes.

## Signs your body is reacting to the past

- Your reaction feels much bigger than the situation in front of you
- You know it doesn't make sense, but you can't talk yourself out of it
- The same kinds of situations set you off again and again
- You feel it in your body first, before you even have a thought about it
- You calm down, then it comes right back the next time

## Why calming techniques only go so far

Breathing, grounding, and stepping away can help a lot in the moment. I use them too.

But if your nervous system is still holding the original lesson, it will keep sounding the alarm every time something reminds it. You end up managing the same reaction over and over without ever changing what's driving it.

## What actually helps

Instead of fighting the reaction or deciding what it means from the outside, it helps to get curious about it.

What is your body responding to right now? What does this moment mean to it? When did it first learn that this kind of moment wasn't safe? And what did it decide it had to do to protect you?
