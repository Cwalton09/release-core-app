Why do you get panic attacks at night? Nighttime, or nocturnal, panic attacks can wake you from sleep with a racing heart, shaking, sweating, shortness of breath, and intense fear, often without a nightmare. They happen when your nervous system sets off its alarm during sleep. A nervous system that's been on guard during the day doesn't automatically switch off at night, and the quiet and darkness can make it feel even more exposed.

You're asleep, and then suddenly you're bolt upright, heart pounding, sure something is terribly wrong. Then you're afraid to go back to sleep.

## Rule out other causes

Please talk to your doctor, especially if this is new. Nighttime symptoms can also come from sleep apnea, heart rhythm problems, acid reflux, low blood sugar, thyroid issues, medications, or alcohol wearing off. Call 911 for chest pain, fainting, or trouble breathing that doesn't settle.

## Why panic happens during sleep

Your nervous system keeps working while you sleep. It still scans for danger, and it still reacts to changes in your body, like a shift in breathing or heart rate as you move between sleep stages.

If your nervous system is already on high alert, a small internal change can be read as a threat, and it sets off the full alarm: adrenaline, racing heart, fast breathing, fear. You wake up in the middle of it.

## Why night can feel scarier

- There's nothing to distract you
- You're alone with your thoughts
- Your body is still, so you notice every sensation
- Stress you pushed through all day finally catches up
- Fear of another attack keeps you on guard as you try to sleep

That last one can turn into a loop: fear of panic keeps your nervous system alert, which makes another attack more likely. ## What a nighttime panic attack can feel like

- Waking suddenly with intense fear
- Racing or pounding heart
- Sweating, shaking, or chills
- Shortness of breath or a choking feeling
- Feeling detached or unreal
- It peaks within minutes and slowly eases

## Common questions

**Can you have a panic attack in your sleep?** Yes. Nocturnal panic attacks wake people from sleep, often without a dream.

**Why do I wake up with a feeling of doom?** A surge of stress hormones during sleep can create a sense of fear before your mind has any thought to attach it to.

**How do I stop panic attacks at night?** Start with a doctor's visit to rule out other causes. Calming the nervous system underneath is what helps the alarm stop going off.

## Why it keeps coming back

When the alarm keeps going off at night, your nervous system still doesn't feel safe enough to fully rest.

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the job, the bills, or the obvious stress in front of you. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *it isn't safe to let my guard down* or *something bad happens when I'm not watching*, and today's stress is just turning up its volume.
