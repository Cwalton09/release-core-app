Why do you feel disconnected from reality or your body? That foggy, dreamlike, "this isn't real" feeling is called derealization. Feeling detached from your own body or thoughts is called depersonalization. Both are protective responses of your nervous system. When something feels like too much, your brain can turn down your connection to the world or your body to help you get through it. They're very common with anxiety, panic, stress, and exhaustion.

It can be one of the scariest feelings there is. The world looks flat or far away. You feel like you're behind glass, or watching yourself in a movie. And you worry you're losing your mind. You're not.

## This is a protective response

Your nervous system has several ways to protect you. Fight or flight speeds you up. But when something feels overwhelming and there's no way to fight it or escape, your brain can create distance instead. It dims your sense of the outside world or of your own body, so the feeling of threat is softer.

It's related to the freeze and shutdown responses. See [why do I shut down when overwhelmed?](/articles/why-do-i-shut-down-when-overwhelmed)

## What it can feel like

- The world feels foggy, flat, dreamlike, or unreal
- Things look far away or strangely bright
- You feel like you're watching yourself from outside
- Your body or voice doesn't feel like your own
- Emotions feel muted (see [why do I feel emotionally numb?](/articles/why-do-i-feel-emotionally-numb))
- You feel disconnected from people you love

## Why it shows up with anxiety

Brief episodes of feeling unreal are common, especially during panic attacks, high stress, lack of sleep, or after something overwhelming. Then a loop can start: the feeling is frightening, the fear raises anxiety, and more anxiety brings more disconnection.

The more you check whether you feel real, the more you notice it. A nervous system on alert watches every sensation.

## When to get help

Please talk with a doctor or mental health professional if the feeling is frequent, lasts a long time, or interferes with your life. Ongoing derealization or depersonalization can be a condition of its own, and it's treatable. Also get checked if it came on suddenly with other symptoms, after a head injury, or with substance use.

If you're thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988.

## Common questions

**Is derealization dangerous?** It feels frightening, but it isn't dangerous in itself. It's a protective response of the nervous system.

**Can anxiety cause derealization?** Yes. Anxiety and panic are among the most common causes.

**Will I lose touch with reality?** People with derealization know the feeling isn't right. That awareness is a sign you're not losing touch with reality.

## Why it keeps coming back

When the disconnection keeps returning, your nervous system is still finding life too much and pulling back to protect you.

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the job, the bills, or the obvious stress in front of you. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *when it's too much, disappear* or *it's not safe to feel*, and today's stress is just turning up its volume.
