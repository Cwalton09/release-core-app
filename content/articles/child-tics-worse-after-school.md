Your child's teacher says they barely noticed any tics. But the minute your child walks in the door, the blinking, sniffing, or throat clearing seems to explode.

It's confusing, and it can leave you wondering if something about home is making it worse. Usually, it's the opposite.

## Kids often hold tics in at school

School is full of pressure: rules, sitting still, classmates, and the fear of being noticed or teased. Many kids work hard to hold their tics in all day so they won't stand out.

Holding them in takes a lot of energy. The urge doesn't go away. It builds.

## Home is where they feel safe enough to let go

When your child gets home, their nervous system can finally relax its guard. Everything they held back comes out.

So a big after-school flare-up often means your child feels safe with you. Their body trusts home enough to stop holding on.

## Other reasons tics spike after school

- They're tired from a long day of focusing and following rules
- They're hungry or overstimulated
- Something stressful happened that they haven't talked about yet
- They're dreading homework, a test, or the next day
- They're excited to be home, and excitement can trigger tics too

## Some kids tic more at school

For other kids, school is where tics are loudest. A hard class, a difficult friendship, teasing, or test pressure can keep their nervous system on alert all day. If you notice tics spiking around certain days or situations, that's useful information. You can read more about [how stress affects tics](/articles/tics-and-the-nervous-system).

## How to support your child

- Try not to point out or comment on the tics
- Give them a calm, low-pressure space after school before homework
- Let them rest, eat, and move their body
- Gently ask how their day really felt, not just how it went
- Talk to their school about understanding and accommodations if needed

Talk to your pediatrician if tics are new, changing quickly, painful, or came on suddenly, especially after an illness. They can help you understand what's going on and what support is available.

## Your nervous system matters too

Kids are very tuned in to the adults around them. When you're worried about their tics, your child can often feel it, even if you never say a word.

That doesn't mean you caused anything. It means that when your own body feels calmer, your child has a steadier place to land.
