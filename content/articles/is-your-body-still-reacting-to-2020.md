Is your body still reacting to 2020? For a lot of people, yes. The pandemic years brought fear, isolation, uncertainty, and pressure that went on for months, and your nervous system learned from all of it. It may still be running the rules it picked up then: stay alert, don't relax, something bad could happen any moment. Your nervous system doesn't check the date. If it was never shown that the danger passed, it can keep reacting as if it's still 2020.

Maybe you noticed it started around then. The anxiety, the trouble sleeping, the stomach issues, the exhaustion, the feeling of always waiting for the next bad thing. Life looks completely different now. But your body still feels the same.

## 2020 was a long, collective stress

The pandemic wasn't one hard day. It was years of uncertainty all at once: health fears, lockdowns, losing people, job and money stress, kids home, news running constantly, not knowing what came next.

The World Health Organization reported that anxiety and depression rose by about 25% worldwide in the first year of the pandemic.

And it didn't simply end. The American Psychological Association's *Stress in America 2023* report described the country as carrying collective trauma from the pandemic years. Adults ages 35 to 44 had the biggest jump in chronic health conditions, from 48% in 2019 to 58% in 2023, and in mental health diagnoses, from 31% to 45%.

## Your nervous system doesn't check the date

Your nervous system learns from experience, and much of what it learns is stored as feelings and reactions, not as memories with dates attached. Researchers call this [implicit memory](/articles/what-is-implicit-memory).

So when your body learned during 2020 that:

- The world isn't safe
- Something bad could happen at any moment
- You have to stay on guard to protect your family
- You can't count on anything staying the same
- You have to hold everything together

…it didn't file that under "2020." It filed it under "how life works." And it may still be following those rules today, even though your life has changed.

That's why you can know the crisis is over and still feel your chest tighten, your stomach knot, or your mind race for no clear reason. Your body is reacting to a time it hasn't been told is over. More on that in [why does my body still react when I know I'm safe?](/articles/why-body-reacts-when-you-know-you-are-safe)

## Signs your body may still be in 2020 mode

- Anxiety, sleep problems, or health issues that started around 2020 and never fully left
- Feeling like you're always waiting for the next bad thing
- Trouble relaxing, even on calm days
- Getting tense around crowds, coughing, or health news
- Feeling like you never fully "came back" to your old self
- Constantly checking, planning, or bracing

Many of the symptoms people developed in those years, like [brain fog](/articles/brain-fog-nervous-system), [exhaustion](/articles/why-am-i-always-tired-nervous-system), and [anxiety for no reason](/articles/why-do-i-feel-anxious-for-no-reason), are signs of a nervous system that never stood down.

If you had COVID and still have lingering symptoms, please talk to your doctor. Long COVID is a real medical condition that deserves care.

## Why it hasn't updated on its own

Your nervous system updates its rules through experience, not just through time passing or through logic. Knowing "it's over" is information. Your body needs to actually feel that it's safe now, in the place where the old rule is stored.

Without that, the rule just keeps running in the background, sometimes for years.

## Common questions

**Can stress from the pandemic still affect me now?** Yes. Long periods of stress can leave the nervous system stuck in protection mode long after the stressful time ends.

**Why did my anxiety start in 2020?** For many people, the pandemic was a prolonged threat with no clear end, which is exactly the kind of experience that teaches the nervous system to stay on alert.

**Is collective trauma real?** The APA used the term to describe how the pandemic affected people's mental and physical health across the country.
