If you've had your MTHFR variant your whole life, why did the symptoms only start recently? Your genes haven't changed since the day you were born. What usually changes is the load your body is carrying. Long stretches of stress, pressure, and uncertainty can push your nervous system into a protective state that brings on the same anxiety, fatigue, and brain fog that often get blamed on MTHFR.

So many people tell the same story. Childhood was fine. Your twenties were fine. You ate whatever you wanted and never thought about vitamins. Then, somewhere in the last few years, everything started to feel harder. A test came back showing MTHFR, and it seemed like the answer.

But if you've had the gene all along, it's worth asking what else changed.

## Your gene is the same one you were born with

MTHFR variants are inherited. You've had yours since birth, and it doesn't switch on at 30 or 40.

They're also extremely common. The CDC says more people in the United States have one or two copies of the most common variant, C677T, than don't have it at all. Most of those people never have symptoms because of it. You can read more about what the gene does in [MTHFR, anxiety, and depression](/articles/mthfr-anxiety-depression).

So if you felt fine for decades with the same gene, the gene alone is probably not what tipped you over.

## What did change: the load on your nervous system

For a lot of adults, the last several years have been heavy. A pandemic. Isolation. Constant news and social media. A tight economy. More pressure at work and at home, and less time to recover.

The American Psychological Association's **Stress in America 2023** survey found that adults ages 35 to 44 had the biggest jump in chronic health conditions since before the pandemic: **58% in 2023, up from 48% in 2019**. Their mental health diagnoses rose from **31% to 45%**. That same age group was more likely than in 2019 to name **money (77%)** and **the economy (74%)** as major sources of stress.

That survey shows a pattern over time, not proof of cause. But it lines up with what so many people with MTHFR describe: the symptoms started when life got harder, not when the gene arrived.

## Stress affects the same things people blame on MTHFR

When your nervous system stays on guard for a long time, it can affect:

- **Mood:** more anxiety, irritability, or low moods
- **Energy:** feeling wired and exhausted at the same time
- **Thinking:** brain fog, forgetfulness, trouble focusing
- **Digestion:** stomach trouble that can affect how you feel after eating and how well you absorb nutrients
- **Sleep:** trouble falling or staying asleep

Stress can even raise homocysteine, the blood marker most linked to MTHFR. In a study of women, plasma homocysteine rose during a stressful task and came back down afterward. More on that in [can stress raise homocysteine?](/articles/can-stress-raise-homocysteine)

This doesn't mean MTHFR never matters. It means a body under constant stress can look a lot like a "methylation problem," even when the gene isn't the main driver.

## Signs the timing points to stress

- Your symptoms started or got worse during a hard season of life
- You feel noticeably better on vacation or after a weekend of real rest
- Methylated vitamins helped a little, or not at all
- Your labs look mostly normal, but you still feel off
- You feel wired and tired at the same time

Always talk with your doctor before changing medications or supplements. If you have symptoms like numbness, tingling, or anemia, or you're pregnant or planning to be, ask about B12, folate, and homocysteine testing.
