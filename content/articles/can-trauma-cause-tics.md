Can trauma cause tics? Trauma doesn't cause Tourette syndrome, which is largely genetic and usually starts in childhood. But stress and hard experiences can make tics worse, and a different kind of tic, called functional tic-like behaviors, often starts suddenly during periods of heavy stress, anxiety, or trauma. These are real and involuntary. Doctors have seen a sharp rise in them in teens and young adults in recent years.

You might notice tics started suddenly after a frightening event, a loss, or a long stretch of stress, or that existing tics flare during hard times.

## See a doctor

New tics, or tics that change suddenly, deserve a doctor's visit, ideally with a neurologist or movement disorder specialist. They can tell the difference between Tourette syndrome, functional tics, and other conditions. Get emergency care for sudden weakness, trouble speaking, or loss of consciousness.

## What research shows

A 2022 review in *Current Developmental Disorders Reports* describes the rise in sudden-onset tic and tic-like presentations in older teens and adults. Compared with typical Tourette syndrome, these often start suddenly and later in life, and they are often linked with anxiety, depression, and stressful experiences.

Stress is also one of the most commonly reported triggers that make tics worse in people with Tourette syndrome and other tic disorders.

## How stress and trauma affect tics

Tics come from the nervous system's movement circuits. When the nervous system is on high alert, those circuits become more active, and the urge to tic gets stronger. After trauma, the body may stay in that heightened state, so tics flare more easily.

## Signs stress or trauma may be part of the tics

- Tics started suddenly after a hard event or season
- They flare during stress, conflict, or reminders
- They come with anxiety, panic, or poor sleep
- They ease when you feel safe and calm

## Common questions

**Can PTSD cause tics?** Trauma and PTSD can make tics more likely to flare, and functional tics are often linked with stress and trauma.

**Are functional tics real?** Yes. They're involuntary and treatable. The nervous system's wiring is healthy, but the way it's working has changed.

**Do stress tics go away?** Many people improve as stress eases and with specialized treatment.
