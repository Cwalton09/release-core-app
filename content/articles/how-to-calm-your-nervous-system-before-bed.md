You're exhausted all day. Then you finally get into bed, and suddenly you're wide awake.

Your mind starts replaying conversations. Your chest feels tight. Your body feels wired, like it's waiting for something. And the harder you try to sleep, the more awake you get.

Sleep isn't something you can force. It happens when your nervous system feels safe enough to let go. So if bedtime is a struggle, the goal isn't to try harder. It's to help your body feel safe.

## Why your body fights sleep

To fall asleep, your nervous system has to shift out of protection and into rest. If it still thinks it needs to stay alert, it will keep you awake. That's its job.

A few things make this harder at night:

- Everything gets quiet, so whatever you've been pushing down all day finally comes up
- Lying still in the dark can feel vulnerable if your body learned to stay on guard
- Screens, late work, and stressful news keep your system activated
- Your body may be bracing for tomorrow

## A simple wind-down routine

You don't need all of these. Pick two or three and do them most nights. Consistency matters more than perfection.

- Dim the lights an hour before bed. Your body reads darkness as a signal to slow down.
- Put your phone across the room. Scrolling keeps your brain scanning.
- Do a "brain dump." Write down everything on your mind so your body doesn't have to hold it.
- Take a warm shower or bath. The temperature drop afterward helps your body get sleepy.
- Stretch gently, especially your neck, shoulders, jaw, and hips.

## Once you're in bed

- Breathe out slowly, longer than you breathe in. Try in for four, out for six or eight.
- Let your body get heavy. Notice the bed holding you up, from your head down to your feet.
- Put one hand on your chest and one on your belly. Feel them rise and fall.
- If your thoughts race, don't fight them. Just notice: "There's thinking." Then come back to your breath.
- Remind your body, "Right now, in this moment, I'm safe. Nothing needs me."

## Why it keeps coming back

A good wind-down routine can take the edge off in the moment. But if the belief underneath is still there, your body will keep staying on guard at night.

And here's the hard part: you usually can't find that belief by thinking about it. It isn't stored as a thought. It's stored in your body, which is why it can feel so obvious once you find it, and so impossible to see on your own.
