Why do I feel faint when I'm anxious? Feeling faint with anxiety is common, but actually passing out from panic is rare, because panic usually raises your heart rate and blood pressure. What does cause fainting with strong emotion is the vasovagal response. The vagus nerve suddenly slows your heart and widens your blood vessels, blood pressure drops, and less blood reaches your brain. It's often triggered by fear, pain, the sight of blood, needles, or standing for a long time.

You might feel lightheaded, sweaty, nauseous, and warm, with your vision going gray or tunneling, right before you need to sit down.

## Get emergency help if…

Call 911 if fainting happens during exercise, comes with chest pain, a racing or pounding heart, shortness of breath, or a severe headache, or if the person has heart disease, doesn't wake up quickly, or is injured. Any first-time fainting should be checked by a doctor, because heart rhythm problems and other conditions can cause it.

## What happens in a vasovagal faint

The vasovagal response is an overreaction of the nervous system. Something triggers it, like fear, pain, blood, or heat, and the parasympathetic side, through the vagus nerve, suddenly takes over. Your heart rate slows, your blood vessels relax, and your blood pressure drops quickly. Most people wake up within seconds once they're lying flat.

The 2018 European Society of Cardiology guidelines describe vasovagal (reflex) syncope as the most common cause of fainting, often triggered by emotional distress or standing for a long time, with warning signs like sweating, warmth, nausea, and paleness.

## Why anxiety makes you feel faint

During anxiety, fast breathing can blow off carbon dioxide and make you lightheaded and dizzy. That can feel like you're about to faint, even though your blood pressure is fine or high. See [dizzy when anxious](/articles/dizzy-when-anxious).

## What to do when you feel faint

- Lie down and raise your legs, or sit with your head low
- Cross your legs and squeeze your thighs, or clench your fists, to raise blood pressure
- Breathe slowly
- Get up slowly once it passes

## Common questions

**Can you pass out from a panic attack?** It's rare. Panic usually raises blood pressure. Feeling faint during panic is often from fast breathing.

**Why do I faint at the sight of blood?** Blood and needles are common vasovagal triggers. It often runs in families.

**Is vasovagal syncope dangerous?** It's usually harmless, but falls can cause injury, and other causes of fainting need to be ruled out by a doctor.
