Can stress cause hair loss? Yes. A big stressor can push many hairs into their resting phase at once. Then, usually about two to four months later, those hairs fall out together. Doctors call this telogen effluvium. It's one of the most common kinds of hair loss, and because of the delay, most people don't connect it to the stress that caused it.

You notice more hair in the shower, on your brush, on your pillow. And you're not sure why, because life has calmed down.

## Why hair loss shows up months later

Every hair goes through cycles of growing, resting, and shedding. Normally only a small share of your hair is resting at any time.

A major stressor, like illness, surgery, childbirth, grief, a breakup, or a long, hard season, can shift a lot of hair into the resting phase at the same time. A few months later, those hairs shed together. So the hair loss often appears after the stress is over, which is why it's so confusing.

## What stress hair loss looks like

- More hair than usual in the shower or on your brush
- Thinning all over, rather than in one patch
- Starting a few months after something stressful
- Usually temporary, with regrowth over several months

## What to check with your doctor

Hair loss has many possible causes, so it's worth getting checked, especially if it's patchy, getting worse, or not improving. Ask about:

- Iron and ferritin levels
- Thyroid function
- Vitamin D and B12
- Hormone changes, including after pregnancy

## Ongoing stress keeps the cycle going

For many people, the shedding settles once the stress passes. But if your nervous system stays on high alert, ongoing stress can keep affecting your body, from sleep and hormones to how you eat and absorb nutrients. ## Common questions

**How long after stress does hair fall out?** Usually about two to four months after the stressful event.

**Does stress hair loss grow back?** Most of the time, yes. Regrowth usually happens over six to nine months once the trigger settles.

**Can anxiety make your hair fall out?** Ongoing anxiety and stress can contribute. A doctor can help rule out other causes.
