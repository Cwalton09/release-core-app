Why does your body jerk when you fall asleep? It's usually a hypnic jerk, also called a sleep start: a sudden, harmless muscle twitch that happens as your body moves from awake to asleep. Most people have them at some point. Experts believe stress, anxiety, fatigue, caffeine, and not enough sleep may make them happen more often, though the research isn't conclusive. When your nervous system is still on alert at bedtime, the handoff into sleep can be bumpier.

You're drifting off, and suddenly your whole body jolts. Sometimes it comes with a feeling of falling or tripping, and your heart races for a moment.

## What a hypnic jerk is

As you fall asleep, your brain and body shift gears. One leading theory is that nerves "misfire" during that transition, causing a quick muscle jerk. Some people also feel a falling sensation, see a flash, or hear a sound.

Hypnic jerks are very common. Estimates suggest around 60 to 70 percent of people experience them. A sleep specialist at the Cleveland Clinic has said that most of the time, they're completely normal.

## Why stress may make them worse

Stress and anxiety keep your nervous system on guard. If your body hasn't fully settled when you lie down, the shift into sleep may be less smooth. That can mean more jolts.

There's also a loop that can form. The jerks startle you, you start worrying about them, and that worry makes it harder to relax into sleep. ## Things that may make them more frequent

- Stress and anxiety
- Caffeine or nicotine, especially later in the day
- Not getting enough sleep
- Intense exercise in the evening
- An irregular sleep schedule

## When to see a doctor

Hypnic jerks are usually harmless. Please talk with a doctor or sleep specialist if they're keeping you from sleeping, happen many times a night, happen during the day, or come with other symptoms like jerking while you're awake, confusion, injuries, or wetting the bed. If your muscles twitch a lot during the day too, see [muscle twitching and anxiety](/articles/muscle-twitching-anxiety).

## Common questions

**Why do I jerk awake right as I fall asleep?** That's a hypnic jerk, a common twitch as your body moves into sleep.

**Can anxiety cause hypnic jerks?** Experts believe stress and anxiety may make them more frequent, though research is limited.

**Why do I feel like I'm falling when I fall asleep?** The falling feeling often comes with a hypnic jerk as your brain shifts into sleep.

## Why it keeps coming back

If your body keeps jolting at bedtime, your nervous system may still be on alert when it's time to let go.
