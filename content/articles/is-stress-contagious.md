Have you ever walked into a room and instantly felt tense, before anyone said a word? Or noticed that when someone close to you is stressed, your body starts to feel it too?

That's not your imagination. Research suggests stress and even pain can spread from one body to another.

## The mice who felt pain they didn't have

In a 2016 study from Oregon Health & Science University, researchers studied mice that were in pain or going through withdrawal from alcohol or morphine. They also housed healthy "bystander" mice in the same room.

The bystander mice had never been hurt and never had alcohol or morphine. But they developed the same increased sensitivity to pain as the mice who were suffering.

The researchers traced it to smell. When healthy mice were given bedding used by the mice in pain, they became more sensitive to pain too. A later study from the same group found that the bystander mice showed activity in some of the same pain-related brain areas as the mice who were actually in pain.

In other words, their nervous systems picked up on something in their environment and responded as if it were happening to them.

## It happens in people too

Humans aren't mice, and we don't know whether smell plays the same role for us. But human research has found something similar with stress.

In a 2014 study led by Veronika Engert, people simply watched someone else go through a stressful test. About a quarter of the observers showed a significant rise in the stress hormone cortisol, even though nothing stressful was happening to them. The effect was strongest between romantic partners, at 40 percent, but it happened even between strangers.

Your nervous system is always reading the people around you. Their tone, their face, their tension. And it can respond as if their stress is yours.

## Carrying stress that started with someone else

This helps explain why so many people carry stress that didn't begin with them:

- A child who grew up around an anxious or angry parent
- Someone who lives with a partner under constant pressure
- Caregivers, nurses, and helpers who absorb other people's pain
- Empaths and highly sensitive people who feel everything in the room
