Why is my anxiety worse at night? For many people, nighttime is the first quiet moment of the day. The distractions stop, and everything your body pushed aside comes up at once. Research on insomnia shows that worry at bedtime switches on your body's alert system, and that alert state keeps you watching for problems instead of drifting off.

You might feel fine all day, then notice racing thoughts, a tight chest, or dread as soon as you get into bed.

## When to get help

If anxiety at night comes with chest pain, trouble breathing, or fainting, get medical care right away. If you've had trouble sleeping most nights for three months or more, talk to your doctor. Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is a proven treatment. If you're in crisis, call or text 988.

## What research shows

A well-known model of insomnia from researcher Allison Harvey describes a loop. People who struggle at night tend to worry about sleep and about how they'll feel tomorrow. That worry triggers physical arousal and distress. The anxious state then makes you watch for signs that something is wrong, like your heartbeat, a noise, or the clock. Watching for danger keeps you awake, which gives you more to worry about.

## Why nighttime makes it worse

- **No more distractions.** Busy days keep feelings in the background. Quiet nights bring them forward.
- **Lying still in the dark.** If your body learned to stay on guard, being still and vulnerable can feel unsafe.
- **You're tired.** A tired brain has a harder time calming worry down.
- **Clock-watching.** Checking the time turns sleep into a test you're failing.

## Common questions

**Why do my thoughts race when I lie down?** Bedtime is often the first quiet moment of the day, so the thoughts you pushed aside come up all at once.

**Is nighttime anxiety normal?** It's very common. If it keeps you from sleeping most nights, talk to your doctor.

**Should I get out of bed if I can't sleep?** Sleep experts often recommend getting up for a while and doing something calm in dim light, then coming back when you feel sleepy.

## Why it keeps coming back

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't only today's stress. It's an old rule your body learned long ago, like *I have to stay alert* or *bad things happen when I let my guard down*, and night is when that rule gets loud.
