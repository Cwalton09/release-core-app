You stand up and your heart races. You get dizzy and foggy. Then, out of nowhere, your face flushes, your skin itches, or your stomach turns after a meal.

Somewhere along the way, someone mentioned POTS. Then someone else mentioned MCAS. Now you're wondering if they're connected, or if your body is just falling apart.

You're not imagining the overlap. But the research is more complicated than social media makes it sound.

## Quick refresher

**POTS** (postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome) is a disorder of the autonomic nervous system. When you stand up, your heart rate jumps far more than it should.

**MCAS** (mast cell activation syndrome) is when immune cells called mast cells release chemicals like histamine too easily, causing flushing, itching, hives, stomach trouble, and more.

## What the research found

**A study of people with both.** In 2005, researchers at Vanderbilt University published a study in the journal *Hypertension* on people with POTS who also had signs of mast cell activation.

Their episodes included flushing, shortness of breath, headaches, lightheadedness, frequent urination, and stomach symptoms like nausea and diarrhea. Common triggers included:

- Standing for a long time
- Exercise
- The days before a period
- Meals
- Sex

These patients had a "hyperadrenergic" pattern: their stress-hormone side of the nervous system was running hot. When they stood up, their blood pressure went up, not just their heart rate. The researchers recommended that doctors consider mast cell activation in POTS patients who flush, and noted that some common POTS medications may need extra caution in this group.

**A review that urges caution.** In 2020, researchers published a review in *Clinical Reviews in Allergy & Immunology* looking at the claimed link between POTS, MCAS, and hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (hEDS). They found that many studies proposing a connection were biased or used outdated criteria. They suggested part of the overlap may come from shared symptoms that are hard to measure, like fatigue and brain fog.

A 2025 clinical update from the American Gastroenterological Association took a middle position: doctors should be aware the conditions are often seen together, but the biology behind it is still being worked out.

## Where the nervous system fits in

What both conditions share is a body that's reacting as if it's under threat.

In POTS, the "fight or flight" side of the nervous system overreacts to standing up. In MCAS, mast cells overreact to triggers. And research shows mast cells respond to stress hormones like corticotropin-releasing hormone, the same chemistry your nervous system uses when it senses danger.

So when your nervous system stays on alert, both systems may have less room to settle.

## Signs your nervous system may be part of it

- Your heart races and you flush in the same episodes
- Symptoms flare during stressful weeks or after poor sleep
- You react more around your period or after big meals
- You feel wired and exhausted at the same time
- You've been told it's "just anxiety," but it feels very physical

POTS and MCAS both need proper medical diagnosis and care. If you faint, have chest pain, trouble breathing, or swelling of your face or throat, get emergency help right away.
