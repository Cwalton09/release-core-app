You've probably noticed it. On the days you're stressed and stretched thin, your kids seem to fall apart more. On the days you feel calm, they seem calmer too.

That's not your imagination. Young children are deeply tuned in to the nervous systems of the adults around them.

## Kids borrow your calm

Babies and young children can't calm themselves down yet. Their nervous systems are still developing. So they borrow regulation from the adults they're close to.

This is called co-regulation. When a calmer adult is nearby, it helps a child's body settle during stress. Over time, children slowly learn to do it on their own.

Research tracking mothers and preschoolers with heart and breathing monitors found that a parent's influence on a child's stress physiology naturally fades as kids learn to self-regulate. But in homes with harsh discipline like shouting or spanking, that influence didn't fade the same way, and children took longer to calm down after being upset.

## How kids pick up on your nervous system

Children read your tone of voice, your face, your body, and your breathing, often without you saying a word. A tight jaw, a sharp tone, or a rushed pace can tell a child's body that something isn't safe, even if you're telling them everything is fine.

## This is not about blame

If you're reading this and feeling guilty, please pause. This isn't about being a perfect parent. No parent is calm all the time, and kids don't need you to be.

What matters is that your child's nervous system learns that hard moments can end, and that connection comes back. Repair matters more than perfection.

## Your nervous system has a history too

Many parents notice they react most strongly to the exact behaviors that were never allowed when they were kids: crying, talking back, being loud, needing too much. That's often a sign that your own nervous system learned something a long time ago, and your child's behavior is touching it.

When your own body feels safer, you become a steadier place for your child to land. You can read more about [your child's behavior as a nervous system response](/articles/child-behavior-nervous-system-response).
