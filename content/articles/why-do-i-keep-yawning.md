Why do you keep yawning when you're not tired? Yawning isn't only about sleep. It's also linked to breathing, alertness, and your nervous system. Many people with anxiety yawn a lot, often because they're breathing shallowly and their body is trying to get a full, satisfying breath. Yawning may also help cool the brain and shift your state. Excessive yawning can have medical causes too, so it's worth mentioning to your doctor.

You yawn back to back, in the middle of a conversation, while working out, or when you're wide awake. It can feel like you just can't get a full breath.

## When to see a doctor

Please talk to your doctor if excessive yawning is new, constant, or comes with fatigue that doesn't improve with sleep, dizziness, fainting, or other symptoms. It can be linked to sleep problems, medication side effects, and some heart and nerve conditions.

## The "can't get a full breath" yawn

A lot of people describe it the same way: they try to take a deep breath, and it doesn't feel complete until they yawn. That's very common with anxiety.

When your nervous system is on alert, breathing tends to get fast and shallow, high in the chest. Your body can feel like it isn't getting enough air, even when your oxygen is fine. A yawn forces one big, deep breath. You can read more in [why can't I take a deep breath?](/articles/why-cant-i-take-a-deep-breath)

## Yawning and your nervous system

Researchers are still studying exactly why we yawn. Some leading ideas:

- **Brain cooling:** One theory, from researcher Andrew Gallup, is that yawning helps cool the brain by bringing in a big rush of air and blood flow.
- **State shifting:** Yawning often happens during transitions, like waking up, getting ready to focus, or coming down from stress.
- **The vagus nerve:** Yawning involves the same nerves and muscles that help regulate your stress response, which may be why some people yawn as their body tries to calm down. See [what is the vagus nerve?](/articles/what-is-the-vagus-nerve)

## Signs your yawning is stress-related

- It happens more when you're anxious or stressed
- You feel like you can't get a satisfying breath
- You breathe fast or high in your chest
- It eases when you feel calm
- You're not actually sleepy

## Common questions

**Can anxiety cause excessive yawning?** Yes. Many people with anxiety yawn a lot, often linked to shallow breathing.

**Why do I yawn when I'm nervous?** Yawning may help your body shift state and take a deep breath when your nervous system is revved up.

**Why do I keep yawning while exercising?** Breathing changes and the brain-cooling effect may play a role. If it comes with dizziness or chest pain, stop and see a doctor.
