All day, you were mostly okay. Then evening comes, you finally sit down, or you get into bed, and the tics get louder.

It can feel unfair. The moment you want to rest is the moment your body won't settle. And if you share a bed or a room, you might also worry about bothering someone, which only adds to it.

There are real reasons tics tend to show up more in the evening.

## You may have been holding them in all day

Many people quietly hold back their tics at work, at school, or in public. Holding them in takes effort. The urge doesn't disappear, it just waits.

When you get home and feel safe enough to stop holding, everything you held back can come out at once. That's why tics often look worse in the evening, even though the day was when the pressure was highest.

## Tired bodies tic more

Exhaustion is one of the most common tic triggers. By evening, your energy is lower, your patience is thinner, and your nervous system has less in reserve to keep the urge contained.

A long, demanding day can show up as a noisy night.

## Quiet makes you notice your body

During the day, your attention is pulled in a hundred directions. At night, it's just you and your body.

Lying still in a quiet room, you notice every sensation, every bit of tension, every urge. And the more you notice the urge, the stronger it can feel.

## The day catches up with you

Night is often when your nervous system starts processing everything it held through the day: the stress, the conversations, the things you didn't have time to feel.

If your body is still on alert from the day, or bracing for tomorrow, that activation can come out as tics right when you're trying to wind down. You can read more about how [stress and the nervous system affect tics](/articles/tics-and-the-nervous-system).

## Do tics happen during sleep?

Tics usually quiet down a lot once you're actually asleep, though some people do have them during lighter sleep. If tics are keeping you from falling asleep or waking you up often, it's worth mentioning to your doctor.

## Why it keeps coming back

Most of the time, we don't know the real reason behind what's driving the trigger. We can guess, but the actual trigger is usually something we would never think of on our own. And until it's found, your body will keep carrying the day into the night.
