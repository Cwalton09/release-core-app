Can anxiety cause numbness? Yes, it can. When you're anxious, you often breathe faster or deeper without noticing. That lowers the carbon dioxide in your blood, and the change can cause numbness or tingling in your lips, face, hands, and feet. In one study, 87% of healthy people had tingling or numbness after just five minutes of fast breathing.

You might notice pins and needles in your hands, a numb face or lips, or tingling that comes with a racing heart or panic.

## Get help right away if…

Sudden numbness on one side of your body, face drooping, arm weakness, trouble speaking, confusion, or a severe headache can be a stroke. Call 911 right away. Numbness that doesn't go away, keeps coming back, or happens without anxiety also needs a doctor's checkup.

## What research shows

Researchers had 15 healthy adults breathe fast on purpose for five minutes. 87% developed paresthesia, the medical word for numbness and tingling, and many had small muscle twitches. The symptoms tracked with changes in blood gases, mainly lower carbon dioxide. The researchers concluded these symptoms are a normal result of overbreathing, not a sign of disease on their own.

A review in the *Journal of Psychosomatic Research* also connects breathing patterns with many physical symptoms people feel when stressed.

## How anxiety causes numbness

- **Overbreathing.** Fast or deep breathing lowers carbon dioxide.
- **Nerve changes.** Lower carbon dioxide makes nerves fire more easily, causing tingling.
- **Tension.** Tight muscles can press on nerves.
- **Paying close attention.** Worry makes you notice every sensation.

## Common questions

**Can anxiety cause numbness on one side?** Anxiety usually affects both sides. One-sided numbness needs emergency care to rule out a stroke.

**How long does anxiety numbness last?** Often minutes, easing as your breathing slows.

**What helps anxiety numbness?** Slow your breathing, especially making your exhale longer than your inhale.
