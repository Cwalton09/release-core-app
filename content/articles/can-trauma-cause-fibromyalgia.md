Can trauma cause fibromyalgia? Trauma doesn't cause every case, but research shows a strong link. A 2014 meta-analysis found that people who had been through trauma were about 2.7 times more likely to have conditions like fibromyalgia, chronic widespread pain, and chronic fatigue, and the link was strongest with PTSD. Fibromyalgia involves a nervous system that turns pain signals up, and trauma is one of the things that can keep that system on high alert.

You might ache all over, feel tender to touch, wake up stiff and exhausted, and struggle with fog and poor sleep.

## See a doctor first

Fibromyalgia is diagnosed by a doctor after ruling out other causes, like thyroid problems, autoimmune conditions, and vitamin deficiencies. Please see a doctor for widespread pain, and get checked quickly for joint swelling, fever, weight loss, weakness, or rashes. If you're struggling with trauma memories, a trauma-informed therapist can help. In crisis, call or text 988 in the US.

## What research shows

The 2014 meta-analysis in *Psychosomatic Medicine* combined 71 studies on trauma and conditions like fibromyalgia, chronic widespread pain, chronic fatigue, jaw disorders, and IBS. People who reported trauma were 2.7 times more likely to have one of these conditions. The link with PTSD was stronger than with abuse alone.

## How trauma can turn up pain

Your nervous system decides how loud pain signals are. After trauma, it can stay in protection mode, a state researchers call central sensitization, where normal sensations hurt more and pain spreads. Poor sleep, constant tension, and stress hormones add to it.

## Signs trauma may be part of your pain

- Pain started during or after a hard time
- It flares with stress or reminders
- You also feel on edge, startle easily, or sleep poorly
- Treatments help a little but not fully

## Common questions

**Can PTSD cause fibromyalgia?** PTSD is strongly linked to fibromyalgia in research, though it doesn't cause every case.

**Is fibromyalgia all in your head?** No. The pain is real. It comes from how the nervous system processes pain signals.

**Can fibromyalgia improve?** Many people improve with a mix of gentle movement, sleep care, medical treatment, and calming the nervous system.
