First, the most important part: chest pain or tightness can be a sign of a heart problem. If your chest pain is severe, comes with shortness of breath, fainting, sweating, or pain spreading to your arm, jaw, or back, call 911 or get emergency care right away. Don't assume it's anxiety. And talk to your doctor about any ongoing chest symptoms so they can be checked.

Once heart and lung problems have been ruled out, though, many people find their chest tightness is coming from their nervous system.

## How anxiety shows up in your chest

When your nervous system senses danger, it prepares your body to act. Your heart beats faster. Your breathing gets quicker and higher in your chest. The muscles around your ribs, chest, and shoulders tighten, ready to protect you.

That can feel like pressure, a band around your chest, or not being able to get a full breath.

## The loop that keeps it going

Chest tightness can easily become a loop:

- Your chest feels tight
- You worry something is wrong with your heart
- The worry makes your nervous system more alert
- Your heart speeds up and your breathing gets shallower
- Your chest feels even tighter

Being more aware of your heartbeat can make the anxiety stronger, which makes the sensations stronger.

## Signs it may be your nervous system

- It shows up during stress, worry, or conflict
- It gets worse when you focus on it
- It comes with shallow breathing, a racing heart, or a lump in your throat
- Your doctor has checked your heart and found nothing concerning
- It eases when you feel calm, distracted, or safe

## Your chest may be bracing

For many people, chest tightness is part of an old bracing pattern. The body learned to tighten up and hold its breath to get through something hard, and it still does it whenever something feels similar.
