Why do you push people away? Usually because your nervous system learned that closeness isn't safe. If people you loved hurt you, left, or weren't there when you needed them, your body may have decided that getting close leads to pain. So when someone gets close now, part of you pulls back to protect you, even though another part of you wants them there.

Things are going well, and then you start finding faults. You go quiet. You pick a fight. You end it before they can leave you.

## Pushing away is protection

It can feel like you're sabotaging good things. But your nervous system isn't trying to ruin your life. It's trying to keep you from getting hurt the way you were before.

The logic is simple and old: *if I don't let them close, they can't hurt me. If I leave first, I won't be left.*

## What it can look like

- Pulling away when someone gets emotionally close
- Picking fights or finding flaws when things are going well
- Not answering texts when you feel too seen
- Keeping conversations light so no one really knows you
- Ending relationships before they can end
- Feeling relieved and lonely at the same time

## Where it comes from

Your nervous system learns about closeness from your earliest relationships. If love felt unpredictable, conditional, smothering, or painful, your body may carry rules like:

- *If I need someone, they'll let me down*
- *People who get close will leave*
- *Being seen means being judged*
- *I'm safest on my own*

Those rules made sense once. Now they keep you from the connection you want. ## Why wanting closeness isn't enough

You can know you want love and still pull away the moment it shows up. That's because the pulling away doesn't come from your thinking mind. It comes from your nervous system's sense of danger, and that happens faster than thought. Read more in [I know why I do it, so why can't I stop?](/articles/i-know-why-i-do-it-but-cant-stop)

## Common questions

**Why do I push away people who love me?** Love can feel risky to a nervous system that learned closeness leads to hurt.

**Is pushing people away a trauma response?** It can be. It's often tied to attachment wounds or past relational hurt.

**Why do I feel relieved when I push someone away?** Your body feels safer at a distance, even when your heart feels lonely.

## Why it keeps coming back

When you keep pulling back from people you care about, your nervous system is still following an old rule about closeness.

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't this person or this relationship. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *people who get close will leave* or *I'm safest alone*, and today's closeness is just turning up its volume.
