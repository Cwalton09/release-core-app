You eat well. You take your vitamins, maybe even the expensive methylated ones. You've done the gut tests and the elimination diets. And you still feel tired, foggy, and depleted, like your body isn't getting what you're giving it.

Here's something most people never hear: digestion isn't just about what you eat. It's run by your nervous system.

## Your nervous system runs digestion

In 2014, researchers Kirsteen Browning and Alberto Travagli published a major review in *Comprehensive Physiology* on how the brain and nervous system control the gut.

They described two branches working against each other:

- **The "rest and digest" side** (parasympathetic, largely through the vagus nerve) helps control the stomach and upper gut, including movement and digestive secretions
- **The "fight or flight" side** (sympathetic) mainly **slows down** the muscles of the gut, **holds back** digestive secretions, and **narrows** the blood vessels that supply the gut

From a survival point of view, it makes sense. If you're running from danger, digesting lunch can wait.

## What happens to digestion under stress

Researchers have tested this directly.

**Stomach acid and emptying.** In a 1983 study published in the journal *Gut*, healthy volunteers were put through a stressful cold-pain test. During the stress, their stomachs emptied more slowly, and their stomach acid output dropped. It then rose again after the stress ended.

**Absorption and the gut lining.** A 2005 review in the *Journal of Gastroenterology and Hepatology* reported that stress can reverse how the small intestine absorbs water and electrolytes, and can make the gut lining more permeable to large molecules.

## Why that matters for nutrients

Your body needs a working digestive system to pull nutrients out of food. Vitamin B12 is a good example. It needs stomach acid to be released from food, and a protein made in the stomach lining, called intrinsic factor, to be absorbed in the small intestine.

Most of the stress research looks at short-term stress, so scientists can't yet say exactly how much long-term stress lowers nutrient levels. But the direction is clear: a body stuck in fight or flight isn't set up to digest well.

## When supplements don't seem to work

If your nervous system has been on alert for months or years, it may be spending its energy on protection instead of digestion. You can add more nutrients, but a body that's still bracing may not be in the state it needs to fully use them.

That's why supplements alone sometimes don't seem to touch how you feel.

## Signs your nervous system may be affecting digestion

- Your stomach reacts during stress, before you even feel stressed
- You lose your appetite when you're anxious, or crave sugar and caffeine
- Bloating, nausea, or a heavy stomach after meals
- You feel depleted even with a good diet and supplements
- Your digestion improves on vacation

Ongoing digestive symptoms, low B12, anemia, and other deficiencies need a doctor's care. Talk to your doctor before changing medications or supplements.
