Why do you eat when you're stressed? Stress changes your body's chemistry and your appetite. Stress hormones like cortisol can increase cravings, especially for sweet and high-fat foods, and those foods can briefly calm the stress response. In one study, women whose bodies released more cortisol under stress ate more afterward and chose sweeter foods. So stress eating isn't a lack of willpower. It's your nervous system looking for relief.

A hard day, and suddenly you're in the pantry. You're not even hungry. You just need something.

## What research shows

In a 2001 study led by Elissa Epel, 59 women went through a stressful session and a calm session on different days. The women whose bodies released the most cortisol during stress ate more after the stressful session and chose more sweet foods. Rising negative mood during the stress was also linked to eating more.

Later research by the same team suggested comfort food may work as a kind of self-medication, briefly turning down the body's stress response. That's part of why it feels so hard to resist.

## Why your body reaches for food

- **Cortisol** can increase appetite and cravings
- **Sweet and fatty foods** can briefly calm the stress response
- **Eating is soothing**, especially if food was comfort growing up
- **Numbing**: food can quiet feelings you don't want to feel
- **Exhaustion** lowers your ability to pause

## Why willpower doesn't fix it

Many people try strict rules, and it works for a while. Then a stressful week hits and the pattern comes right back. That's because the urge isn't coming from a lack of discipline. It's coming from a nervous system that needs to feel safe, and food is the fastest relief it knows.

## Stress eating or something more?

Stress eating is very common. But if you feel out of control around food, eat large amounts in secret, or feel intense shame afterward, it could be binge eating disorder. Please talk with a doctor or eating disorder specialist.

## Common questions

**Why do I crave sugar when I'm stressed?** Stress hormones can increase cravings for sweet foods, which briefly calm the stress response.

**Why do some people lose their appetite under stress?** Short-term stress can shut appetite down, while ongoing stress more often increases it.

**Is emotional eating the same as stress eating?** They overlap. Both use food to manage feelings rather than hunger.

## Why it keeps coming back

When stress keeps sending you to food, your nervous system is still searching for a way to feel safe.

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the food or the stressful day. It's an old rule your body learned long before, like *I have to soothe myself because no one else will* or *feelings aren't safe to feel*, and today's stress is just turning up its volume.
