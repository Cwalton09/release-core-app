Can stress cause a stroke? Stress alone doesn't usually cause a stroke, but research shows ongoing stress is linked to a higher risk of one. A large international study of more than 26,000 people found that stress at home, stress at work, and recent stressful life events were all linked to higher odds of stroke. Stress can raise blood pressure, the biggest risk factor for stroke. It can also make healthy habits harder to keep.

This doesn't mean a hard week will give you a stroke. It means that long-term stress matters for your heart and brain, and calming your nervous system is part of taking care of them.

## Know the signs of stroke: BE FAST

Call 911 right away if you or someone near you has:

- **B**alance loss or sudden dizziness
- **E**yes: sudden vision loss or double vision
- **F**ace drooping on one side
- **A**rm or leg weakness or numbness, especially on one side
- **S**peech that's slurred or confused
- **T**ime: call 911 now, and note when it started

A sudden, severe headache unlike any you've had is also an emergency.

## What research shows

The INTERSTROKE study, published in *JAMA Network Open* in 2022, compared 13,350 people who had a stroke with 13,462 people who didn't, across 32 countries. People who reported stress at home or at work, or recent stressful life events, had higher odds of stroke. The link held for both main types of stroke, after accounting for other risk factors.

The study also found something hopeful. People who felt more in control of their lives at home had *lower* odds of stroke.

This kind of study shows a link, not proof that stress caused each stroke.

## How stress affects your blood vessels

When you're stressed, your body releases adrenaline and cortisol. Your heart beats faster and your blood pressure rises. That's helpful in a real emergency. But when stress goes on for months or years, it can keep blood pressure higher and add to inflammation. Stress also affects sleep, eating, drinking, smoking, and exercise, which all affect stroke risk.

## Lower your risk

- Know your blood pressure and work with your doctor to manage it
- Take prescribed medications as directed
- Move your body, sleep, and limit alcohol and smoking
- Find ways to bring your nervous system out of fight-or-flight

## Common questions

**Can a sudden shock cause a stroke?** Intense emotional events can raise blood pressure quickly. In people already at risk, that may play a part, but most strokes come from long-term risk factors.

**Can anxiety feel like a stroke?** Anxiety can cause numbness, tingling, dizziness, and blurry vision. Never guess. If you notice stroke signs, call 911.

**Does lowering stress lower stroke risk?** Managing stress can help lower blood pressure, and the INTERSTROKE study linked feeling in control with lower risk. It works alongside your medical care.
