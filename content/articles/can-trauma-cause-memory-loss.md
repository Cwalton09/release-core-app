Can trauma cause memory loss? Yes, in a few different ways. Many people who went through trauma have gaps in their memories of that time, especially from childhood. Trauma and PTSD can also make everyday memory and focus harder, because a nervous system on alert puts survival ahead of storing details. Research on traumatic stress shows it affects the hippocampus, a key part of the brain for memory.

You might not remember large parts of your childhood, struggle to recall details of a hard event, or notice you forget things easily in daily life.

## When to get support

Memory gaps are common after trauma and don't mean something is wrong with your brain. But please see a doctor if memory problems are getting worse, come on suddenly, or come with confusion or personality changes. A trauma-informed therapist can help you work with trauma safely. If you're in crisis, call or text 988 in the US.

## What research shows

A 2006 review by researcher J. Douglas Bremner in *Dialogues in Clinical Neuroscience* describes how traumatic stress affects brain areas involved in memory and fear, including the hippocampus, amygdala, and prefrontal cortex. People with PTSD often show problems with verbal memory and changes in these areas.

## Ways trauma affects memory

- **Gaps.** Some periods of time, especially childhood, may be hazy or missing.
- **Fragments.** Trauma memories are often stored as pieces: images, sounds, smells, or body feelings, rather than a clear story.
- **Everyday forgetfulness.** A nervous system on alert has less room for daily details.
- **Body memory.** Your body may react to reminders even when your mind doesn't remember. See [what is implicit memory](/articles/what-is-implicit-memory).

## Why your brain may protect you

Not remembering can be a form of protection. When something is too much to take in, your nervous system may shut down or step back (dissociate), and the memory doesn't get stored the usual way. See [derealization and feeling disconnected](/articles/derealization-feeling-disconnected).

You don't need to remember everything to heal.

## Common questions

**Is it normal not to remember your childhood?** Many people remember little before age 3 or 4. Large gaps beyond that are common after hard childhoods.

**Can trauma memories come back?** Sometimes memories return, often as fragments. Recovered memories can be unreliable, so work with a trained professional.

**Can stress cause memory problems without trauma?** Yes. See [can stress cause memory loss](/articles/can-stress-cause-memory-loss).

## Why it keeps coming back

The real root is often very different from what you think. It usually isn't the memory itself. It's the rule your body learned back then, like *It's not safe to feel this* or *I have to get through it*, and your nervous system is still following it.

When your body keeps reacting to things your mind can't remember, your nervous system may still be protecting you. Most of us don't know what it's still reacting to.
